# Croatoan clues: conversational rework

Concept approved in conversation and implemented, 28 October 2026. Original: `~/Downloads/croatoan-clues.html` (preserve unchanged).

## Learning intention

Provisional audience: ages 10–11, English, desktop/tablet with voice and a text alternative. Learn to distinguish a historical clue from an explanation, connect an explanation to a source, and recognise what remains unknown. Roanoke provides the investigation; memorising its chronology is not the primary goal.

Opening: “We’ll use clues to explore what might have happened at Roanoke. We’ll learn to tell what someone found from what they thought it meant.” Give the brief context: Indigenous communities already lived here; English settlers arrived in 1587; John White returned in 1590 and found the settlement empty. Do not begin with nine compulsory story screens or an unsupported test.

## Illustrative back-and-forth, not a script

Start together with the carved word: explain that White reported finding CROATOAN and that it named a place and a people. Ask one accessible question about what he actually found. If the child confuses the carving with proof that everyone survived, acknowledge the possible destination and explain the missing link: he did not meet the settlers there. Stay with that distinction using the visible source.

If the child already distinguishes observation and inference, move on without repeating the same question. Offer a different source, such as White's description of the dismantled houses or his attribution of damage to his belongings. Invite a reason: what does the account tell us, and which part is White's explanation? Correct factual misreadings specifically. Do not demand an exact phrase or use a regex to judge a spoken explanation.

When useful, compare a later account or an archaeological find after its provenance has been checked. Ask what it adds and what it cannot establish. Later does not mean worthless; eyewitness does not mean infallible. Two passages by White are not independent corroboration. Nor are a modern summary and the account it summarises.

Offer a less-supported new source when the child's work suggests readiness, then ask for their own explanation using evidence and a remaining question. They may change their mind, remain uncertain or argue for more than one possibility. No compulsory number of clues, no winning theory, and no locked final test.

Close conversationally with confidence about the skills actually practised: distinguishing a clue from an explanation, or explaining an idea with evidence. Store the child's confidence separately from observed work; respect declining or stopping.

## Domain and tutor contract

The experience owns source IDs, provenance, displayed text, selected excerpts, viewed supports and actual learner submissions. Report those facts to the tutor after acknowledged actions. Record current work separately from attempt history. A revised answer supersedes the old answer without erasing it.

Score only genuinely objective checks, such as identifying a visible word or distinguishing two explicitly labelled dates. Assess open historical reasoning through the tutor's evidence-linked interpretation, clearly separate from deterministic scores. Never mark a theory correct because it appears in an authored `fits` array. An unsupported claim needs help, even when it names Croatoan; a justified cautious claim need not match a preferred destination.

Candidate tutor actions: inspect a source, compare sources, show contextual timeline, focus a visual detail, and return to map. No stage-advance action or universal mastery gate. Explain a new question before asking the child to answer it. Small questions during scaffolding are participation, not a hidden terminal examination.

## Historical corrections and limits

- White's report of the carving is evidence in a surviving account; the original carving is not an object this experience possesses. Artwork is a modern reconstruction, not a photograph or primary evidence.
- No distress cross does not prove everyone was safe. Dismantled houses do not prove a calm departure. Broken belongings do not by themselves identify an attacker or establish the settlers' fate. White's blame is his attribution.
- Do not infer Croatoan people's feelings as a single objectively correct answer. Explain whose perspective a surviving account represents and the limits of accounts mediated by English writers.
- The three destinations/events in the original are possibilities, not an exhaustive or mutually exclusive account of what could have happened.
- Distinguish the 1590 visit from publication. The inspected White transcription identifies its edition as Hakluyt, 1600; use that metadata rather than labelling every passage simply “written in 1593”.
- Recheck the original Lawson, Strachey, Buxton-find and map-patch cards against inspectable sources before reusing their wording. Do not present modern paraphrases as quotations.

## Visual story opening

A child-paced, replayable and skippable four-beat opening precedes the investigation: Roanoke’s place on Earth (a full-screen world map that connects to the local island map), English families arriving in 1587 and White leaving for supplies, the 1587–1590 delay, and the empty settlement in 1590. Each beat pairs canonical narration with a visual, caption and dated events; no opening quiz or learning judgment. The child can interrupt with questions. The story can be reopened without replacing a pending investigation question or notebook. The `viewed` list records displayed context, not confirmed listening or understanding.

The canonical beat text lives in `src/experience/introduction.mjs`, reused by the host, model context and teaching package. The period sequence is an orientation, not a mandatory teaching/test progression. The map identifies present-day North Carolina separately from 1587 geography. Public-domain Natural Earth outlines provide geographic context; the local map remains an illustration.

Inspected context references: [NPS 1587 voyage](https://www.nps.gov/fora/learn/historyculture/1587-the-lost-colony.htm), [NPS directions and location](https://www.nps.gov/fora/planyourvisit/directions.htm), and the previously consulted 1590 rescue account. War is presented as a cause of delay, not the complete explanation of every intervening event.
