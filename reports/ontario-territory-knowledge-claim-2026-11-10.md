The second case uses [Valleyview Public School's official Kenora address](https://vvs.kpdsb.on.ca/), independently repeated in [Grand Council Treaty3's school directory](https://gct3.ca/cultural/gikinooamaadiwin-education/school-directory/). ## Source-fit own-school application

The B3.1 task uses an adult-prepared, dated and locally verified packet for the learner's **actual school**, not an assumption that success with either worked example proves every school's location. The adult verifies the public school address and relevant Nation/community territorial and treaty resources, separately attributes school/board or municipal statements, supplies readable paraphrases and map legends, and identifies uncertainty. The worked cases are public schools; the canonical competence and own-school protocol do not require a publicly funded school. Use publicly available, locally verified school resources without collecting a pupil identity, home address or private personal information.

Ask the learner to identify the supported traditional territory or territories and applicable treaty reference, explain which source supports each, and distinguish a present reserve. A near miss chooses the nearest reserve, names only generic Indigenous peoples, copies one region's treaty into another, or claims a map proves exclusive parcel title. The second regional packet tests transfer while retaining different names and source limits. Where a local treaty is not established by suitable resources, the protocol does not invent one; unresolved local information remains a resource-verification condition for that application.

This is an authoring protocol, not evidence that a child passed or a demand for a classroom study.
