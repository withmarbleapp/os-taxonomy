This dependent proposal reviews all **52 retained Grade 2 codes: 41 specific and
11 overall expectations**, using existing topics. It builds on the unmerged
[Grade 1 proposal, PR #93](https://github.com/withmarbleapp/os-taxonomy/pull/93),

narrow or merge any mastery claim, edit assessments, change dependencies, or

mastery or proof of delivered teaching. Partial records retain concrete conditions
and candidates; they are not instructions to add topics. The six specific codes

codes are reviewed independently and reference their children without automatic
Full rollups or extra direct links.

## Reuse decisions that avoid unnecessary additions

- **A2.1:** **Planning a Task** (`mt_QR3vxbN1o4`) already assesses breaking a
  larger task into ordered steps. It combines with existing algorithms and first
  programs; another decomposition topic is unnecessary. The actual science
  investigation/model remains an explicit performance condition.
- **C2.6:** **Classifying Materials** (`mt_zHnOGwHIEz`) already plans property
  tests, records results and groups materials, with floating as an assessment
  example. **Describing Material Properties** (`mt_auVZZEuXjs`) includes
  absorbency/waterproofness. This supports the required classifications without
  inventing a buoyant-force theory claim.
- **C2.7:** **Health and safety signs** (`mt_DRNXFr0ds6`) is in Personal and
  Social Development, but explicitly includes household poison/hazard symbols.
  Reuse it. Explaining multiple actual substance-symbol meanings still needs a
  checked resource and assessment; a generic request to tell an adult is not Full.
- **E1.1–E1.2:** environmental responses, viewpoint comparison and task planning
  exist already. Keep the local/household application and verified community
  resources explicit rather than creating another planning or national-context
  topic. Australian Country-specific evidence is not relabelled as Canadian.
- **B2.5:** the camouflage claim already supplies multiple functional physical
  adaptation examples. The source permits physical and/or behavioural cases;
  neither later evolutionary theory nor an additional behaviour topic is imposed.

## Conditions and candidate review

The mapping records all remaining conditions. Important distinctions include:

- **Life histories:** current cycle and growth claims contribute, but a minimum
  two-organism example does not certify the complete required group set or
  appearance-and-behaviour comparisons through full cases. Improve the source-fit
  assessment sample before assuming a missing topic.
- **Household substances and mixtures:** generic safety or food preparation
  contributes only where its actual operation matches. Storage/disposal effects,
  useful liquid/solid combinations and non-transition cases need explicit tasks.
  The later solution/recovery candidate is not lowered to match one easier clause;
  its mixture/solution wording also warrants a separate factual review.
- **Mechanisms:** lever/pulley knowledge and ramp examples are present, but the
  complete named category/use set and varied with/without force comparisons are
  not established. This is not approval for a new collection of machine topics.
  The later mechanism and force-vocabulary claims retain their whole meanings and
  ages; no 7–8 context is manufactured from one matching term.
- **Water:** physical distribution, environmental importance and fairness can
  support future work but are not a complete account of community access to
  drinking water. Do not equate freshwater volume with drinking-water availability.
  Air/water-property completeness, actual built-water cases and the required
  locally grounded plans remain explicit. Normal resource/task work may resolve
  conditions without changing the taxonomy.

## Separate assessment-quality correction

The existing **States of Matter Vocabulary** (`mt_1JFUNQDwAJ`) prompt uses an
ambiguous kettle-steam example. Its classification claim remains useful, but
visible white mist must not be presented as gas. The proposed clarification is:

> If you point to water in a cup, an ice cube, and air inside an inflated balloon,
> can {{name}} correctly call each one a liquid, solid, or gas — and use those
> words naturally?

This retains the same three-state classification demand with a concrete gas
example. It is a proposed narrow correction, **not an authored topic change in
this alignment batch**. The mapping records the quality issue wherever this
holder contributes. It does not require another mastery topic or difficulty tier.
