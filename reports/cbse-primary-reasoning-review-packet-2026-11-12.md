## Whole claims and fit

C-1 combines a multi-operation planning claim with a concrete modelling claim. The first whole card requires deciding and justifying operations, a multi-step plan, and correcting a wrong-operation step. Its guide adds meaningful intermediate results, an unknown, estimation, arithmetic checks and contextual remainders. The modelling card requires the action, quantity-to-model mapping, using the model to answer, interpretation in real units, and reverse model-to-story construction. The source puzzle and daily context jointly exercise text interpretation, visual reasoning and information analysis; no keyword algorithm or selected-answer score substitutes for them.

C-2 combines ordering/construction of mathematical patterns with generation and explanation of hidden features from a rule. A short prefix never proves a unique infinite rule. The authored probes require an explicit consistent rule, testing a new case and explaining why it works. For the broader repeating-pattern claim, separately check abstraction to new materials and the smallest repeat unit, including a unit longer than AB. For the broader shape-pattern claim, separately generate from a stated rule and explain an implicit feature structurally. A right next-dot option alone awards neither whole topic.

C-5's whole card requires translation among situations, drawings and equations, naming the referent of each quantity. Its full guide also requires part-whole versus comparison distinctions, relational equality, unknowns in different positions and reverse translation. The proposed source assessment additionally connects equal-group concepts to two procedures for the same remaining quantity. Merely drawing a decorative picture beside an answer, or reciting two unrelated procedures, fails the connection probe.

## Original assessment materials and delivery

The following tasks are authored variations, not official handbook wording or additional official requirements. Provide counters, small paper cards and pencil/paper. The adult may read a clue faithfully in the learner's familiar mathematics language; the child interprets the quantities and relationships. Do not translate this into a nationally fixed English/Hindi language pair. Demonstration or scribing may remove a transcription barrier without supplying a plan or explanation. Keep a supported attempt separate from independent evidence. The printable [pattern cards](cbse-primary-reasoning-pattern-cards-2026-11-12.svg) provide the non-verbal display; the learner constructs responses on blank paper or with objects.

### A. Constraint grid and daily model (C-1)

Give these four rows: `2 + □`, `9 − □`, `4 + □`, `7 − □`. Give the number cards **1, 3, 6, 8**, each to be used exactly once. Every result must be a positive odd single-digit number. Ask the learner to arrange the cards, calculate, check every constraint, and explain which of 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9 can appear as results. Then ask whether another valid arrangement exists and how the learner knows all arrangements have been considered.

The two valid row assignments are `(1,8,3,6)` with results `(3,1,7,1)`, and `(3,8,1,6)` with results `(5,1,5,1)`. Addition rows require odd cards; the subtraction rows require even cards. Eight cannot occupy the last row because the result would be negative, forcing six there and eight in the second row. Thus 1, 3, 5 and 7 can occur; 9 cannot. The possible result set is a union across both arrangements, not a claim that every result appears together. One valid arrangement alone does not establish impossibility of 9.

Next: a buyer starts with **24 tokens**, buys three balls and two balloons, and has **4 tokens left**, exactly the price of one ball. Ask the learner to build their own counter/bar model, name known and unknown quantities, choose and justify operations, and find the price of one balloon. They should derive spending 20, three balls costing 12, two balloons costing 8 and one balloon costing 4. Ask what each number represents and how the result checks against all the information. The remaining 4 is not added to the spending. Give a model back and request a matching story that preserves the quantities and action.

Transfer: change only the starting total to **26** while the remainder is still 4 and still the price of one ball. A balloon now costs **5**; the total spent is 22, the three balls still cost 12 and the two balloons cost 10. Repeating the old balloon price shows failure to update the model. Do not accept an unexplained option or an inverse check alone as proof that the operation choice matches the situation.

C-1 Full authoring requires both puzzle constraint reasoning and a self-constructed daily-life model with quantity interpretation, justified steps and a changed case. A correct total without the visual/semantic mapping is Partial evidence. Wrong arithmetic within a correct plan is distinguished from a wrong operation or lost condition.

### B. Non-verbal pattern transfer (C-2)

Use the first card strip in the printable material. Its dot counts are **3, 5, 4, 6, 5, 7, 6, ?**. Ask the learner to draw the next three cards, explain their rule and test a later card. The intended interleaving has odd-position counts 3,4,5,6,7… and even-position counts 5,6,7,8,9…; next three counts are **8,7,9**. Positions 9 and 10 have 7 and 9 dots. Ask why the two position sequences grow and how the same relation generates a new case. A correct 8 with no rule is insufficient.

Then use all three before/after 3×3 examples and the new grid in the printable material. The rule replaces each even numeral with a black circle, preserving every odd numeral and every cell's position. Ask the learner to infer it, draw the new transformed grid, explain two changed and two unchanged cells, and classify an additional new value. The test includes **0**, which is even: explain it as zero equal pairs or a multiple of two, rather than treating it as missing information. Accept another rule consistent with all supplied examples only if the learner states it, tests new cases, and explains its scope; use a distinguishing new case rather than asserting that a finite sample logically proves the intended rule is unique.

For independent whole-topic checks beyond source-level success: construct a longer-than-AB repeating pattern, identify its smallest repeat unit, reproduce its structure using different objects, and invent another pattern. Separately generate a stated number/growing-shape rule, notice a feature not stated by that rule, and justify it structurally. For example, starting at 2 and repeatedly adding 4 stays even because both the start and step are even. Do not confuse a repeating unit of items with a repeated growth step. Physical flat-tile/solid-block work remains part of the separately accepted NCERT shape protocol where applicable; it is not silently added as a new CBSE source requirement.

C-2 Full authoring requires rule inference, application to new cases, and explaining the abstract relationship using the visual patterns. A copied symbol grid, a lucky next item or merely restating the observed items is insufficient. Supported recognition can inform teaching, but the independent probe requires construction and explanation.

### C. Connect concepts, procedures and representations (C-5)

Give **four trays with 12 cubes in each**, and remove **three from each tray**. The learner constructs a model or annotated drawing and explains two ways to calculate the remaining total: `4 × (12 − 3) = 36` and `48 − 4 × 3 = 36`. Require explicit mappings: 4 is the number of groups, 12 the original group size, 3 the removal per group, 9 the remaining group size, 48 the original total, 12 the total removed and 36 the total remaining. The two occurrences of 12 have different referents; label them. Explain why the procedures express the same action and how each relates to the concrete model. Formal property names are unnecessary. For inverse translation, provide one of the equations and ask for a matching new story or model.

Change to **five trays** with the same per-tray quantities. The new total is **45**: `5 × 9` and `60 − 15`. Removing only three from the overall total is a revealing near miss; the removal is per group. Connect this result to the changed picture, rather than mechanically reusing 36. The purchase task in A supplies a second context connecting part-whole and unit price, without turning a single equal-groups trick into a universal connection claim.

Separately check the whole target's broader guide duties: a part-whole story with total10, knownpart6 and unknown4 can be represented by `6 + □ = 10` and `10 − 6 = □`; explain why `6 + 10` misuses the whole as a part. Use a distinct comparison story with counts5 and8, align comparison bars and identify difference3. Judge `6 + 4 = 10 + 2` false and `6 + 4 = 7 + 3` true by comparing both sides. Include an unknown in another position, and reverse picture/equation-to-story construction. These preserve the broader whole claim; they are not invented official source clauses.

C-5 Full authoring requires explicit concept–procedure–representation connections in the solved and changed problems. Two correct calculations without an explanation connecting them and the situation are insufficient. Correct equation recognition without independent construction is insufficient for whole-topic attainment.
