## Actual attributed accounts and their limits

A. National Park Service, *Coastal Processes—Erosion*, updated8March2019, actual main body read: waves can dislodge material and move sediment; effects depend on material and wave conditions. Its photos identify an actual Virginia wave-cut bluff and Hawaiian sea arch. Use named accounts as attributed observations, not a local Ontario coast or a pupil experiment. Source: https://www.nps.gov/articles/coastal-processes-erosion.htm . No numerical pressure, surf classification or wave-energy calculation is a pupil gate.

B. National Park Service, *Seawalls, Bulkheads, and Revetments*, updated5April2019, actual main body read: structures can protect landward assets yet increase erosion at their base or nearby beaches and restrict beach movement. Some historically valuable assets are difficult to relocate, so a simple “remove every wall” judgment is not supported. Source: https://home.nps.gov/articles/seawalls-bulkheads-and-revetments.htm . Its projected SandPoint removal is not relabelled completed2026 action.

C. NOAA, *What is shoreline armoring?*, updated23September2026, actual main body read: coastal protection can affect sediment movement, public beach access and habitat. Benefits and costs depend on the site. Source: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/shoreline-armoring.html . Use the competing needs and conditional mechanism, not a universal engineering prescription or numerical national building inventory.

D. US Consumer Product Safety Commission, *Public Playground Safety Checklist*, actual complete checklist read. Purpose-built surfacing, guardrails, condition checks and adult supervision are different safeguards. Its US dimensions are not Ontario compliance rules or a measurement task here. Source: https://www.cpsc.gov/safety-education/safety-guides/playgrounds/public-playground-safety-checklist . Do not infer that any cushion, playground, depth or fall is safe.

E. Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, safety-belt mechanism answer, dated15April2014, actual answer read. Proper restraint can spread crash forces and allow a more gradual slowdown, reducing harmful effects; the account also distinguishes preventing ejection. Source: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/faq/what-do-you-say-drivers-who-think-wearing-safety-belt-isnt-going-prevent-injuries-if-they-are . This is an attributed mechanism, not pupil crash testing, a current child-restraint fit/legal lesson or a guarantee against injury.

## C1.1 natural forces and human interventions

Teach from cards A–C. Draw two labelled original panels: (1) waves acting on a natural beach/cliff, moving material and changing habitat; (2) the same coast with a road/building behind a protective wall, neighbouring beach and habitat beside it. Mark water→shore push, sediment movement and the assets at issue. The sketch is an explanatory model, not a measured map or photograph of a particular current site.

Learner independently explains effects on both natural and built settings, and assesses a human intervention: a wall may protect an asset while changing erosion or movement elsewhere. Identify a benefit, a cost/possible unintended effect and an evidence limit. Do not require every intervention to have every effect. Ask whose needs are served and what further site evidence would guide a choice. A wall cannot make waves cease to act.

Fresh transfer: a fictional coastal path is moved farther inland instead of placing a wall against the beach. Give the facts “space inland is available; the path can be moved; no irreplaceable building stands behind it.” Explain how relocation changes exposure without stopping natural erosion. Then change one fact: a culturally important fixed building is threatened and cannot presently be moved. Reconsider the choice and information needed; neither wall nor relocation is a universal best answer. Keep the supplied model separate from actual NPS/NOAA records.

The complete PreventingErosion claim separately requires actual comparison of at least two solutions and evidence from tests or observations. Use a safe tray test with adult-selected clean sand/soil, controlled slope and equal measured gentle water input, comparing unprotected model ground against two designs (for example mesh/plant-root analogue and a small barrier). Predict, record actual movement, compare repeated trials and discuss limited model-to-coast transfer. No supplied result table is substituted for pupil observations; a chosen design need not win and a classroom tray does not certify real coastal construction. Wind may be described from a verified source/model; no blowing dust into faces. Its land/water ancestor retains four landforms, three water bodies and a map/model showing their relationships, separately from identifying one beach.

## C1.2 human activities and protection

Use two distinct activities: playing on elevated playground equipment and travelling in a vehicle. Ask what changes motion during a fall/collision and why a large abrupt contact force may be harmful. Contrast prevention of a fall (guardrail) with reduction of harmful impact if one occurs (appropriate tested surfacing). Explain a restraint's different function using card E: gradual slowing and spreading force; it does not stop a vehicle crash from happening. A correctly named device without a mechanism is insufficient.

Independent assessment: provide original cards “guardrail”, “tested protective surface”, “properly used vehicle restraint”, “painted warning sign”, “damaged protective surface”. Learner selects fitting measures for both activities, explains the force harm each addresses, distinguishes prevention from impact reduction, and states a limitation. A sign may inform behaviour but does not cushion an impact. Damaged/unsuitable protection cannot be assumed effective. Actual equipment selection/use remains with a responsible adult and current applicable guidance; do not test a child fall, crash, restraint or protective item to failure.

Fresh transfer: change a playground from ordinary standing-height movement to a hypothetical elevated fall, and ask why a soft-looking surface alone is not enough evidence of suitable protection. Separately change a restrained passenger to an unrestrained toy figure in a drawn braking vehicle; predict continued forward movement and explain the restraint's purpose. Use drawings or low-speed enclosed adult-operated toy models only, never a child impact. The source asks harmful force effects and protective devices, not actual injury, clinical diagnosis or formula calculations.

Whole ThingsPeopleDesign mastery remains a separate product-feature explanation, an environment designed for users and a named service plus whom it helps. Supply an actual bottle/lid, an accessible low tap or clearly attributed photograph, and an actual library/bus service account; each needs a purpose/feature/user explanation. A protective-surface card alone does not award this broad claim.

## C2 independent motion and change assessment

Set up safe tabletop objects: a light toy/block, a soft ball, dough, sponge, adult-selected thick elastic, paper and a weak encased classroom magnet with a steel paperclip (small parts managed by adult). No strong loose magnets, glass breaking, elastic aimed at faces, iron filings, vacuum experiment or dangerous momentum demonstration. Adult preflights actual surfaces/equipment; do not invent a guaranteed outcome.

After separate teaching, require the learner to make and explain a push and a pull, compare gentle/stronger pushes on the same object under comparable conditions, and predict/test direction. Demonstrate starting, slowing/stopping, direction/speed changes and shape changes using safe examples. Compare a squeezed sponge with dough after release: spring-back and stay-changed differ, and the original material remains. Perform distinct squash, bend, twist and stretch with appropriate materials. State what was observed, what was predicted and where the result differed. A word-matching task is not the performed investigation.

Use magnet/paperclip across a visible gap and direct hand contact to classify non-contact/contact interactions; gravity is distinct from magnetism. Explain gravity acting on a safely released soft ball and name everyday uses of forces. Give a fresh object and a new direction/strength or material condition; independently predict an effect, test safely and explain it. Stronger does not always mean “moves farther” when the object/material/surface has also changed. A stationary object can still have forces acting.

Whole source understanding integrates force examples, different application ways, motion/deformation changes and daily use in a fresh explanatory account. This is assessed separately from historical C2.1–C2.4 labels and from entire contributor awards. No new topic, pair, age context or hard edge is proposed by this candidate.

## Complete eleven-card operational support appendix

Use these additional original materials and independent tasks for the full contributor/prerequisite claims. Source success alone awards none of them. These supports retain the ten saved guides' current learner actions, constraints and assessment boundaries; they do not upgrade their inherited source-verification/confidence metadata or claim tested efficacy.

1. **Land and water model.** Supply paper, clay/cutouts, a familiar story setting or verified real-area reference. Construct and label at least four landforms (mountain/hill/valley/plain, with cliff as a contrast) and three water bodies (river/lake/ocean, with pond as a contrast). Mark the river's downhill route and connect a side-view model to a bird's-eye map; blue water is not blue sky. Explain that a model preserves important shapes/positions while omitting detail, not copying full size. The generic coastal sketch does not replace this model construction or the familiar/real-setting anchor.
2. **Erosion comparison.** Retain the tray comparison above; use an unprotected control and two distinct solutions, equal slope/soil/input water/pour height. A living cover where configured, or an explicitly artificial mesh analogue, is not automatically a real rooted plant experiment. Show binding by roots only where roots are actually present; moss need not have true roots. Learner records soil lost/retained, makes their own comparison chart and independently selects a best-supported solution. Fresh windy-flat versus rainy-slope cases require mechanisms, effort/cost and limits. Screen outputs are labelled models and do not become measured real soil.
3. **Force language.** Draw reproducible separate scene cards on paper: a pushed door, pulled cart, twisted jar lid, stretched band, squashed sponge, released soft ball, a sliding block, a paper parachute and a floating toy boat. Mark only the objects initially; the learner supplies force words in fresh cards. Explain gravity/weight as Earth's pull (a force), mass as amount of matter without calculation; friction and air resistance oppose relative motion, water supplies upthrust, magnetism differs from gravity. Require at least five accurate scenarios and the complete varied force vocabulary when assessing the whole language claim. Air/water contact need not be visible. No formula or field vocabulary is added.
4. **Push/pull application.** Physically compare same-object gentle/strong pushes, starting from rest and from existing motion. Add an opposing cushion to slow/stop and a sideways tap that bends an already moving path rather than instantly setting exactly sideways travel. Compare a lighter/heavier similar-sized object only as a setup-dependent demonstration, not a controlled numerical mass law. Hinge/track constraints matter. Learner predicts before testing, makes fresh direction/strength explanations and attributes stopping to opposing interaction rather than a push used up inside the object.
5. **Friction investigation.** Use the same sliding block, repeatable same release/push and at least three surfaces; do not mix rolling and sliding trials. Predict and mark a stopping point, repeat, measure actual distances, record a table and draw a simple bar chart. Draw friction arrows opposite sliding and explain the observations only within the controlled comparison. Compare preflighted shiny-rough foil and dull-smooth card as a prediction/check, with no guaranteed stopping order. Distinguish shininess from friction, helpful grip from slowing slip, and feel gentle hand-rubbing warmth. A gently pushed block or a block on a suitable slope can be held still by grip; a free level resting object with nothing pushing has no horizontal friction to balance. The learner explains a changed pair, not merely names a surface.
6. **Contact and distance.** With adult-managed weak magnets and a paperclip, physically experience an attraction across a gap and distinguish it from pushing two wooden blocks into contact. Predict/test paper in the gap and a magnetic steel tray; tray effects depend on equipment and must be preflighted, not assigned a universal answer. A paper barrier need not block magnetic attraction. Contrast steel versus aluminium, two magnets' attraction/repulsion and Earth's gravitational pull; do not say all metals are magnetic or every invisible pull is one force. Explain from a supplied physical fact that magnetism does not require air; no pretend classroom vacuum observation. Classify novel contact/non-contact scenarios with reasons beyond memorised sorting.
7. **Object/material distinction.** Supply intact safe wood/metal/plastic spoons or cups and different wooden objects. Ask separately “what object?” and “what made of?”, compare same object/different materials and different objects/same material, and require a fresh example. Use a multi-material pencil/shoe and identify parts. A plastic-cup melting question is a spoken hypothetical, never a child heating activity. Material includes more than cloth.
8. **Naming materials.** Prepare at least ten actual familiar objects containing wood/plastic/glass/metal/water/rock, also paper/fabric. Glass remains intact adult-managed; never test it by bending or breaking. Include water/ice, rock across different objects, soft plastic, dull/coloured metal, clear-plastic versus glass and shiny-plastic versus metal comparisons. Sensory/behaviour clues are not certain identification tests; the adult must know actual materials. Independently name and sort by substance, record multi-material finds under relevant categories and interpret a fair equal-time material hunt tally. No fabricated class inventory/results.
9. **Material properties.** Independently use at least five property words, describe three materials with two properties each, and tie words to observed behaviour. Test equal water drops on preflighted absorbent fabric/paper versus waterproof plastic; compare soft-but-waterproof and rough-but-absorbent counterexamples. Use a safe torch aimed at clear sheet/tissue/wood and observe transmitted light on a surface beyond, never through a material toward the source/sun. Distinguish transparent/opaque and intermediate transmission, shiny from transparent, stretchy from bendy and stiff. Predict/test a preflighted shiny absorbent material versus dull waterproof plastic without promising every fabric/plastic has that property. Choose an umbrella covering using tested evidence.
10. **Changing solid shapes.** Provide dough, sponge/elastic, soft wire/paper and rigid wood/stone. Perform all four distinct actions; predict, apply, release, and record springs-back/stays-changed/barely-changes for materials/actions. No rigid item is forced until it breaks. Compare the same lump before/after a simple reshape, including a safe balance comparison with nothing added/removed, and explain material identity despite shape change. A bent paperclip remains metal. Independent new-material transfer, not only recognisable pictures, is required.
11. **Things people design.** Keep the product, designed environment and designed service task above, using actual applicable examples and explicit user/purpose/feature relationships. A library or bus is a service, not necessarily a protective device. No saved guide exists for this claim; its complete three card criteria still govern.
