# Ontario Grade 5 Social Studies: source-fit review

All 41 retained Grade 5 expectations now have reviewed dispositions: **37 Full and 4 Partial**. Full means that the authored knowledge and prepared assessment/task programme account for the source duty. It does not report classroom attainment, default-branch acceptance or database publication.

The [current ledger](ontario-grade5-social-current-ledger-2026-11-11.json) binds each code to its actual contributors and task dossier. The [source review](ontario-grade5-social-source-review-2026-11-11.json) records the verified official course identity and catalogue reconciliation. The course identifies its edition as 2018; the retained catalogue uses its established 2026 source identity. Neither is silently rewritten.

## What changed

The government/citizens strand accounts for 21 expectations. Its three new factual repertoires distinguish Canadian rights/responsibilities, the constitutional consultation relationship, and current government/representative structures. The last deliberately includes studied First Nation, Métis and Inuit arrangements within one shared institutional claim, avoiding an additional Inuit-only node.

The historical strand adds two factual repertoires: [New France institutions](new-france-institutions-review-2026-11-11.json) and [early colonial relationships with later Métis emergence](early-colonial-relationships-review-2026-11-11.json). The latter retains four actual early encounter packets and a separately dated longer-term community outcome. It neither labels every mixed-ancestry person Métis nor invents a pre-1713 Mattawa collective.

Existing research, source comparison, map production, conclusions and historical organisation claims supply the remaining operations. The [inquiry programme](ontario-grade5-historical-inquiry-design-2026-11-11.json) contains actual source selections and research/map products. The [integrated historical programme](ontario-grade5-historical-relationships-integration-2026-11-11.json) preserves whole prior case repertoires and explicitly accounts for benefits, harms, different communities, European conflict effects and present connections.

The resulting inventory is 70 topic–expectation pairs across 16 topics: five new knowledge claims and eleven reused claims. Those counts are associations, not a completion percentage or evidence that a pupil has mastered a topic. All new Grade 5 contexts use Ontario ages 10–11. No prerequisite edges, old mastery claims or prior scoped intervals are changed.

## Conditions retained

- **A2 and A2.5:** the source combines period interactions with Métis community language. The programme separates earlier causal relationships from the later collective emergence documented by the sources. It does not resolve that chronology by backdating people or communities.
- **A3.2 and overall A3:** a source-supported pre-European First Nations–Inuit interaction has not yet been verified in the prepared packet. The attributed Haudenosaunee founding-peace contribution is retained, but geographic overlap or a later tradition alone cannot establish the missing chronology. This does not imply that such interactions never occurred or that a new taxonomy topic is needed.

These are source/resource conditions. Real learner records or a classroom trial are not required to complete an authoring review, and no learner estimates are inferred or changed.
