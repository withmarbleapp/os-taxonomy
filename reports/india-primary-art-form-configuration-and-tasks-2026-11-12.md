# Primary art-form configuration and original tasks

This original, untrialled task portfolio accounts for eleven retained art-form
configuration conditions: seven suggestive Foundational syllabus positions and
four formal Preparatory LS2 positions. It does not turn the suggestive table into
seven compulsory grade deadlines. Existing ConditionalArtFormBinding judgments,
topic candidates, ages and historical reports remain unchanged.

## Programme and resource contract

Before delivery, the educator records the selected visual and performing forms,
available materials, communication language, access adaptations, actual observation
setting and an appropriate facilitator. Use relevant local forms where available;
do not claim that these original classroom examples are a named local tradition.
The worked configuration below uses paper drawing/collage and original voice,
rhythm and dramatic presentation. Other school choices require corresponding
actual materials, process instruction and independent tasks. One performance does
not establish every alternative form.

NCFSE Part C section 6.5 (PDF354, printed340) makes LS2 the baseline from initial
implementation and LS1 the fuller provision as resources become available. These
are resource-conditioned learning-standard sets, not taxonomy difficulty levels.
The same passage calls for opportunities in visual and performing arts. Retain
both in the integrated portfolio and preserve the source's emphasis on variety;
a single copied drawing cannot stand for the broad baseline. This worked example
does not certify all LS1 forms or an actual school's provision.

Supply blank paper, scrap paper of contrasting colours/textures, pencils or
crayons, washable glue, child-appropriate scissors or pre-torn pieces, two ordinary
objects with contrasting safe sound qualities, and a clear table/display area.
Body tapping or voice can replace an unavailable object; adapt physical handling
without an adult making the learner's expressive decisions. No instrument purchase,
forced public performance, private biography or mandatory positive emotion is
required. The directions below are reproducible materials; no missing illustration,
recording or completed pupil product is assumed.

## Foundation: free expression and environmental communication

First offer open exploration: combine, draw or arrange the supplied materials to
express an idea about something actually noticed outdoors or imagined. A learner
can choose a quiet arrangement, drawing, card or short role-play. Ask what they
intend and allow a different idea from the adult's. Do not require an emotion or
interpret colours as having one correct feeling. Preserve the environmental
context: distinguish a real observation from an invented creature or scene.

Then give a different purpose: help a visitor care for the observed place. Choose
**a card/poster or a role-play**. For the visual route, choose an intended viewer,
image, arrangement and optional words; test whether a peer can explain its intended
care message. For role-play, invent a visitor and helper exchange and make a
deliberate voice/action choice. These are alternatives for this source panel,
not two universal requirements. Ask the learner to change one feature for another
viewer or situation and explain its effect. Copying the adult's finished message,
tidiness or agreeing with adult taste does not establish creative decisions.

Independent evidence: an own product or performance, an identifiable expressive
choice, a reason connected to the purpose and an independently chosen adaptation.
For an early learner, pointing, showing and an oral explanation are legitimate;
written fluency is not an extra art gate. A positive feeling about nature remains
a separate contextual channel, not inferred from a correct care message.

## Foundation: actual visit and creative response

Before this task, select an actual accessible visit and identify what can really
be observed there. The source table includes garden/park observation and nearby
museum/monument opportunities. Bind the intended source panel and setting: use a
real permitted garden/park visit for nature observation, or an actual accessible
museum/monument visit for that cultural route. A prepared virtual visit can serve
only when the educator supplies and checks actual attributed views beforehand,
records that it was virtual and checks that the selected programme permits it.
This document does not claim a museum page, image or virtual tour was visited.

On the visit, choose two observable details, such as a branching outline and
rough/smooth surface, or a repeated shape and its arrangement. Record them through
sketches, descriptions or supported photographs where permitted. Do not damage
plants, remove protected material or infer an object's history from appearance.
Back in class, make a drawing, arrangement or dramatic representation that retains
those actual details while adding an own expressive decision. Explain which parts
were observed and which were changed imaginatively. Invite a reasoned response:
which detail was effective or surprising, and why? Another legitimate response
need not agree.

Independent changed case: revisit a different actual detail or supplied checked
view and create a new response with an explained change. A fictional visit told
as a memory, copied photograph or unrelated artwork is insufficient. If no visit
or checked virtual resource has occurred, record that delivery condition as
unfulfilled; an authored task is not a completed learner observation.

## Foundation: imagination, language and purposeful revision

Use this original fictional seed: a paper bird finds an empty frame and wonders
what could belong inside it. Do not supply the ending. The learner chooses what
happens, creates a visual story or short performed ending and explains one choice.
Another learner may choose a different plausible ending. After discussion, change
the intended viewer or a material and revise the work deliberately. Aesthetic
response means noticing and explaining an effect; adult preference is not the key.

For the language-linked source panels, offer an original short spoken prompt in
the actual classroom language: describe a real seasonal change previously observed
by the class, or clearly introduce an imagined seasonal scene. The learner invents
a short rhyme, scene, paper construction or dramatic account connected to it and
discusses the choices. If a language competency is also assessed, provide its own
language-specific check; a visual product alone does not establish speaking,
listening or reading. Existing role-bound language contracts continue separately.

Independent evidence: an own imaginative addition, actual production, a purposeful
change and an explanation grounded in the work. The supplied story seed and adult
teaching example are starting points, never a completed answer to reproduce.

## Preparatory: varied creation and practical processes

Prepare a small display and performance about a familiar shared place. Make two
different visual works, for example a textured collage and a line drawing, and a
short original sound/dramatic presentation. The learner chooses the idea or
imagined feeling to communicate, plans, tries materials, reviews and presents.
No private experience is required. Retain both visual and performing exploration
and genuine variety within the selected programme, without declaring every
national art form compulsory.

Teach one trial using scrap paper: compare a drawn edge with an overlapped torn
edge, show joining and shaping, and explain how changing placement changes a
composition. Independently choose and actually use a process in a new own work.
Describe the relevant material, tool and process and show their effects, rather
than only recite names. Offer pre-torn pieces or assisted tool handling while the
learner makes the expressive choices. Whole safe-cutting or composition topic
credit still requires the complete existing claim and its own assessment.

For the performing trial, compare an ordinary object's safe tap with a voice sound
or body tap. Model an original four-pulse pattern: tap, tap, rest, voice. The
learner invents a different pattern or short dramatic sound sequence, rehearses
and presents it, explaining a chosen sound and an intended effect. A changed case
removes one object or changes the audience: adapt the actual presentation and
explain the choice. Naming an instrument, copying the model or describing an
unperformed beat is insufficient. No numerical tempo, named traditional style or
all-musical-elements mastery is inferred from this bounded process task.

## Preparatory: collaborative response in the surroundings

Use the actual class display/performance and another appropriate artwork or
performance that is genuinely present in the school's surroundings. Record the
actual work and its context; an original class display is not labelled a historic
regional tradition. Discuss an observable feature and its possible effect. Ask
each learner for their own response, including uncertainty or dislike, and a
reason. Hear an actual peer's different view and respond to its meaning. An adult
must not invent another child's opinion and report that the learner listened.

In a joint new work, each learner proposes an idea, hears another, agrees a fair
contribution and actually completes it. Record how both ideas affected the work.
Two solo products laid side by side or a peer doing all the making do not meet
collaborative production. Change a role, material or presentation space and repeat
planning and adaptation. Communication access support may assist expression;
quiet participation is not absence of thought or artistic response.

## Preparatory: independent integrated goal

After teaching, give a fresh brief: create a small shared welcome display and
performance for a new class reader/viewer. Independently choose ideas, visual
materials and a performing route; create and present varied work, describe and
demonstrate relevant processes, and respond to another actual surrounding work.
Retain actual shared contribution and own/other responses. Use a new arrangement
or material limitation to test adaptation. Do not mark the goal complete merely
by counting the three competency labels.

Separately offer time, choice, support and optional reflection on what was
interesting, difficult or worth trying again. The goal's engagement duty remains
contextual: record opportunities and any voluntarily shared response without
scoring smiles, compliance or permanent enjoyment. The independent creative work
and practical processes remain required even when no positive feeling is stated.
