# Ontario Grade 1 Science and Technology — 9 November 2026

This proposal reviews all **55 retained Grade 1 catalogue codes: 44 specific and
11 overall expectations**, against the official 2022 source and its process
context. It reuses existing claims and preserves the previously accepted B2.2
pilot. Read the [complete code-by-code mapping](ontario-grade1-science-mapping-2026-11-09.json)
for direct contribution roles, remaining conditions, candidate-only support,

wider Ontario backlog at accepted base `45e2bc0`.

## Reuse decisions

B2.4 has Full semantic support through **Bones & Muscles**, **The Heart & Blood**
and **Body Parts & Senses** together. Their existing evidence supplies varied
locations/functions; no anatomy topic is created or broadened.

Habitat/interdependence claims contribute to B2.1 and B2.6 even though some hard
prerequisites lack a matching Ontario interval. Missing local prerequisite
context is a readiness review issue; it does not erase semantic contribution.

Weak matches were rejected: season knowledge and clothing choice alone do not
establish an energy comparison; identifying an embedded computer does not explain
its impact; an environmental response list does not demonstrate an executed
waste project; observing annual daylight does not explain the cause of seasons.
Those items remain prerequisites, candidates or explicit conditions, not inflated
direct links.

## Matters requiring a concrete follow-up

- **C2.1:** retain the direct body-energy and sunlight-warming components, then
  review assessment of the common energy idea across contexts. Later technical
  vocabulary, energy stores and conservation are not equivalent mastery evidence;
  another concrete example or a Grade 1 clone does not resolve the generalisation.
- **D2.2:** retain the skeletal-support component and review recognition of
  supporting frameworks across natural and built examples. Generic construction
  practice does not establish that generalisation, and formal load-path analysis
  is not an elementary requirement. A new topic is not pre-authorized.
- **C2.5 and D2.8:** existing natural-resource/material claims contain relevant
  content. Review their whole-claim age fit before adding a 6–7 interval; do not
  create grade/country clones merely to avoid the decision. Material origins has
  semantic support even though its proposed local context remains deferred.
- **E2.1:** causal seasonal content exists within a polar-specific claim; the topic
  titled Why seasons change is observational. Resolve assessment/context fit or
  justify a scope-preserving extraction before changing claims.
- **A3.3:** verify resources and their scientific contributions from more than one
  community. A single biography or an unverified general statement about
  ecological knowledge is insufficient. Resource work should precede new-topic
  proposals.
- **Other Partial conditions:** integrated performances, environmental quality,
  practical action, source variety, temporal observations and adaptive
  interpretation are detailed per code. A condition is not automatically a new
  topic, and normal task design may discharge some of them.

## Readiness diagnostic

For each direct holder, the mapping lists hard-prerequisite IDs without an
explicit Ontario context covering the full 6–7 interval. This is a narrow public
context diagnostic, **not** an implementation of a consumer's selection algorithm
or a certification of selectable coverage. Framework/global guidance, consumer
rules and actual prerequisites need separate consideration. No prerequisite edge
or age-only context is invented to make the diagnostic disappear.

There are **10 distinct referenced prerequisites** in that diagnostic.
They include habitat/living-things vocabulary, organism naming, material grouping,
data presentation, smart devices and planet naming. Review
those contexts and any over-strong historical dependencies without weakening
claims or changing edges as an incidental alignment edit.
