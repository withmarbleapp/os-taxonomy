## People, features, services and needs

Use accurate resources for the learner's actual community: a natural feature, built feature, and services/occupations, including a service the learner uses. Ask how people interact with each to meet needs, and what might happen if a feature/service were unavailable. Existing Features, Needs/Wants, Goods/Services and Jobs contribute; Actions/Consequences accounts for the absence/action reasoning. Naming places or jobs without explaining the relationship is a near miss.

The [City of Toronto drinking-water account](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/water-environment/tap-water-in-toronto/) is a worked service case: the City describes treating and testing Lake Ontario water and supplying it to people. Only introductory provider/need facts are used; treatment chemistry or a drinking demonstration is not required. The previously reviewed garbage/organics and public-library resources provide additional service examples with their actual provider limits. An adult verifies the appropriate provider/resources for another locality; Toronto is not a universal model.

For B1.3, the learner creates an actual ordered plan for responsible interaction with a familiar built/natural place. They name steps, resources/information and responsibilities, and explain a plausible enhancement. Existing Planning, SpecialPlaces and Actions/Consequences contribute. Saying only “be good” or repeating an adult's completed plan is insufficient. A changed resource or location checks transfer. The source asks for creating/explaining the plan; no cleanup study or measured environmental benefit is invented as an extra obligation.

## Relative distance and a map with non-standard units

Use actual familiar landmarks and a common reference point. The learner describes relative position/direction and compares which place is closer/farther, grounded in route lengths or a direct model of them. Existing ComparingLengths supplies the comparison operation alongside PositionalLanguage and FindingPlacesOnAPlan. Changing the reference point checks that “farther” is relational rather than an absolute label.

Then measure a short familiar route using equal units, record the number/unit and put that distance on the **same map** as the location symbols and key. A stated consistent informal unit connects the map to the route. Existing Maps and MeasuringLength account for the integrated operation; separate unrelated successes do not. Unequal units, gaps, unsupported reference changes or a drawing disconnected from its measurement are near misses. The task does not impose a formal numerical scale or cardinal-direction system.

B3's overall description combines these performances with the already reviewed territorial-resource, distinct-area and service protocols for the learner's familiar community. It requires coherent community description and basic location/distance use, rather than rolling up topic labels or the new task title alone.
