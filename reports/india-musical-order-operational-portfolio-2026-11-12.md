## Materials and declared scale

Provide a suitable pitched instrument or unforced voice, an actual heard reference and an accessible response method. Do not require a particular national tradition, tuning system, fixed concert pitch, note-name inventory or Western staff. Each has four notes with the same tone/gap timing and amplitude envelope; this removes phrase length as a shortcut. Equal electrical amplitude does not guarantee equal perceived loudness. Configure comfortable playback and keep attention on heard pitch order; no auditory playback or classroom preflight is asserted here. The same task can use actual instrument sounds with the stated keys.

Before independent work, let the learner hear the full ordered set in both directions, explore the instrument/range and practise matching a single comfortable reference. Repeat with a changed reference, compare the attempt and make an actual audible adjustment. If listening comparison succeeds while production remains difficult, record those operations separately; volume, effort or strain is not evidence of pitch accuracy.

## Teach the features separately

Use teaching files T1–T6, with the adult keys concealed until after each attempt. T1/T2 pass through neighbouring ranks upward/downward. T3/T4 continue one direction but skip a rank. T5/T6 change direction. Demonstrate each slowly on the actual instrument or by replaying the sound, then compare contrasting sequences. A staircase gesture or rank tokens may support the explanation after hearing; printed numbers must not supply the independent answer.

Ask what happened between successive heard notes: did it go up, down or stay the same; did it move to the neighbouring rank or pass over one; did its direction turn? Actually sound a missing intermediate rank to check a suspected skip. Replay a monotone-skip phrase beside a direction-changing phrase and ask the learner to distinguish their features, rather than call both by an unexplained label. A beat-copy, louder sound or correct mathematical next-term rule is insufficient.

## Independent hearing and reproduction

Shuffle fresh files N1–N6 and hide file names, note diagrams, ordered keys and completed answers. All have four notes. Ask the learner to describe or accessibly trace the heard direction and say whether a neighbouring rank was passed over. Require a reason tied to the sound, with replay available under a declared consistent support arrangement. Then reproduce a short heard sequence with the voice or suitable instrument, compare with the original and make an actual correction where needed. Keep perceptual discrimination and production evidence separate rather than invent a professional tuning tolerance or diagnose a learner from one attempt.

The fresh set contains both directions of neighbouring steps, both directions of monotone skipping, and two different direction-changing paths. After one successful sequence, change the reference/start or sequence and require a new response. Do not accept remembering the first file position, reading a displayed rank string or the adult producing the answer. If actual configured delivery cannot supply audible comparisons, this condition remains open; a paper pattern is not a substitute.

## Boundary and transfer

Use R, which contains repeated notes, and ask which transitions stay the same or rise. Keep that descriptive answer without assigning an unsupported official class. Present one of the fresh sequences with changed tempo or comfortable loudness but the same pitch order, and then a version with one pitch-order change. Ask which relationship changed; this checks that speed or loudness is not being mistaken for pitch order. The adult produces and checks those variants from the actual key; no unseen recording is asserted.

Finally supply another short previously unheard sequence in the configured familiar scale and ask for the feature explanation, reproduction and comparison again. The teacher records the actual sequence, support and result. This is independent transfer, not a sum of earlier successful child tasks. Any changed scale or unfamiliar tradition needs its own familiarisation and checked configuration; this example does not certify every possible scale.
