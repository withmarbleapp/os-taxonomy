## One new claim: formulating mathematical problems

`mt_rCy4qtUwuT` represents choosing an answerable quantity, shape, spatial or measurement question from a situation, identifying relevant available/needed information and an appropriate mathematical criterion or method. It accepts speech, pointing, drawings or a model. There is no hard English grammar/question-word prerequisite.

The closest existing claims were read in full. Making Sense of Problems and guided multi-step planning start from a given problem. Existing modelling claims represent, predict, interpret and check; they do not explicitly require choosing the question. Asking for clarification is different. Scientific-question and statistical-investigation claims have their own scope and do not exhaust non-statistical mathematical formulation.

A neutral box/object/ribbon scene lets the learner choose the unknown. Near misses include repeating the adult's question, a preference without a mathematical criterion, or an unsupported question whose needed information is not identified. A different shape/fit or measure situation checks transfer. Legitimate curiosity is not labelled wrong simply because a bounded task cannot answer it. Solving remains separate, and the two new dependencies are soft.

[Silver's original account](https://flm-journal.org/Articles/2A5D152778141F58C1966ED8673C15.pdf) distinguishes generating problems from solving supplied ones. It supports this operation boundary; it is not used to claim a new teaching-efficacy result or establish the NCERT stage ages. The new claim is honestly added to the structured research dataset's missing-record list; existing research records remain intact.
