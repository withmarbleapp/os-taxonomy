## Concrete task protocol

Read the brief historical context and the three attributed case summaries aloud or offer them in text. Required facts are provided; target-language pronunciation, programme participation or cultural ceremony performance is not required.

Ask who leads a selected action, what is being renewed, and how that can connect people with family or community knowledge and identity. Require the historical context and contemporary agency, rather than accepting only generic respect. Then use another case to check transfer, naming its different people and source. A role/relationship diagram can show the reasoning without collecting personal testimony.

A strong explanation connects **disruption → community action → identity/belonging**, keeps Indigenous peoples contemporary and distinct, and transfers the relationship without treating a case as universal. Near misses include saying only that everyone should be respected, treating culture as permanently gone, or naming a programme without explaining its historical/identity relationship. Personal family disclosures are not requested.

This is a source-fit authoring protocol, not a classroom study or evidence that any learner passed. With the new claim and these resources/protocol, Grade 1 A1.5 is accounted for at the authoring level and changes from Partial to Full. Its earlier recognition component remains intact. No real-child records are required to establish that the design addresses the expectation.
