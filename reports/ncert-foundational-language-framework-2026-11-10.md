# NCERT Foundational language framework review

This slice reviews all 21 formal Foundational language records in the NCERT national baseline: three goals and 18 competencies. It uses the accepted 2023 framework's October 2024 printed edition, especially PDF pages 215 and 233–234. The official stage window is 3–8, with attainment referenced to stage end; the new contexts do not impose the same task at age three. The selected primary view remains Grades 1–2.

The result is 14 Full source-fit authoring dispositions, five ConditionalLanguageBinding dispositions, one ConditionalGraphRepresentation disposition and one ContextualReadingEngagement requirement. These categories are separate. A reviewed conditional requirement is not Full graph coverage, a missing topic, an excluded language or evidence that a child has attained anything. The [code-level report](ncert-foundational-language-framework-2026-11-10.json) retains the exact conditions and candidate paths. The [original protocols](ncert-foundational-language-protocols-2026-11-10.json) supply framework task designs rather than fabricated observations or a validated assessment battery.

The change adds 28 scoped associations through 15 existing topics. No authored claim changes. Five intrinsic dependency corrections are separately documented below; the language links do not remove any further gate. Each association is a direct shared-operation contribution, not a prerequisite-only link or a certificate that an English learner estimate proves proficiency in another language. The current schema does not bind a learner's actual language or script; consumers must preserve that distinction.

## Language roles and material binding

The framework identifies R1 as the language of first school literacy and R2 as another language. It does not require this review to select a nationwide Hindi/English pair. The source's practical language choices and the preference for a familiar R1 remain intact. The three-language policy is not an instruction to extend this primary slice into Middle-stage R3.

For the oral goal and its seven competencies, the precise exception for very unfamiliar R2 remains: emergence at Foundational stage end can be followed by consolidation early in Preparatory. This does not lower R1 requirements or create taxonomy difficulty levels. The role-labelled task records must state actual language, familiarity, materials, assessor competence and assistance when used. Evidence in one role is not copied across to the other.

A parameterised task is a concrete authoring design with declared inputs and response criteria. For example, the same conversation operation uses an unscripted exchange in each role, while the written-symbol task binds its inventory to the actual script and authoritative local materials. It does not invent a universal alphabet, impose English pronunciation, or require the user to choose all learner languages before framework curation can proceed.

## What is represented now

The shared claims cover conversation, descriptions, listening and narrative organisation, literary language, verse composition, vocabulary inference/use, syllable analysis, text purpose and main information. After separate review, the registered question-word correction permits the complete shared instruction candidate. The whole-claim tasks also retain broader requirements: syllable work includes written-word pronunciation support; the story-message claim includes informational connections; literary listening spans poems, stories and non-fiction; poetry composition remains distinct from merely repeating a supplied verse.

The syllable route uses expert-checked spoken units in the actual language. Spoken syllables are not automatically letters or akshar boundaries. A written word can support pronunciation analysis without pretending that its script divides like English. The source permits this route; a fixed English sound inventory is not added.

Verse creation in R2 can be oral, with optional recording or adult transcription. That directly contributes to the composition operation but does not demonstrate the existing written-poetry claim's complete mastery. Its complete written and recitation requirements remain intact and can be assessed separately through an appropriate R1 writing task. No new oral-versus-written clone is created.

Independent R1 reading remains independent reading. Listening to an adult tell a story is not substituted for decoding a text. Neither generic comprehension nor a topic association supplies the target-language vocabulary, script knowledge or actual learner evidence.

## Conditional graph and script cases

Five records retain explicit language/script binding: the two literacy goals, the R1 print-convention and full-symbol requirements, and the emergent R2 symbol/use requirement. English `Knowing all letters` requires a specific 26-letter, two-case inventory; `Understanding print` fixes a left-to-right path. Neither is published as a universal R1/R2 replacement. The parameter contracts require the actual inventory, reading path, punctuation, meaningful recognition and productive use. They do not invent a fixed frequent-symbol count.

One competency, expressive reading, retains conditional graph representation. Its candidate also remains conditional under the broader R1 literacy goal, giving two withheld contributions. The unchanged hard path is `mt_V-ldQp56bF` → `mt_ZhvwM6LMBL` → `mt_1KCwbGvm1F`, which fixes left-to-right print. A competent right-to-left reader is a counterexample to universalising this path, not proof that left-to-right knowledge is wrong for English.

The initial review withheld instruction and paragraph candidates too. The separately reviewed [five intrinsic prerequisite corrections](language-intrinsic-prerequisite-corrections-2026-11-10.md) and a complete source-task/hard-chain recheck now resolve the oral goal, instruction competency and paragraph competency. The unchanged instruction task covers receiving, clarifying, giving and repairing an ordered sequence. The paragraph task includes an authored focused piece and an independent supplied-text boundary repair, with meaningful headings and grouping. These source operations are accounted by the unchanged whole claims; Full was not calculated merely from softened-edge counts.

Initial dispositions and candidate paths remain in the JSON report and frozen Git checkpoints. The [resolution ledger](ncert-language-graph-resolutions-2026-11-10.json) records exact new contributions and retained boundaries. No further dependency changes, learner mastery transfer or universal script inference is made.

## Reading engagement

C-10.9 remains a formal in-scope contextual requirement. The protocol supplies access, choice and low-stakes opportunities to revisit varied suitable books, and separates access barriers from a child's response. It does not demand enthusiasm, a compulsory reading log or a stable positive-feelings score. Accurate reading and recommending books are different operations and do not establish voluntary interest. No learner field, score or new mastery topic is added.
