## Complete whole card

Name: **Energy resources and limited supplies**. Type CONCEPTUAL; Science / Energy.

Description: Trace familiar energy resources to the natural world and explain how repeated use affects a declared limited supply of useful fuel. Distinguish natural origin from processing, transport and local storage; explain why a new local delivery does not make the natural supply unlimited. Contrast these stock-limited fuel cases with sunlight and moving air without promising that either is available whenever needed.

Evidence:

1. Trace coal, oil and natural gas to natural underground sources, and sunlight and moving air to their natural origins. Distinguish a source from a shop, delivery or power station handling the resource.
2. Use a simple stated stock model to explain that repeated fuel use reduces the useful fuel available and cannot continue indefinitely when no replacement is supplied. Explain that this is a useful-resource limit, without claiming all energy or matter vanishes.
3. Track a transfer between two declared available supplies: explain which local store gains and which source supply loses, and why delivery changes local availability without creating unlimited natural resources. Respond to a changed supply/use case with the model's boundaries explicit.
4. Explain a fitting way to reduce unnecessary use while preserving a stated essential service, relating the choice to the limited supply. Correct the claims that all natural resources behave as identical disappearing piles and that wind/sunlight must be available at every requested time.

Assessment prompt: After study, can {{name}} explain where the fuels, sunlight and moving air come from, show what happens as a given fuel supply is used, and explain a delivery from a second supply? Can they choose a change that preserves the needed service and makes the given useful fuel last longer, without calling every natural resource the same kind of pile?

## Complete teaching guide and actual resources

Use the four complete original cards already authored in `ontario-grade1-energy-resource-supply-task-design-2026-11-12.json`: underground fuels, moving air/sunlight, finite-stock model and new delivery. They supply the facts rather than rely on a teacher inventing answers. Read them aloud and point to natural source, processing/distribution and local store. Use one different teaching transaction before the independent check.

Prepare two labelled paper locations, “available supply A” and “available supply B,” and a shop mat. Use ten counters at A, four at B and none at the shop. These are the two declared available supplies in this closed model; the numbers are original modelling conditions, not real reserve measurements. Move counters physically when a delivery occurs so the donor and recipient are both visible. A counter represents useful fuel before use, not every atom or residual material after burning. Used counters go to a marked “already used for this purpose” area rather than being said to disappear from the universe.

Show the three resource origins with the actual text cards; do not claim a power station or shop manufactures unlimited primary resources. Contrast sunlight at Earth and moving air with withdrawing pieces of coal from the particular declared supply. The contrast is about replenishment/availability, not a universal claim that a renewable source has no material/environmental limits.

Use two purpose cards: an occupied room must remain suitably warm under the model's conditions; a declared empty room needs no heating during that period. Model a choice preserving the needed service while avoiding unnecessary use. Children explain the case; there is no real fuel handling, heating adjustment or necessary-service curtailment.

Put explanatory cards away before assessment. Read neutral case facts if needed, but do not supply the source/stock explanation. The learner may move counters, draw fewer/more marks, point or speak; an isolated counting slip is recorded separately from resource reasoning. Teach again after recording the attempt and use a changed case to recheck.

## Full independent assessment and key

Ask the learner to trace the three fuels and two contrasting resources to their natural origins. Present a fresh model with six counters at supply A, four at B and a local store initially empty. Move two from A to the store; then the stated job uses the two. Ask which supply changed, what remains useful for later jobs and whether repeating the same job can go on forever without replacement. The key is finite useful stocks under stated conditions, not exact formal totals.

Now deliver two counters from B to the store. Ask what grew locally, what decreased at B, and whether that proves an unlimited natural source. Both supplies were declared available before the delivery, so the model does not confuse new accessibility with creation. Ask what changes if no more delivery is allowed, or if the local store receives fuel from a third explicitly supplied deposit; more available fuel can extend use without proving unlimited total resources.

Ask the learner to choose between keeping the stated essential room suitably warm and heating the declared empty room too. Require the causal useful-supply consequence and preservation of the need. Then change the case: the second room is now occupied and also needs warmth. A previous “leave it unheated” answer no longer follows; require a different appropriate plan or acknowledgment that the supplied information is insufficient. No preferred real household heating instruction is implied.

Finally ask whether all natural resources must be the same kind of pile, and whether wind can be demanded during a declared still period. Accept the source-specific difference and availability limit without demanding the words renewable/nonrenewable. Full requires all four evidence criteria, the two-location transfer and changed need. Repetition, correct arithmetic alone, generic saving slogans or saying every resource/energy vanishes is insufficient.
