# Ontario Grade 4 Mathematics — 24 October 2026

The Grade 4 pass maps the official 2020 curriculum onto the shared taxonomy after a reuse audit. All 60 retained expectations were checked against staging: 48 specific and 12 overall. Source wording, source IDs and stored hierarchy are unchanged. [Official Ontario source](https://www.dcp.edu.gov.on.ca/en/curriculum/elementary-mathematics/grades/g4-math).

The pass adds **186 Grade 4 topic-code pairs across 130 topics** (126 existing and four new). Fifty codes are directly linked; ten overall expectations roll up their children. The graph contains **1,842 topics / 3,919 dependencies**. Every old pair is preserved, including Grade 1's 152 and Grade 2's 124 pairs. One genuine Grade 3 foundation component increases that grade from 155 to 156 pairs.

Only two new topics represent missing mathematical or computing knowledge: **nested loops in code** and **stem-and-leaf plots**. **Finding the median** and **multiple-bar graphs** extract focused assessment claims from broader existing statistics/display topics, whose content remains intact. The median covers odd/even data counts, fractional results and ties. Nested loops support block or text code; geometry and conditional examples are not cumulative prerequisites. Stem-and-leaf plots retain repeated values and use an explicit place-value key. Multiple-bar graphs distinguish series through a key and avoid confusing counts with proportions for unequal group sizes.

Thirteen hard prerequisites become supportive soft links where they imposed an optional sequence, advanced bundle or method. Nine hard and three soft dependencies are added on existing topics; the four new topics add five hard, eleven supportive soft and four incoming soft dependencies. Decimal equivalents retain generic fraction and place-value foundations rather than requiring prior hundredths-counting or a full percentages bundle. Written and mental multiplication retain calculation foundations without requiring all 12×12 tables or timed facts. Inequalities retain ordering and inverse-operation foundations. All existing topic descriptions, evidence, questions, types, subjects and domains remain unchanged. Two cosmetic title repairs remove misleading age suffixes: **Adding and subtracting decimals** and **Partitioning shapes into equal shares**.

Eight additional source-backed component links support the foundations: four Common Core number/fraction expectations, three English fraction expectations and Ontario Grade 3 B2.6 on reading/writing multiplication/division sentences representing arrays. No source catalogue row or global age band was added. No topics were merged or retired, and no learner or teaching-research records were changed.

The actual Canadian hard-prerequisite closure contains **130 holders / 225 topics / 270 hard edges**, with zero missing or later-age prerequisite issues. All fourteen new hard edges pass checks for every source country. No previously clear edge/country context becomes flagged. The existing US rounding-to-four-digit-place-value flag remains on the same edge; its diagnostic changes from a missing US source to a later US age after a genuine Grade 4 component is added. No age was invented to clear it. The full hard and soft graph is acyclic; stored graph/DB fields, source pairs, age bounds, centrality and embedding presence are independently verified.

All 48 specific and 12 overall expectations are judged Full through the listed claims and rollups. This is a contextual curriculum composition judgment, not certification that a learner has mastered every facet of a broader linked topic. Investing uses a business investment/profit-or-loss context; donating uses time, which the official teacher supports allow. These contexts do not certify investment products or cash-donation skills. The mapping does not recertify other Ontario documents or previous Australian coverage judgments.

No teaching research was added. The portable projection retains **1,049 unchanged records** and now lists **793 topics without research**, including the four new claims. The historical research archive is unchanged.
