### Oral account A: place and activity

« À Toronto, le campus Spadina de l'Alliance Française est un lieu pour apprendre le français. Son adresse est le 24, chemin Spadina. On y trouve des salles de classe, un espace d'exposition et un théâtre. On peut y découvrir des activités culturelles. Sur la photo, je vois une entrée et une bannière. La photo seule ne montre pas ce qui se passe à l'intérieur. Pour cela, je lis aussi la présentation du campus. »

Teacher key: actual Toronto place; learning and cultural activity; public account, not photo alone, supports interior uses. Two facts may be address and presence of teaching/cultural spaces. The photograph supplies an observed feature; it does not independently establish institutional history. Ask why this place can matter socially/culturally and require a relation such as meeting/learning/sharing art, not merely “important because famous”.

### Oral account B: group and documented outward contribution

« La Franco-Fête est un festival de Toronto. Son histoire commence en 1983. Ce groupe organise des activités culturelles en français. En 2015, il a participé au volet culturel francophone des Jeux panaméricains et parapanaméricains de Toronto. C'est un exemple précis de contribution à la vie culturelle d'un grand événement. Cela ne veut pas dire que tous les habitants de Toronto ont participé au festival. »

Teacher key: a named local group, French-language cultural activity, dated concrete contribution to the Games' cultural programme. Do not demand a pupil know sports results, claim every athlete heard it, infer measured international impact or treat the mission alone as the contribution.

**Un lieu pour le français — Toronto**

Le campus Spadina de l'Alliance Française se trouve au 24, chemin Spadina, à Toronto. Il comprend des salles de classe, un espace d'exposition et un théâtre. Des personnes y apprennent le français et découvrent des activités culturelles. Le lieu a donc un rôle éducatif et culturel. La photo aide à reconnaître son entrée; la présentation du campus nous renseigne sur ses usages.

## Four independent source protocols

Quelle activité en français as-tu entendue ? Quels détails t'aident ? An English content test or reading the transcript cannot supply listening evidence.

For Grade 3 add the complete Ottawa/Sudbury accounts and one new supported Ontario comparison. ## Full hard-path support and independent boundaries

All 16 complete cards, nine hard edges and four saved learn/assess guides were read. Twelve cards have no saved operational guide; this absence is explicit. The following original support tasks keep the existing complete ancestor operations available without awarding any ancestor from culture-only success or changing an age interval:

- **Questions and past:** introduce `qui/quoi/où/quand/pourquoi/comment` with the photo and dated festival timeline. Learner asks and answers appropriate questions, explains how question words signal a question, then constructs three questions for an older person's childhood (`À quels jeux jouais-tu ? Où jouais-tu ? Avec qui jouais-tu ?`). No actual grandparent interview or family history disclosure is needed. For an old source/photo ask who used it and when, and pose a past/present place question. Do not invent its answer if the source lacks it.
- **Familiar/special place:** use the actual classroom/school place rather than claim the campus is a place the child belongs to. Learner draws it and labels two observed features, explains their own reason it matters (or an actual school-group reason where appropriate), and suggests a fitting care action. No compulsory positive emotion, family claim or spiritual affiliation.
- **Natural/managed/built:** using adult-checked local school-area photos or an actual accessible school observation, identify a natural feature, managed planted area and constructed feature. Learner sorts actual examples, locates them on their own simple neighbourhood map using position words, and explains why a maintained planted area is managed despite natural plants. If the actual locality lacks a category in view, supply a correctly attributed local image rather than falsely relabel the campus photo. Record actual evidence before whole-claim credit; the culture resource does not certify this repertoire.
- **Complete land/water model:** use the original declared story setting `Un village près d'une vallée`: a mountain lies north of a valley; hills lie east; a plain lies south; a river passes through the valley to a lake south of it, then towards an ocean beyond the plain. The learner names/describes mountain, valley, hill and plain and river, lake and ocean; constructs/draws a model retaining those shapes/relative positions; explains a top-down view and one detail intentionally omitted. Contrast side and top views; blue water on a map is not sky. Water flowing downhill is an age-appropriate model condition, not a claim about every real drainage path. This declared story is not Toronto's topography or a local survey. Use the unchanged complete existing guide for additional photograph/real-setting work when teaching the whole claim; no geographic whole credit follows from a festival location dot.
- **Sentence and oral rehearsal:** contrast `Le campus.` (fragment) with `Le campus accueille des activités.` (complete subject/verb statement); learner composes another complete sentence, expands it with a supported detail, says it aloud more than once, rehearses with a partner and then writes independently. Content must remain accurate; no copied full model or imagined fact.
- **Writing vocabulary and planning:** explicitly distinguish `composer`, `planifier`, `brouillon`, `réviser`, `corriger`, `relire`, `genre`, `destinataire`, `but`, `récit`, `compte rendu`, `consigne`, `paragraphe`, `ordre` and `détail`. Learner uses compose/revise/edit distinctions, names audience and purpose and explains a matching choice; orally rehearses, jots ideas/key words and plans a separate original story with beginning/middle/end before drafting. A factual timeline alone does not establish the story-planning criterion. Use original scene “a class prepares an exhibition, loses a label, checks the source, replaces the label accurately” as a fictional story seed; do not present it as a real class event. Pupil supplies complete sentences, details and sequence.
- **Form/audience:** retain the earlier complete writing-process binding and independently choose form/tone before drafting, then change the intended reader. Record meaning/structure changes, not only changed salutations.

These tasks and the public prior bindings supply a teaching/assessment route for the whole hard path.
