## six-machine-primer

A lever is a rigid bar that turns about a support; a selected long effort arm can help lift a load. A ramp is an inclined plane: a sloping surface for raising a load along a longer route. A wedge narrows to an edge and can separate material. A screw has a spiral thread and can hold parts or lift a load. A pulley guides a rope; a fixed pulley can change pulling direction, while a suitable moving-pulley arrangement can reduce effort. A wheel and axle turn together: turning a large knob or crank can turn its smaller shaft.
These are useful physical arrangements, not six kinds of motor. Their benefit depends on the arrangement. They do not create energy or guarantee less force in every configuration. Gear-force vocabulary, formulae for moments and numerical mechanical advantage are not this source task.

## library-ramp-and-cart

In fictional CommunityA a team must move a box of books onto a low platform. The same box can be lifted directly or drawn along a stable ramp. The declared ramp route takes longer travel but less pulling force for this selected arrangement. A cart rolls the boxes along a level path, reducing resistance compared with dragging that same load in this model. The vehicle wheels are not claimed to be the same lifting arrangement as the knob-and-shaft example.
The model gives the team enough route space and adult-selected safe equipment. It also declares a narrow route where the cart cannot pass; carrying smaller loads on another suitable route is an available alternative. Learners compare access, effort, space and assistance needs rather than say newer technology is always better.

## water-pulley-community

In fictional CommunityB a rope over one fixed overhead pulley lets an adult pull down while raising the same water container. Compared with lifting directly, the direction of effort changes. This one fixed pulley does not by itself halve the ideal force. The model also supplies a suitable two-supporting-segment moving-pulley arrangement with lower effort but a longer pull.
People benefit differently depending on reach, grip, available space and maintenance. In this model a damaged rope is removed from use until a responsible adult repairs/replaces it. Learners explain the benefit and changed limitation; they do not operate a well, lift water, suspend loads or test damaged equipment.

## movement-and-environment

This is an original comparative model, not measured local data. For the same short book-delivery route and load, the model supplies three options: dragging a box, a hand-pushed cart with wheels/axles, and a fuel-powered vehicle. The supplied fuel vehicle emits exhaust on this journey; the manual cart has no exhaust from burning fuel during use. Building either option uses materials; the model does not say the manual option has no environmental footprint.
The existing firm path can carry the cart while retaining nearby habitat. A proposed widened path removes declared animal cover and vegetation. A longer existing route avoids removing that stated habitat but needs more time. Learners assess this movement technology’s benefit and environmental costs under both route conditions, not label a machine universally green.

## Six source-supported everyday uses

- lever: A selected crowbar arrangement moves a nail by turning about its support; the learner studies a drawing, not a working tool.
- inclined plane: A ramp gives a longer sloping path for raising a load.
- wedge: A tapered doorstop fits under and holds an ordinary door in the supplied example; no emergency-exit use is prescribed.
- pulley: A fixed pulley lets a person pull down to raise a flag or selected load. This direction benefit does not by itself prove reduced force.
- wheel and axle: A large knob turns its connected smaller shaft, as in the selected doorknob arrangement.
- screw: A threaded fastener turns to join selected materials; a picture/model supplies the example, not child tool handling.

## Force-comparison teaching and independent assessment

Original illustrative model data, not observed classroom results. Every row uses the same load and start/end height. The values are input-force readings in the same arbitrary force units, measured along the stated direction during slow, steady movement. They compare required push/pull force, not time, distance, energy use or total work. Values describe selected arrangements, not universal constants.

| Arrangement | Input force (same units) | Input direction / distance |
|---|---:|---|
| direct vertical lift | 10 | up |
| selected longer smooth ramp | 6 | up along the longer ramp |
| selected lever, effort farther from support than load | 4 | down at the effort end, over a greater distance |
| one fixed overhead pulley | 10 | down on the free rope |
| selected moving pulley with two supporting rope segments | 6 | along the free rope over a greater distance; this model includes resistance |

- Compare EACH machine row with direct lift: less/equal/greater input force, plus a direction or distance difference. Comparing machines only with each other or comparing time/distance instead of force is insufficient.
- Identify direction change without force reduction; use evidence to reject the claim that every pulley halves force.
- Changed lever: effort closer to the support than the load, supplied input force15 for the same load/height. Explain why the first lever conclusion cannot be repeated.
- Reject a comparison using a different heavier load, stating what must match.

A supplied-data comparison can account for the source comparison operation. It does not award whole Testing Push & Pull Designs mastery, which requires actual building, testing, recording, improving and re-testing.

kind:

materials:
Adult-selected low-load tabletop ramp, toy lever with stable pivot, pulley education kit and force indicator with its instructions; no suspended/heavy loads or sharp wedges/working tools. A supplied-data version can be used but is labelled as a model, not a child observation.

controlledComparisons:

- Same load/height: direct slow lift vs one fixed pulley, then a suitable moving-pulley arrangement. Include fixed-pulley direction change as a countercase to all-machines-halve-force.
