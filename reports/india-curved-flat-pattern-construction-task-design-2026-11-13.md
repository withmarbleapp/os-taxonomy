## Exact proposed claim

Neutral title: **Constructing cylinders and cones from flat patterns**. Procedural Mathematics / Geometry. Assemble closed cylinders and cones from purpose-designed supplied flat pieces, explain which piece forms each surface and which edges meet, and use a changed or mismatched set to predict and check whether the pieces form the intended closed solid. Independent net design, circumference/sector formulae and surface-area calculation are separate.

Four evidence criteria:

1. From a supplied rectangle and two matching circles, predict which edges should join, then actually roll/join the rectangle and attach the circles to make a closed cylinder.
2. From a supplied circular sector and matching circle, predict the join, then actually roll/join the straight sides to make the cone and attach its base.
3. Explain the correspondence between flat pieces and curved/flat surfaces, and inspect joins for gaps, overlaps and closure, distinguishing the mathematical surface from tape/card thickness.
4. With fresh supplied patterns, construct both solids again and explain why a deliberately mismatched base or a wrong-edge join fails; choose a fitting alternative and check it by construction. Adult completion or recognition of a finished model alone does not pass.

Assessment prompt: Given these fresh flat pieces, can the learner show how to make a closed cylinder and cone, predict which edges meet, explain what each piece becomes, and find/fix a base that does not fit?

## Full original teaching and assessment protocol

<a id="teach-cylinder"></a>

Teach A first. Lay the pieces flat; let pupil feel/trace the rectangle and circle boundaries and predict what can become a curved side and closed ends. Ask which rectangle edges should meet. Commit to a prediction before assembly. Roll without sharp folds, align the correct edges and tape externally; attach both circles, using short external tape pieces without cutting away a mismatched mathematical base. Trace the straight paper edge now forming a circular rim. Inspect actual gaps and explain why the original flat rectangle is now a curved lateral surface, while circles remain flat bases. Naming a cylinder without doing this is insufficient.

<a id="teach-cone"></a>

Teach B independently. Trace the two straight radial edges and the curved arc on the supplied sector, which can be called a fan-shaped piece without requiring new formal vocabulary. Predict the result; bring the straight edges together, roll/tape, and fit the base circle. Trace the arc becoming the base rim and the joined straight edges running toward the tip. Do not crease the curved side into polyhedral faces. Open/reassemble if needed to see the correspondence. Explain why a full disk with no suitable radial join or a triangle is not the supplied lateral pattern. No derivation of216degrees or circumference is required.

Contrast a supplied existing cube net separately: its straight fold lines make distinct flat faces, whereas A/B roll a curved surface. This comparison does not certify the old polyhedral whole claim; its complete independent operations remain separately assessed. Likewise a cone placed on an already made cylinder is a Building3D composition task, not evidence the pupil made either surface.

<a id="independent-c-d"></a>

Remove completed A/B models and step-by-step solutions. Supply fresh C/D pieces plus A's larger circle as a distractor for C. Learner predicts, selects and constructs C and D independently, explains the part/surface mapping and checks actual closure. C needs its15mm-radius bases; the20mm-radius circle is too large and cannot be made correct merely by crushing/folding the rim. D must work despite not looking like B's fan. Ask pupil to demonstrate why joining the wrong rectangle edges changes its rim and prevents the supplied bases fitting. A supported accessible way to hold/tape is allowed and recorded; adult choice of joins/assembly is not scored as independent pupil construction.

<a id="changed-pattern-transfer"></a>

For the changed case, give a stated practical need: keep B's base and use the actually supplied D pattern to make a taller cone, then compare B/D height after construction. Both B and D fit the30mm base, but D is taller; no assumption that same base fixes the cone's whole form. A different cylinder-height variation is optional only if an additional checked matching pattern is actually supplied; no such additional asset is needed or claimed in this packet. Do not ask for unsupplied independent net design under this claim. Error feedback returns to actual edge/curve matching, predicts again and checks rather than asserting failure from appearance.
