# Reusable oral and writing prerequisites — 11 November 2026

Four whole-claim prerequisites become soft support. The topic descriptions,
evidence, assessment prompts and teaching guides do not change. The
[exact correction register](language-reusable-prerequisite-corrections-2026-11-11.json)
records each preimage, postimage and discriminating counterexample.

| Procedure | Previous hard prerequisite | Why the complete prerequisite is supporting knowledge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paraphrasing What You Hear | Main Ideas & Note-Taking | Accurate spoken restatement can demonstrate the complete heard-material operation without independent multi-paragraph reading or written notes. |
| Writing to inform | Writing Process Vocabulary | Relevant topic facts can be conveyed through drawing, dictation or writing without naming the entire writing-process glossary. |
| Simple Stories with Beginning and Ending | Writing Process Vocabulary | Sequenced written sentences, temporal words and an ending/reaction do not require correct use of every process and genre term. |
| Saying Sentences Before Writing Them | Writing Process Vocabulary | Complete oral rehearsal, repetition and partner practice can succeed without explaining the whole process glossary. |

Each prerequisite remains available as useful support. All other edges and the
full procedural evidence remain. In particular, the writing routes still reach
sentence building; expressive reading still reaches print direction and sight-word
requirements. This change does not make an arbitrary language/script compatible
with every whole claim.

The [Preparatory language review](ncert-preparatory-language-framework-2026-11-10.json)
retains the original hard paths as historical evidence and records the complete
remaining current hard paths separately. Five graph-conditional records and two
language-conditional goals are affected, but their dispositions and withheld
candidate associations remain unchanged. Actual role-bound materials, assessment
protocols and all remaining claim requirements need their own compatibility
review before those candidates are associated. R1 and R2 remain source-defined
roles, with separate language evidence; no fixed national pair is selected.

The source-duty context remains the [NCF-SE 2023 framework, October 2024 printed
edition](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf), selected Preparatory
language tables on PDF pages 236–240. This is an original prerequisite rationale,
not a redistribution of NCERT wording or a claim of intervention efficacy.

No source record, scoped association, age, topic ID or guide is added or removed.
No learner estimate, historical evidence or downstream publication changes.
