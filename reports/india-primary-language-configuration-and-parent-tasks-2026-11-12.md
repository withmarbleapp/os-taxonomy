# Ten language configuration records: finite framework task packet

This is a reviewed framework authoring packet for the ten remaining ConditionalLanguageBinding records in the formal primary framework. It supplies concrete tasks and independently assessed parent portfolios. No disposition, association, claim, age or hard edge is changed here. Framework authoring with configuration requirements is distinct from certifying every named-language programme. Historical judgments remain visible; the independent source/task/path/material peer found no substantive blocker.

Configure each role separately using the [existing role-configuration contract](india-primary-language-role-configuration-contract-2026-11-12.md). Declare actual language/variety/script, taught symbol inventory and authoritative local reference, appropriate complete texts, accepted forms/readings, access supports and competent checking before delivery. The examples here are original English illustrations only, desk-checked and untrialled; they do not choose a national pair or credit one product to both roles. A language may use an alphabet, akshar system or other script conventions; no fixed English inventory, direction, capitalization or grammar is imposed universally.

## Concrete configuration modules

<a id="print-book-conventions"></a>

**Print and book conventions (Foundation R1 C-10.2; Preparatory R2 C-2.2).** Use the actual [original English print sample](assets/india-primary-language/original-english-print-sample.svg) for this illustration: cover/title, two numbered interior pages, separated words and the punctuation described below. For another configuration, supply its corresponding actual layout/text before delivery. English illustration: cover “A Map for Our Table”; page 1 “Mina draws a path. ‘Where does it go?’ asks Dev.”; page 2 “‘It goes to the books,’ says Mina. Dev adds a label: BOOKS.” The pupil identifies cover/title, where to begin, the actual reading path and return between lines/pages, boundaries between words and the function of the punctuation actually present. Ask them to point to the evidence, not infer knowledge from fluent recitation. Change the layout and text: a notice says “Story morning: Friday, 10:00, Room 4. Bring a drawing if you choose.” Ask for its beginning, selected word boundaries and meaning of the actual punctuation. For another script, change the layout and key to its genuine conventions; a pupil who correctly reads right-to-left is not wrong for failing the English illustration.

<a id="script-inventory"></a>

**Script inventory (Foundation R1 C-10.3; Foundation R2 C-11.2).** Bind the actual taught inventory and forms to a source chart before delivery. Do not equate all possible Unicode combinations with the intended inventory or invent a universal symbol count. In an English R1 illustration, supply the complete uppercase and lowercase A–Z/a–z chart, mixed-order recognition cards and a fresh productive word-writing case. Recognition in chart order alone is insufficient; mix position, case and context, then use the recognised forms to read/write words. For R2, identify the actual frequently occurring forms in the chosen introductory materials and assess their use in words and simple sentences; do not silently require the entire R1 inventory. English R2 illustration words are cat, map, sun and red, with sentences “The map is red.” and “The cat is in the sun.” The configured inventory/key records the letters actually used. Change to a separately checked new word/sentence set sharing taught forms. A word copied from an adult does not establish independent productive use. An English alphabet whole claim is used only where its complete actual-English criteria fit; it is not linked as a universal script proxy.

<a id="actual-language-grammar"></a>

**Actual-language grammar and structure (Preparatory R1 C-3.4).** Configure a brief grammar key covering the sentence and text structures required in the actual material, with accepted varieties explicit. English illustration: compare “The children carries the map” with “The children carry the map,” and a clear paragraph with a pronoun whose referent is missing. The pupil repairs the actual mismatch, explains the intended meaning and writes a fresh clear text; selecting a correction without producing usable writing is insufficient. Teach with a different example. For independent transfer, change subject/number, time reference and audience, and ask the pupil to revise for the new meaning. Do not impose English articles, adjective order or mandatory overt pronouns on another language. Source grammar competence is not equated with naming a full glossary. Retain all separately associated whole claims and their actual-language compatibility requirements.

## Independently assessed integrated parents

Each parent has its own fresh integrated task. The adult may read neutral instructions and provide access support, but cannot supply the content, word readings, sentence construction or correction being assessed. Record configuration, assistance and actual evidence separately. Missing programme configuration is not evidence that a generic curriculum concept is absent.

<a id="foundation-r1-integrated"></a>

**Foundation R1 CG-10: reading and writing together.** Supply the actual configured two-page sample plus a fresh story passage, an original poem, a short model news item, written instructions, recipe and publicity notice. Retain the complete expressive-reading and written-composition checks in the role contract. Have the pupil read a fresh appropriate passage accurately with suitable phrasing/expression, explain its people/events and an evidence-supported interpretation of what the writer communicates, then write an original coherent paragraph explaining the task or an ordinary actual classroom experience. The pupil uses the configured inventory and print conventions in this integrated work. Independently change the passage/topic and check a new paragraph, rather than count the component tags.

Concrete English supplementary reading materials: model news item, explicitly fictional—“A gust moved the display map this morning. The class added paper tabs. Visitors can now see the paths.” Written instructions—“Put the map flat. Add a label beside each path. Check that the words can be read.” Recipe—“Fruit bowl: one banana and one apple. Ask an adult to prepare the fruit. Put the pieces in a bowl and mix with a spoon.” Reading the recipe is the task; no preparation is required. Publicity notice is the story-morning notice above. Ask for each text's relevant information and purpose; one memorised story cannot replace all reading forms. For poetry, use the original paper-map verse in the [creative portfolio](india-primary-creative-writing-role-portfolio-2026-11-12.md) and invite an evidence-based response to its wording/imagination. Appreciation is not compulsory liking.

Keep phonological analysis in the actual language: a configured English example blends/segments the sounds in map, or blends/segments syllables in window and table, with accepted pronunciation recorded. The source permits phonemes or syllables; neither English phonics nor a fixed English irregular-word bank is made a universal gate. Book access/choice and the separate reading-interest duty retain their contextual channel. The parent fluency judgment does not automatically promote that separate affect/engagement record or award every broad topic.

<a id="foundation-r2-integrated"></a>

**Foundation R2 CG-11: emergent reading and writing.** Use a separately configured introductory R2 set; do not import the R1 portfolio's results or demand identical fluency. After teaching one set, independently blend/segment the appropriate spoken unit, recognise frequent written forms, read fresh simple words/sentences and write meaningful words/sentences with those forms. The English illustration uses the R2 word/sentence set above; a fresh checked change replaces the noun/location while retaining taught forms. Record which forms were recognised and independently used. A traced letter or memorised spoken sentence does not demonstrate the complete reading/writing operation. A genuine source unfamiliar-R2 exception is used only at its actual scope; it does not erase R2 learning.

<a id="preparatory-r1-oral"></a>

**Preparatory R1 CG-1: oral understanding and expression.** Listen to a new actual-R1 explanation, select and summarise its central ideas, converse with a partner about a practical choice and make a short coherent presentation explaining a different familiar process. Use the original read-aloud and changed accounts from the role contract, then supply a fresh decision: arrange a reading area so people can find materials and move around it. The pupil proposes a plan, listens to the partner's constraint, responds meaningfully and revises the explanation. Check listening, central meaning, own coherent expression and configured sentence structures independently, including an independently generated complex expression. English teaching model: “Because the path is narrow, we will put the table beside the wall.” The dependent cause clause and the main plan must both carry meaningful information. Fresh independent premise: “Visitors cannot see the map from the chairs; the doorway must remain clear.” Ask the pupil to explain a plan with a cause, time or condition relation using an actual complex structure, without supplying its wording. Accepted English examples include “If visitors cannot see the map, I will move it beside the table” and “When visitors arrive, we will keep the doorway clear.” A simple/compound-only answer may express a good plan but does not satisfy this explicit complex-structure check. A competent actual-language checker declares the corresponding dependent/main-clause or other appropriate complex structure and accepted forms before delivery; English conjunctions and word order are not imposed universally. Change the constraint to an unavailable table and require a new independent complex explanation rather than copied phrasing. A prepared monologue alone is insufficient. The summary component retains its exact 10–11 interval; this integrated stage-end offer does not claim earlier readiness for that entire broader topic or import compulsory written note-taking.

<a id="preparatory-r1-writing"></a>

**Preparatory R1 CG-3: understanding/experience in simple and compound structures.** After the pupil makes or revises an actual paper display, write an independently authored explanation of what they did and why, using simple sentences and appropriate coordinated/compound structures in actual R1. English illustration teaching contrast: “The map moved. We held it down.” versus “The map moved, so we held it down.” A second contrast uses “and” to add a fact and “but” to mark a contrast. The independent pupil text uses its own content and fitting relations, not copied conjunctions. Ask the pupil to organise related material, use a useful heading where appropriate, revise an ambiguous reference and transfer to a changed arrangement. A grammar key checks the actual language's structures; English conjunctions/order are not universal substitutes. The creative forms task remains separate source evidence, not automatic parent promotion by child count.

## Exact fresh Preparatory R2 reading inputs and keys

These original English illustrations are distinct from the previously supplied story passages and creative-writing poem. Configure actual-R2 equivalents and accepted readings before delivery; do not credit the English result to another role or language.

<a id="fresh-r2-poem"></a>

**Fresh poem:**

“Window squares of morning light
Make the reading corner bright.
On the table, maps unfold;
Paper paths have tales to hold.”

Ask the pupil to read the actual written poem, explain what scene the words suggest and point to a phrase supporting the interpretation. Accept a grounded explanation of light in the reading corner and imaginative maps/paths suggesting stories; do not require liking it, one fixed moral or the exact words of this key. Record the actual words, phrasing and response separately from memorised recital.

<a id="fresh-r2-information"></a>

**Fresh information passage, declared classroom model:** “A classroom map is a small drawing of a place. A key explains its marks. A blue square can stand for a table if the key says so. Map marks show where things are; they do not have to show every detail.” Key questions: What does the key do? Must every blue square always mean table? Why can a map leave out details? Correct meaning is that the key specifies the marks; the table meaning depends on this key, and a useful map preserves needed positions without copying every detail. A remembered colour rule without reading the actual condition is insufficient. No whole map-making mastery follows from this reading task.

<a id="changed-r2-information"></a>

**Changed information passage, declared model:** “Our reading box has three pockets. One holds picture stories, one holds facts and one holds poems. A label names what goes in each pocket. Labels help people put books back. A label does not tell us whether a reader will like a book.” Ask what the labels help people do, whether they predict enjoyment and how the three pockets differ. The pupil must derive the new meaning from this actual text, rather than reuse the map-key answer. Access to books and voluntary preference remain contextual, with no quota or positive-feelings score.
