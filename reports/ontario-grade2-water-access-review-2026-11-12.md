## Complete new card

Name: **Water availability and drinking-water access**. Type CONCEPTUAL; Geography / Places & Environments.

Description: Distinguish physical fresh-water availability from safe, usable drinking-water access in particular communities. Compare accurately attributed cases from different world regions, explaining how water sources, distance, treatment/supply arrangements and changing conditions affect daily life. State what the case evidence establishes and what remains unknown instead of inferring safety or access from the mere presence of water.

Evidence:

1. Distinguish water being physically present, its fresh/salty condition, and safe drinking water being obtainable where and when people need it. Explain why a nearby freshwater source or a pipe alone does not prove all those conditions.
2. Describe and compare at least two accurately attributed studied community cases from different world regions, identifying the relevant natural water input and access/supply arrangement while retaining place, date and case limits.
3. Explain at least two case-supported consequences for community life, connecting an access condition or change to collection time, school/daily activities or supply reliability. Do not generalise a community case to everyone in a country or assume infrastructure removes every problem.
4. Apply the distinction to a fresh declared changed case: reason separately about physical water, access and quality, explain the resulting community consequence and identify information still needed. Do not invent an observed result, guaranteed solution or safety judgement.

Assessment prompt: After studying communities in different parts of the world, can {{name}} explain where their water comes from, how people obtain drinking water and how that affects daily life? If the water source remains but the supply route stops working, can they explain what changed and what they still need to know, without deciding that all water nearby must be safe?

## Complete guide and accessible resources

Teach the three actual dated community cards already authored in the public household-water resource, with their original UNICEF attribution. The cases are Khormaloq in Afghanistan, São Francisco Macuxi in Brazil and selected Điện Biên schools in Viet Nam. Read the actual cards aloud; identify the cases as particular places and a dated account. They represent different world settings, not every household in a country. Use an ordinary world map or a teacher-supplied location description to locate the countries; a full independent map-making repertoire is not the gate.

Use the public lake/treatment/tower/pipe, groundwater/well and rain/roof/tank route cards as additional original models. Explain three separate questions: Is water physically present? What access route/arrangement is supplied? What quality/suitability facts are actually known? In a declared model, fresh water contains little salt compared with seawater; that distinction alone is not a drinking-water test. A frozen fresh-water store or a distant river is not automatically a usable home supply. No water tasting, testing or treatment procedure is taught or required.

Construct a three-column comparison mat headed source/input, access/quality facts and consequence for daily life. Model one separate fictional case, then have the learner construct the rows for the actual community cases using the case cards. Keep factual source information available while learning. If a child gives only “they have water,” ask which of the three questions that answers. If they generalise from a case to a whole country, return to the named place/date and the evidence boundary.

Do not present a water pipe, nearby source or a photograph of clear water as a quality guarantee. Explain that source protection, treatment and distribution can all matter; the role of each is supported by the already read EPA source-water body. WHO's service definition is used only to substantiate the distinction between access/availability and quality, not as a child vocabulary, clinical or numerical threshold requirement.

## Actual dated read-aloud case cards

These original paraphrases retain the dated UNICEF attribution, not current national claims. Facts may be read neutrally; explanatory rows and keys are put away for the independent task.

### Khormaloq village, Farah Province, Afghanistan

Account: UNICEF,25August2025. The account contrasts a previously distant stream, described as unsafe, with a solar-powered piped supply near homes. Families previously made repeated collection journeys.

Teacher consequence key: Near-home supply changes the collection burden and access for daily household use. Evidence limit: The account is dated and local. Do not infer present conditions, every household in Afghanistan, or a detailed treatment process not supplied.

### São Francisco Macuxi community, Roraima, Brazil

Account: UNICEF,25August2025. River collection involved travel and seasonal difficulties. A community-identified well supplied a new system. The account reports less time needed to collect water.

Teacher consequence key: Reduced collection time leaves more time for other daily activities. Evidence limit: Reported operation during drought does not prove an inexhaustible source or guarantee every future season. Do not generalise to all Brazil.

### Selected schools in Điện Biên, Viet Nam

Account: UNICEF,25August2025. The account describes collecting and treating rainwater for daily school use.

Teacher consequence key: The supplied collection/treatment arrangement supports daily water use at the studied schools. Evidence limit: Do not invent a measured attendance gain, claim all schools share this arrangement, or describe the fictional empty-tank model as a reported event.

## Actual independent task, changed cases and key

Remove the completed comparison rows and explanatory answers. Keep the neutral dated case facts available or read them neutrally; this assesses the learner's comparison and causal reasoning rather than memorising names. Ask the learner to construct their own comparison of two actual cases from different world regions, identifying the natural water input, the supplied access arrangement and a specific daily-life consequence. Use the third actual case as a fresh comparison. A copied headline or a global volume chart alone is insufficient.

Then provide these complete original changed-case cards, explicitly models rather than reported UNICEF events:

**P:** A declared freshwater lake remains beside a town. The pipe bringing treated water to a school is broken. No substitute delivery is supplied. Ask what physical source remains, which access changed and what daily activity could be affected. The key separates remaining water from unavailable school delivery; it does not assert a new measurement or a repair already done.

**Q:** A working tap is close to homes. Its water quality has not been supplied or checked in the case. Ask whether closeness alone proves safe drinking water and what information is missing. The key recognises access convenience while withholding an unsupported safety conclusion; it does not declare the actual tap unsafe.

**R:** A school model's roof/tank supplies water after treatment. The tank is now empty during a declared dry period and no refill is supplied. Ask whether opening its outlet creates water or guarantees supply; explain one possible effect on daily school use and the further supply information needed. Do not call this a reported event in Viet Nam or invent a guaranteed alternative.

Full performance requires accurate two-case comparison and third-case transfer, supported community consequences, separate source/access/quality reasoning across changed cases and an explicit evidence limit. Naming water sources alone, stereotyped country claims, copying supplied explanations or invented guaranteed solutions are insufficient. Give feedback after recording the independent attempt, then use a changed case to check again.

The complete concept may be taught/assessed orally with labels or drawings; a factual reading allowance does not supply the causal answer. It does not award the whole natural-water, reservoir-graph, Australian environment, environmental-action or fairness topics. It does not require an actual pupil to fetch water or a live community intervention to author the resource.
