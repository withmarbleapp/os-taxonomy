## Reproducible materials and conventions

Supply identical unit cubes; rectangular prisms, cones and cylinders; safe paper/precut shape pieces; square card grids with numbered rows/columns; counters and empty trays; neutral clue cards and the original tables below. An adult may make/read cards and provide access support without choosing the reasoning. Keep completed keys concealed for independent tasks. Teach with different examples, then change one relevant condition and require a new construction/explanation.

Grid rows increase from top to bottom and columns from left to right. For an n×n card a clockwise quarter-turn sends cell(r,c)to(c,n+1−r); a vertical mirror sends(r,c)to(r,n+1−c); a horizontal mirror sends(r,c)to(n+1−r,c). These are adult keys, not formal-coordinate prerequisites or universal page directions. Learners may predict by manipulating actual cards, then explain the result. Declare the turn centre/mirror line, initial facing and reading viewpoint rather than guessing them from appearance. Use marker/overlay or an adult-operated safe hole punch where a cut is required; no hazardous implement or body movement is a source gate.

A cube model is specified by occupied unit-cube positions(x,y,z), withxleft→right,yfront→back,zbottom→top. Front looks fromy<0, right-side fromx>allcubes, top fromabove. A top occupancy drawing records which grid positions are occupied, not stack height. A front/side silhouette can hide cubes behind others. These diagrams are declared model views, not photographs or field observations. Predict before revealing/turning; copying the model after every action is teaching, not independent prediction.

## Class III spatial parent: three families and integrated transfer

<a id="a3-views"></a>
1. **Different views and incomplete information.** The learner builds modelA from cubes at(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0):anLfootprint. The learner builds candidateB using just two cubes at(0,0,0),(1,0,0). From the declared front both show two adjacent squares. Ask whether that view alone determines which model is present; keyno. Ask for another useful view, then reveal the top, which distinguishes theLfrom the two-square line. Independently match a turned copy of the completeLobject to its original and reject the two-cube object. One matching visible face is insufficient to establish congruent whole objects.

<a id="a3-change"></a>
2. **Turn, flip and fold.** On a3×3card shade(1,1),(2,1),(2,2). Predict a clockwise quarter-turn:shaded cells(1,3),(1,2),(2,2). On a separate identical original card predict a vertical flip:(1,3),(2,3),(2,2). Compare the different results and check with actual manipulation. Separately fold a blank3×3paper left-to-right along its middle column and place a matched transferable mark at(1,1); it corresponds to(1,3)on unfolding. The adult supplies a safe way to demonstrate the correspondence. Changing only the fold tohorizontal corresponds(1,1)to(3,1). No claim that a rotation and reflection are identical.

<a id="a3-missing"></a>
3. **A hidden or absent part.** Construct a2row×3column rectangle from six equal square pieces. Conceal one corner position and remove its piece; pupil predicts the needed part from the whole outline/visible joins, selects a fitting piece from equal square/half-square triangle/different-sized square and explains why it completes the figure without gap or overlap. Change the missing position and give two fitting triangles instead of one square. The learner must actually compose the missing region; a guessed piece name is insufficient. Treat a shape pattern with insufficient visible constraints as unresolved rather than invent one unique hidden continuation.

<a id="a3-transfer"></a>
**Independent integrated transfer.** Supply a new3×3shape occupying(1,2),(2,2),(3,2),(3,3). Learner predicts and makes its clockwise quarter-turn:(2,3),(2,2),(2,1),(3,1), then restores a covered unit region using fitting pieces. For a separate cube object, choose a second view that distinguishes two supplied candidates. Explain which conclusion came from a view, actual matching or a stated whole-outline constraint. A pupil who can follow the teacher's turn but cannot predict the fresh arrangement has not completed the parent.

## Class IV spatial parent: four families and integrated transfer

<a id="a4-views"></a>
1. **Two silhouettes still leave a hidden distinction.** The learner builds both cube models, adds/removes the distinguishing cube, and uses the constructions to investigate the hidden-view question. An adult may scaffold a different teaching example but does not construct the assessment models for the learner. ModelA consists of(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1):four cubes. ModelB adds(1,1,0):five cubes. Both have front column heights[2,1]and side heights[2,1], but top occupancy isLversus full2×2square. Predict whether front+side alone distinguish them, explain why not and select the additional view that does. Then compare complete objects by turning/matching, not by equating one face with the whole. The source exercise does not certify the broader drawing-all-orthographic-views claim.

<a id="a4-fold-cut"></a>
2. **Cut/fold correspondence.** Fold a blank4×4card left-to-right along the centre between columns2/3. An adult makes one safe hole at the centre of folded position(1,4), through both layers. Predict the unfolded positions:(1,1)and(1,4). Check, then change to a top-to-bottom fold with the corresponding hole on the lower layer at(4,4); unfolded positions are(1,4),(4,4). A hole on the fold itself is a different case and is not silently treated as two separate holes. Mark the active fold and layer count before predicting.

<a id="a4-missing"></a>
3. **Hidden pieces with matching constraints.** An outlined2×2square is assembled from two equal rectangles, each1×2. Cover one rectangle while showing its shared join and the full outline. Select the missing rectangle from a turned1×2piece, a1×3piece and a unit square; rotate/overlay to justify the fit. Then remove the join clue and offer two triangular halves of the whole square:more than one decomposition can complete the same outline. Explain what extra information distinguishes the intended pieces; do not pretend an outline uniquely fixes its construction.

<a id="a4-symmetry"></a>
4. **Mirror halves.** On a4×4card supply left-half cells(1,1),(2,2),(3,1), with a vertical mirror line between columns2/3. Independently complete(1,4),(2,3),(3,4), explain equal perpendicular distances informally by folding/overlaying, and reject a plain translation of the left half. Change one left-half cell to(4,2); its partner is(4,3). The source needs the declared mirror relation, not every advanced grid-transformation operation.

<a id="a4-transfer"></a>
**Independent integrated transfer.** Start with a4×4Lmark(1,1),(1,2),(2,1). First turn clockwise, then reflect vertically. After the turn:(1,4),(2,4),(1,3); after the reflection:(1,1),(2,1),(1,2). Reverse the order and predict before checking:reflection gives(1,4),(1,3),(2,4), then clockwise gives(4,4),(3,4),(4,3). Explain why the changed order can change the final figure. In a separate covered-piece case state which visible constraint permits a unique answer and which leaves alternatives.

## Class V spatial parent: four families and integrated transfer

<a id="a5-views"></a>
1. **Multi-layer hidden information.** The learner builds a3×3base with stack height2at every border position, then builds and alters both models to investigate their hidden views. Compare modelA whose central height is1with modelB whose central height is2. A has17cubes andB18. Their top occupancy grids are both full3×3;both front and side silhouettes show heights[2,2,2]. Ask whether those views determine the hidden centre:they do not. Supply an additional actual clue that the centre has only its base cube, then identifyAandexplain17=9base+8borderupper cubes. Remove that clue and retain both possibilities. Do not count an unseen cube twice or infer every occupied top cell has the same height.

<a id="a5-ordered-changes"></a>
2. **Direction and order.** On a5×5card shade(1,2),(2,2),(2,3). Predict clockwise quarter-turn:(2,5),(2,4),(3,4). Then reflect horizontally:(4,5),(4,4),(3,4). Reverse the operations:horizontal reflection first gives(5,2),(4,2),(4,3), then clockwise gives(2,1),(2,2),(3,2). Explain the difference and repeat with an anti-clockwise turn. Anti-clockwise of the original is(4,1),(4,2),(3,2). The adult checks using the actual card rather than turning a spoken direction into an unstated answer.

<a id="a5-hidden-folds"></a>
3. **Two folds and a hidden repeat.** Fold a blank4×4paper left-to-right, then top-to-bottom. An adult safely makes one hole at the centre of folded cell(4,4), through all four layers. Predict four unfolded centres:(1,1),(1,4),(4,1),(4,4). Explain which mirror correspondence each fold contributes. Change the hole to(3,4)on the folded lower-right quarter; unfolding gives(2,1),(2,4),(3,1),(3,4). No cut on a crease is assumed. Provide paper layer access/adaptations without supplying the prediction.

<a id="a5-water-mirror"></a>
4. **Two declared mirror directions.** A4×4card has cells(1,1),(1,2),(2,1). A vertical mirror produces(1,4),(1,3),(2,4); a horizontal water-line-style mirror produces(4,1),(4,2),(3,1). Both are reflections across the stated line, not arbitrary rotations or an assertion about optical depth. Complete missing mirrored parts and explain how equal distance from the actual line is preserved. Change the line/dot position, and require a new construction rather than memorisedleft/right reversal.

<a id="a5-transfer"></a>
**Independent integrated transfer.** Give a new5×5card(1,1),(2,1),(2,2),(3,2), two ordered actions“half-turn then vertical mirror”, and one occluded unit region whose whole outline is known. Predict half-turn:(5,5),(4,5),(4,4),(3,4), then vertical mirror:(5,1),(4,1),(4,2),(3,2); construct a fitting missing region. Separately explain the17/18cube ambiguity from a fresh declared centre clue, explicitly separating what the views establish from what the added clue supplies. A single correct mirror item cannot replace the full parent.
