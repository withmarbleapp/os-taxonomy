## Original fact cards with actual source boundaries

**Anatomy.** Roots absorb water/minerals and anchor; stems support and transport; leaves use light in food-making; flowers support reproduction. In a studied complete flower, stamens produce pollen and the pistil contains ovules; after fertilisation, ovules can become seeds and the ovary can become fruit. Fruit encloses/protects seeds and may aid their dispersal; seeds contain a developing plant and reserves, enabling a new plant under suitable conditions. In a studied pine, seed cones bear seeds on scales; opening can release them. A cone is not itself one seed or a flower with petals. Use AMNH *Parts of a Flower*, actual definitions222–226, https://www.amnh.org/learn-teach/curriculum-collections/biodiversity-counts/plant-identification/plant-morphology/parts-of-a-flower ; and USDA ForestService *Pinus*, selected reproductive/seed passages PDF13–14, https://www.fs.usda.gov/nsl/Wpsm/Pinus.pdf . No claim that every flower contains every part, all cones open immediately, every scale bears viable seed or every fruit is fleshy. No advanced internal-anatomy inventory is a pupil gate.

**Sunlight and gases.** For the green plants studied, photosynthesis uses light energy, water and carbon dioxide to make sugars/food; oxygen is released. NASA JPL's actual2012 *Watching Earth Breathe* transcript distinguishes this from plant respiration, which releases carbon dioxide. https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/videos/watching-earth-breathe-the-seasonal-vegetation-cycle-and-carbon-dioxide/ . Most plants get energy directly from the Sun this way; do not change “most” to “all”, claim soil supplies ready-made food, equate energy with carbon-dioxide matter, or say plants never use oxygen. MyNASAData’s actual fast-carbon-cycle passages170–175 explicitly connect carbon dioxide and water with sugar/oxygen production, https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/basic-page/energy-and-matter-cycles ; use that bounded process, not its entire nitrogen/carbon curriculum or formula. This account does not require the whole older Photosynthesis topic's word equation, chlorophyll mechanism or mineral-ion detail.

**First Nation food and shelter.** Onondaga Nation's own *Food* account identifies corn, beans and squash as cultivated foods and describes gathered seasonal plants/berries, gratitude and taking only what is needed. Its *Homes* account describes historical elm-wood/bark longhouses, distinguishes present varied homes and continuing ceremonial/government uses of longhouses. https://www.onondaganation.org/culture/food/ and https://www.onondaganation.org/culture/homes/ , actual food main body and homes historical/present sections. Attribute this to Onondaga/Haudenosaunee, not every Indigenous community; present-day people are not frozen into a historical dwelling. No historical date or nutrition claim is added.

## Reproducible original anatomy and process materials

Make two paper panels from these instructions; these are explanatory schematics, not photographs of a pupil's specimens. PanelF: draw a plant with branching roots below a soil line, upright stem, two leaves and a flower at its tip. Beside it draw an enlarged complete-flower cutaway: petals around two stalks ending in pollen-bearing anthers (stamens); centrally a stigma/style leading down to an ovary with two ovules (pistil). Draw a fruit section with seeds beside this, connected by a “possible development after fertilisation” arrow. PanelP: draw a pine branch with needle leaves and a seed cone; inset one scale with seeds on its upper surface and a separate winged seed. Do not draw pine fruit or infer every plant is a flowering plant. Keep a labelled teacher copy and a separate blank learner copy.

Supply nine original removable part/function cards: root—water/mineral uptake/anchorage; stem—support/transport; leaf—light/food-making; flower—reproduction; stamen—pollen; pistil—ovules/seed-and-fruit development; seed—new plant/reserves; cone—seed-bearing/protection/release in studied pine; fruit—encloses seeds/protection or dispersal. In independent tasks remove the completed pairings, require placement and explanation, and use a changed orientation/example. Reproduction supports continuation of plant populations; do not claim a flower keeps one adult alive in exactly the way roots provide its water.

For gases, draw a leaf with three separate arrows into it: sunlight (energy), water and carbon dioxide (materials); draw sugar/food inside the plant and an oxygen arrow outward. Leave labels blank for assessment. Light is not a gas/material ingredient; carbon dioxide is not oxygen. A separate teacher note states that living plants also respire. This is a qualitative model, not a chemical-equation or gas-measurement experiment.
