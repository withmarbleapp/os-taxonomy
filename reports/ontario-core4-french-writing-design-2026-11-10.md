# Ontario Core French Grade 4: writing

Eight D1/D2 records receive source-fit designs through 18 scoped component associations: seven shared topics and one attributed community claim for retained French written forms. Six exact Ontario ages 9–10 contexts are added and two extended. The [complete design](ontario-core4-french-writing-design-2026-11-10.json) contains original French models, source-code bindings, performance conditions and reuse comparisons.

Learners create varied French messages for real stated purposes and audiences. The design includes all four pre-writing modes, model-based drafts, genuine content revision, a few concrete French conventions, useful presentation, digital writing and reading their own work aloud. Before/during/after writing reflection involves teacher and peer, followed by monitored improvement. Copying a final model or demonstrating an English writing skill alone does not establish these French performances.

The new written-form claim adapts `mt_sPd4fWeY9x` from [dahbiahmed’s community PR #23](https://github.com/withmarbleapp/os-taxonomy/pull/23) at `749ae042d5186d8e3ab96c01d0a63096f0229925`. It checks taught French spellings and accents retained without a visible written model and reused meaningfully. Oral vocabulary, sight-word recognition and reference-assisted editing do not establish this knowledge. Homophones require meaning context; no speed norm is imposed. Its new original guide is low confidence and untested. No France catalogue, ages, associations, edges or approximate centrality are imported.

Two broad writing-craft vocabulary prerequisites change from hard to soft. Choosing appropriate form/tone and producing/reviewing coherent audience-matched writing do not require every rhetoric, voice and cohesion term in the broader vocabulary topic. The complete target choices, explanations, development and review remain required. All other edges and every existing authored topic, guide and context interval remain intact.

This depends on the prior reading proposal and brings the proposed Core Grade 4 design to 32 of 44 records. Twelve cultural/intercultural records and the other primary French grades/programmes remain in scope. Full denotes source-fit authoring design, not observed learner attainment or validated teaching efficacy. The delayed retrieval check belongs to the selected written-form claim; it is not a universal Ontario delay or word-count requirement. No staging publication or learner-state change is included.

Validation: generation, dataset validation, generated-artifact check and all 118 tests pass. An independent baseline comparison preserves all 1,861 existing authored topics, 1,049 guides, old context intervals and all edges except the two registered corrections. Regression checks bind the eight sources to exact scoped associations, actual French production, four writing modes, convention/presentation work, teacher-and-peer reflection and the attributed written-form claim.
