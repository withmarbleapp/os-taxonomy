# Ontario Core French Grades 5–6: shared language operations

This checkpoint adds [source-fit designs for 64 expectations](ontario-core56-french-shared-design-2026-11-10.json), 32 per grade. All 26 contributing topics already exist. The proposal adds 182 component pairs in 52 exact Ontario ages 10–11 and 11–12 contexts, with no new topic, guide, authored claim change or prerequisite edit.

The actual [2013 official curriculum](https://www.dcp.edu.gov.on.ca/en/curriculum/elementary-fsl) clauses and examples were read for both courses. Delivery codenames contain misleading copied grade names; actual course identities and complete selected code sets were checked. These are language-operation expectations outside the four culture strands. The 24 culture records require separate Quebec and eastern/western/northern Canadian community evidence and receive no association in this change.

The earlier whole [oral](ontario-core4-french-oral-design-2026-11-10.md), [interaction](ontario-core4-french-interaction-design-2026-11-10.md), [text-form](ontario-core4-french-textforms-design-2026-11-10.md), [reading](ontario-core4-french-reading-design-2026-11-10.md) and [writing](ontario-core4-french-writing-design-2026-11-10.md) tasks remain required. Each row binds its exact earlier report, protocol IDs and whole contributing claims, plus the actual grade-specific adaptations. Existing phonological, vocabulary, connected-text, picture/text, teacher/peer reflection, planning, feedback, process-language and goal-setting duties stay intact. No prior English result or different French corpus certifies these tasks automatically.

The grade comparison identifies real changed conditions rather than inventing levels:

- Grade 5 explicitly retains self/family/friends/environment topics and interactions with both teacher and peers. The existing complete tasks already account for those duties.
- Grade 6 listening actually includes familiar **and new** French words. An original workshop instruction provides contextual explanations and visual/realia support. Novelty is checked for the actual learner; already familiar words cannot be relabelled as new, and meaning is checked from heard French.
- Grade 6 guided interaction uses actual open partner replies, modelling and teacher/peer support. A complete predetermined dialogue is insufficient. Listening reflection discusses factors that obstruct understanding and possible strategies without fabricating a learner failure.
- Grade 6 reading revisits recently learned words in changed connected text. Writing uses several actual French conventions, including a meaningful location-preposition revision, alongside the complete existing writing process. The source's illustrative grammar, products and styles do not become an exhaustive compulsory inventory.

All source-specific supports remain available. Actual French input/output and whole prior operations matter; older age labels, reference IDs and link counts alone establish neither coverage nor attainment. Historical contexts are preserved, and generated compatibility envelopes derive only from real added contexts. A grade boundary does not create a new mastery claim or difficulty level.

Combined with the earlier 44-record Core Grade 4 proposal, these designs account for 108 of the 594 retained primary FSL records at this checkpoint. The other 486 remain in scope: 24 Core Grades 5–6 culture records and 462 Extended/Immersion records. All dispositions concern authoring designs; repository acceptance, classroom use, learner evidence and staging publication are separate.
