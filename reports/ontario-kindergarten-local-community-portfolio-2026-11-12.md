## Configuration and actually supplied source facts

This is an explicit Toronto city-community example, used as local only for a school/programme whose actual community basis permits that region. Otherwise it remains a comparison and a qualified educator substitutes actual local places, people/groups, functions and suitable access. No pupil address, family history or external contact is needed. A city-wide example is not assumed to be every Toronto pupil's familiar neighbourhood or a visit they made.

Fact card A — **Love Park**, supplied source description and separately attributed image. Waterfront Toronto's project history and dated 23 June 2023 opening account document an off-ramp site changed to public park space, with retained trees, planted areas, paths and a constructed pond. The privately viewed linked aerial shows those visible features at one moment. It does not establish visitor trends, ecological improvement, an earlier state or a fieldtrip. Use only a permitted actual source view or an adult's faithful original feature drawing labelled as a representation; do not imply the unseen detail was observed. The park as a whole is managed, trees are living/natural components and paths/constructed pond are human-made features. A park is not wholly untouched just because it has plants.

Fact card B — **High Park natural areas**. The City of Toronto's page (modified 6 October 2026, read 12 November 2026) identifies remnant Black Oak savannah, woodland and Grenadier Pond, which supports plants and animals. It also identifies cultivated gardens, constructed paths and buildings. Adult vocabulary: a woodland has trees, a pond is a body of water, and people manage the park around these features. Do not call every park feature human-made or all of High Park untouched. The City asks visitors to use formal trails to protect sensitive natural areas, and to put litter in suitable receptacles or take it home. The selected child's care task does not include prescribed burning, wildlife handling, pond access or a cleanliness survey.

Fact card C — **High Park Nature Centre**, a real community group and place. The City's same account identifies a non-profit organisation supported by the City, located in the former High Park Forest School, offering nature walks, workshops and camps. Two supplied facts are its actual setting and its educational activities. Explain its social/environmental contribution: people can learn about the park and its living things together. This is not a claim that the pupil attended a session, that a specific member said something, that every programme is currently available or that a particular person founded it. The complete Important Local People and Places assessment still separately requires a documented individual/group contribution and why it matters, not merely naming an occupation.

Fact card D — **Fort York library**, a distinct built place and staff group. The current Toronto Public Library location page identifies 190 Fort York Boulevard and staff available to answer branch questions. The library's actual Using the Library body explains borrowing physical/digital materials and branch assistance. Teacher says: people use a library to obtain information and books; staff help people use the service. A real borrowing event or card application is not required by this source duty and is not recorded as performed. Do not infer a building's function only from its silhouette or transport policy, hours, event listings or a person's appearance. These are supplied public facts, not a pupil interview.

Sources: https://www.waterfrontoronto.ca/our-projects/love-park ; https://www.waterfrontoronto.ca/news/love-park-officially-opens-on-torontos-waterfront ; https://www.toronto.ca/explore-enjoy/parks-recreation/places-spaces/beaches-gardens-attractions/high-park/ ; https://tpl.ca/locations/fo/ ; https://tpl.ca/using-the-library/ . Relevant actual bodies were read; High Park's current operational closure/fees/programme schedules are not adopted as lasting facts. No new High Park or library photograph is claimed as inspected.

## Reproducible original teaching materials

Make four large cards with the place/group names and the bounded facts above, in original plain language; an adult may read them neutrally. Prepare a feature mat with separate headings **natural feature**, **managed place**, **human-made feature**, and a blank two-column **same/different** mat. Draw original clearly labelled representative symbols for a tree, a pond, a planted bed, a path and a library; these are generic teaching symbols, not documentary depictions of a site. Keep real source images separate and attributed.

For a care-action demonstration provide two clearly labelled classroom receptacles, clean reusable paper and an adult-confirmed local sorting rule, or use an actual classroom plant with its verified care instruction. Never introduce litter to the environment, ask children to handle unknown waste or assume that every paper/product belongs in one universal bin. The care action is actually performed only during delivery; planning it does not supply an observation.

## D22.1: actual community people and their work

Teach card C with the nature-learning function, then use card D for an independent contrast. With source facts available but no completed answer, pupil identifies the real group and describes its actual work: Nature Centre helps people learn through nature programmes; library staff help people use information/borrowing services. Ask which supplied fact supports the answer. A costume-based guess or a claim that a library worker planted the park fails the work/function distinction.

Change the circumstance: a visitor needs to find a book rather than learn about a woodland. Pupil selects the relevant group/place and explains why; ask what more they would need to know before asserting that today's particular event is available. Use an actual substituted local group where the Toronto example is nonlocal. The Social Roles whole claim additionally retains one person's two settings, changed responsibility and independent transfer; source recognition alone does not award it.

## D22.2: actual natural and human-made places and functions

Using A/B/D, ask pupil to recognise the studied places/features and explain a function: a pond is living-space/water for organisms, paths let people move through a park, library provides access to books/information. Sort natural/managed/built with reasons; explicitly ask why planted/maintained parkland is managed although trees are living. The source requires natural and human-made, while the whole contributing claim also retains the managed distinction, actual location/map language and the complete land/water model repertoire separately.

Fresh countercase: a drawing shows a building with no name or source facts. Ask whether its roof shape proves it is a library; key no. Compare an actual attributed library fact with an unsupported silhouette. Changing a sign or a pond's appearance cannot automatically establish a changed service or safe drinking water.

## D23.2: change, prediction and reason

Original supplied hypothetical: many visitors leave the formal path and repeatedly walk over one selected planted patch. Ask what might change and why. Accept a reasoned possibility that plants could be damaged/ground disturbed; no claim that this was observed at either park or that a given number of plants died. Contrast a path sign with actually remaining on the path: a sign alone does not prove visitor action. A fresh case removes a classroom plant's verified water supply; learner predicts a supported consequence, distinguishes it from an observed result and says what observation would check it. Keep complete animal and plant needs and the supplied-condition limitation; never perform harmful deprivation as the assessment.

## D23.3: describe and practise a suitable care action

Use a changed local rule/material or another suitable plant instruction and ask the pupil to adapt, not repeat “always put everything in the blue bin”. A feasible classroom practice supports the task without inventing an outdoor visit; actual local action remains explicit.

## Complete whole-path support appendix

The thirteen cards/evidence/assessment prompts, all eleven hard edges and all eight saved learn/assess payloads have been read (Shapes of Land and Water unchanged from the complete earlier French path review). Five cards have no saved guide; their whole evidence remains active. No existing guide confidence or source-verification flag is upgraded.

Keep these whole checks separate from source-only task credit. They are actual reproducible task designs with delivery-material preflight, not a claim that a pupil already performed them:

1. **Question words and questions about the past:** provide an actually attributed old public photo/object record selected by the educator, recording publisher/date/type rather than using a family photo or asserting a new drawing is old. Pupil produces who/what/where/when/why/how questions, three childhood/past-life questions addressed to a supplied historical-person case rather than requiring contact with a grandparent, a question about the old object/photo and a past/present-place question. An adult may read facts but cannot supply the pupil's questions. A changed old record tests fresh question formation. The child's personal family history is unnecessary.
2. **Important local people/places and Special Places:** record two supported place facts and explain its social/cultural/spiritual importance as supported, without requiring all three categories or imposing a personal belief. Describe a documented real group contribution and why it matters; Nature Centre's work supports the educational contribution, not a fictional named founder. For Special Places independently draw an actual familiar place the pupil belongs to, label two features, explain its actual connection to self or another person and suggest care. A Toronto source picture cannot invent that connection. An ordinary classroom/school example permits participation without private identity disclosure.
3. **Land/water and managed-feature mapping:** retain at least four landforms and three water-body types, independently produce/interpret a model with their distinct shapes, then identify natural/managed/built local features and describe their actual locations on a simple educator-verified plan. Original reproducible model: paper/base with a raised mountain, lower rounded hill, level plain, low valley, narrow river, bounded lake and wide ocean edge, each visibly distinct and labelled only after the pupil identifies them; explain a changed feature/location. It is a model, not the geography of Love Park or High Park. Full local-feature evidence includes a real accessible observation opportunity where the existing assessment expects it; supplied photos do not become a walk.
4. **Actual local plants/animals and microhabitats:** educator selects an authorised accessible actual local garden/school-ground habitat, a suitable picture identification key checked for what is genuinely present, and two safely observable microhabitats. Prepare a blank record with date/place, organism name, plant/animal, observed location/conditions and observed/not-found columns. Pupil predicts before looking, searches/observes with appropriate supervision, names the actual variety (at least three real plants and animals across the observed sample), records conditions, compares findings and explains the smaller place inside the larger habitat. No guaranteed woodlice/worm count, handling unknown organisms, destructive search or pond dipping is required by this portfolio; preserve respectful practice and record actual access adaptations. A manufactured picture scene is practice, not firsthand searching. If actual records are absent, that whole-claim performance remains unverified.
5. **Habitat vocabulary and needs relationships:** with original labelled sketches of woodland, desert and pond plus a small patch beneath a woodland log, pupil uses habitat/environment/microhabitat/conditions/shelter in a running description, distinguishes scale and shelter, explains three contrasting habitats and transfers to a fresh case. Supply bounded case facts (frog needs water/damp cover/insect food; bee takes flower food and can pollinate studied flowers; some plants use other pollination routes). Pupil matches every need to a feature, explains a plant as well as an animal case, designs a habitat for a new supplied organism and traces organism dependence without treating soil as a living partner or moving an actual organism to an unsuitable place. Supplied cases are labelled models and do not assert those organisms were observed in the selected parks.
6. **Living/dead/never alive and life-process language:** provide a real potted plant, clean wooden spoon, fallen leaf of known plant origin and pebble, plus clearly declared pictures of a moving toy/car and a healthy seed. Pupil justifies a three-way sort from life processes/material origin and transfers to another item, rejecting movement alone. Retain at least five life-process terms used in context and at least two plant examples, not a recited list. Use observed growth/response records or clearly identified time-compressed representations where needed; do not claim the pupil observed several days from one illustration. Getting rid of waste is explained plainly without an unsupported single-photo criterion or the assertion that plants simply excrete oxygen.
7. **Whole plant/animal needs and Social Roles:** independently distinguish animal water/food/air/shelter from plant water/light/air/suitable-temperature needs, explain a missing need and an additional supplied animal case. The unchanged whole needs guide includes a real multi-day seedling comparison as well as a compressed care model; supplied facts or a single prediction do not certify that actual observation. Keep humane suitable care, any actual investigation's conditions and observed timeline explicit, with no animal deprivation. The source D23.2 prediction can be assessed separately. Social Roles retains the same person across two settings, the person–relationship–duty connection, an actual non-sensitive self-application where required and a changed circumstance with independent rationale; copying the adult's chosen care action does not supply this explanation.

Whole performance can remain unobserved while a complete source-authoring contract exists.
