# Creative writing: three framework records and optional graph candidates

This is an original candidate task portfolio for Preparatory R1 C-3.3, R2 C-3.3 and R2 CG-3. It proposes source authoring with the same actual-role configuration contract as the four-language remedy report. It does not activate any withheld association, expand ages, certify a named programme or promote another parent. The literal source requirements remain privately bound to the pinned NCERT framework.

The three source duties are not a requirement to use a digital editor or publish publicly. Optional Making multimodal texts and Narrative Writing candidates retain their whole claims. Complete source authoring and publication of a graph association are separate decisions; a withheld optional candidate need not make a concrete source task unimplementable.

## Complete original study material

For an English illustration only, read this original model with the pupil:

“On the windy classroom balcony, Asha held the corner of a large paper map. She wanted to show every visitor where the reading tables stood. Her friend Dev was arranging the chairs. A gust lifted the map from the table.

‘Catch that corner!’ Asha called. Dev held it down with a book. ‘A book hides the path,’ he said. Asha frowned, then folded a small paper tab around the edge. Together they attached another tab on the opposite side. The map stayed flat, and its paths were visible. When the visitors arrived, Asha stepped back so they could find the tables themselves.”

Teach the setting, characters, problem, ordered attempts and resolution using the model. Compare “They fixed it” with the actual action and its effect. Ask how Dev's line reveals the reason for trying a different solution. Do not claim that one reading proves independent narrative writing.

Original poem model:

“Paper corners catch the air,
Little maps go everywhere.
Fold a tab and hold it tight,
Paths stay clear and words in sight.”

In this English example, air/everywhere and tight/sight form rhyme pairs. Clap or speak the rhythmic phrasing without prescribing a universal metre. Compare the sound pattern with a plain information sentence. A learner's new poem may use repetition, alliteration or another appropriate pattern rather than this English rhyme scheme. Writing an original poem, recognising a pattern and reciting from memory are separate evidence channels.

For conversation as its own form, contrast these two short exchanges: “Hello.” “Hello.” and “Where should this map go?” “Beside the door, where visitors can see it.” “That space is narrow. Could we use the table?” The second exchange develops a purpose and a response; labels and greetings alone do not demonstrate the same operation.

For poster/invitation study use the complete fictional book-swap and story-morning examples and audience/fact/revision checks in the role-configuration contract. Those are models for writing choices, not texts to copy as independent work.

## Independent source portfolio and exact keys

**R1 C-3.3:** Produce all five forms in actual R1: poster, invitation, original simple poem, story and dialogue. Use the complete independent writing prompts and changed cases in the role contract for the first two. For a fresh story, give this brief: “Two fictional children must make a shared display visible, but their first plan leaves one part hidden. Invent what they do and say.” Require the pupil's own text; the map model's names, tabs and solution cannot simply be copied. Source evidence is appropriate information/purpose in all five forms; one poem cannot stand for the portfolio. For the separate dialogue, write an exchange between a visitor who cannot find something and a host who changes an explanation after misunderstanding. Both participants must respond meaningfully to each other.

**R2 C-3.3:** Separately author an actual-R2 story, poem and conversation. A product already assessed in R1 is not transferred automatically. Use a different original brief: “A fictional group prepares a quiet place to share a story, but the first place becomes unavailable.” For the poem, choose an ordinary feature of the pupil's actual making activity—paper, sounds, shapes or movement—and create new wording with an intentional appropriate pattern. For conversation, have two fictional speakers explain and resolve a different misunderstanding about the group's plan. An adult can demonstrate a different case during teaching but cannot supply the independent lines.

**R2 CG-3:** Compose a portfolio that explicitly demonstrates writing to express understanding, experience, feelings and ideas, then integrate these purposes in a new piece. For understanding, explain the pupil's own paper-display arrangement with a named topic and relevant accurate details. For experience, describe the actual classroom making activity they just undertook; do not fabricate a personal history. For feelings, write a fictional character's response to a changed plan, using the character's words/actions without requiring the pupil to report a private feeling or a positive mood. For ideas, propose and explain another display arrangement. Ask the pupil to write a fresh piece connecting at least two purposes and explain the choices. Check all four channels independently before a whole-goal authoring judgment; the goal is not certified by counting child links.

For every independent product, a competent configured-language reader checks actual script, meaning, audience and purpose. The reader identifies one genuine ambiguity or mismatch; the pupil revises it and explains why the revision helps. Changed case: the audience is younger, the display location changes, and a required material is unavailable. The pupil adapts information, sequence and dialogue rather than copying earlier lines. Retain both drafts and the explanation when assessing a learner; this curation work records no learner drafts.

Near misses are adult copying, replacing names in the supplied model, a list of speaker labels without response, a poster without essential event facts, poem recitation substituted for original writing, or one role's product silently credited to the other. Diagnose the missing operation and teach with another case before reassessing independently.

## Unchanged whole-claim and prerequisite support

The current candidate hard closure contains ten cards and no saved teaching-research guides. These original supports cover its operations; source-only success remains separate from whole-card success.

**Writing Poetry / Rhyming words:** In the configured language, compare actual rhyming and non-rhyming word endings, explain the sound relation, generate a new matching example and transfer to another checked set. In the English illustration use cat/hat versus cat/cup, then light/night versus light/leaf. A repeated letter is not sufficient. During shared reading of the original model, pause before an expected rhyme and invite the pupil to join in with the predictable line or pattern; then use a fresh checked rhyme for transfer. Recognition/generation without this shared participation does not exhaust the Rhyming words card. Identify rhythm or another intentional pattern in the model, compose a new verse, learn an appropriate poem and recite it from memory with expression. Keep the complete written composition, recognition and recitation requirements separately visible.

**Simple Stories / Narrative Writing:** Teach a beginning that establishes who/where, ordered events, a problem, a meaningful response and an ending using the original map story. Independently create the fresh story above. For the complete simple-story claim, write at least three sequenced sentences with a beginning, middle and ending or reaction; use appropriate actual-language temporal expressions to make the order clear. For the whole developed narrative, require a described setting, developed character, clear problem/resolution, dialogue revealing feelings or personality, descriptive detail and temporal organisation with a satisfying ending. A short source story may succeed without demonstrating every developed-narrative clause. After reader feedback revise a misleading detail, then create a new story for a changed audience/setting. Do not treat speaker labels or an event list as complete dialogue/narrative.

**Building sentences / Oral rehearsal / Writing to inform:** Preserve the exact complete subject/verb, fragment distinction, expansion, repeated oral rehearsal, partner practice, independent writing, named topic, relevant facts and informative diagram-label checks in the role contract. Verify actual-language compatibility; no English construction is silently universalised. A successful source poster is not evidence of every one of these whole claims.

**Basic Informational Writing / Paragraphs:** Supply an original declared classroom display plan with two sections: arrangement (map on a table; paths visible) and visitor access (clear entrance; labels at readable positions). The learner names the topic, chooses relevant facts, groups related material, uses an appropriate heading/subheading and identifies where a paragraph break helps. Change the plan to a wall display with a different entrance and ask for a fresh organised explanation. Accurate isolated facts without organisation do not satisfy the whole paragraph claim. For the complete basic-information claim, introduce the topic, define a relevant term (for example, an entrance is the place visitors come in), develop points with facts and concrete details, and write a concluding statement that wraps up the arrangement and access explanation. Check introduction, development and conclusion independently; a heading alone is not a conclusion. Retain the complete current-card criteria rather than assuming one example exhausts them.

**Making multimodal texts:** The optional whole claim requires more than a source poster. Independently create an informative paragraph with labelled diagram and caption; choose images/font sizes/layout in an actual suitable digital tool for a persuasive purpose and explain those choices; publish a picture-book page whose illustration adds meaning beyond its text, then edit both after feedback. A local file, print or classroom copy can be the publication; no public upload is needed. A hand-drawn source poster does not demonstrate the actual digital-tool criterion. This candidate remains withheld from new source associations until the complete actual-role operations and material mechanics are reviewed. That restraint does not impose digital production as an extra source requirement.
