### Exact complete teaching materials and script

Use one adult-selected cool light directed onto a ball/globe with a marked point north of the equator; keep light fixed and avoid direct viewing. Use a removable tilted stick as axis and a distant wall arrow so its direction stays parallel at each orbital position. Model distances/sizes are explicitly not to scale. Add cards `one spin: about24hours`, `one orbit: about365days`, `north`, `south`, `summer`, `winter`. These are studied labels, not required arithmetic.

Whole prerequisite lesson: “The Sun is a star. It looks much bigger and brighter than the other stars because it is much closer to us. In our solar system the planets travel around the Sun.” Show one near and one farther identical symbolic star card to illustrate apparent size, explicitly a comparison model, then require an independent explanation. The complete Sun-star claim does not require listing all eight planet names, but its description's eight planets are retained in the model caption. Ask learner to describe Sun-centred planetary paths independently; do not award it merely for a memorised label.

Daily model: rotate only the ball at one orbital position. The learner predicts then observes the mark pass from light to shadow and back, points out simultaneous light on the other side and explains the fixed light did not turn off. Repeat with a different mark and changed starting orientation. Then walk one orbit, distinguishing the two movements and their different studied timescales. Link ground apparent sunrise/set to the mark's turning; acknowledge that a moving-light model can mimic the sky view but does not establish the real scientific cause. Require all existing Spin&Orbit full assessment duties, not only the three abbreviated card lines.

Seasonal model: maintain the same tilt direction while moving the ball to the opposite side of the light. Compare the selected northern mark's longer illuminated rotation arc and more directly arriving light with its shorter/slanted counterpart at the opposite position. Use a separate torch/paper comparison at fixed torch-to-centre distance to see a slanted patch spread wider; do not pretend a small nearby lamp reproduces solar parallel rays precisely. The learner observes actual footprints, points to concentrated versus spread light and connects that to stronger/weaker warming over the seasonal pattern. Numerical square totals, angle degrees and a temperature measurement are not gates for this source-only protocol. They remain requirements/operations of the complete older guide if awarding that topic.

State: northern/southern seasonal patterns contrast at the same time; this is not the whole Earth moving near enough to make everyone summer together. Tilt changes the sunlight angle/daylight exposure, not merely which hemisphere is a little nearer. Weather on a particular day can vary; this model explains a repeating seasonal pattern, not every daily forecast. Keep warm weather separate from the cause of day length.

### Independent source assessment and key

Put away completed labels/arrows/explanations. Leave neutral studied number cards available if needed, while recording whether names/timescales are independently recalled for the whole existing card. Give changed start orientations and a second marker.

1. Learner manipulates daily spin, predicts and then explains light/shadow for the mark, simultaneous day elsewhere and apparent solar motion. Differentiates the full orbit/year; independently states the studied24/365 time relations for the whole existing topic.
2. Learner sets the tilted model at two opposite orbital positions, keeping the axis direction fixed. Explains which selected hemisphere receives more direct light and a longer lit part of the spin, then what changes at the opposite position. Points to opposite seasonal conditions north/south and rejects global-distance-only cause.
3. Fresh mark/position: predict before manipulating; construct the explanation rather than repeat the teacher's spoken path. Explain one daily change and one seasonal change as effects of the distinct Earth–Sun relations. A list of clothes or four seasonal names is insufficient.
4. State model limitations: not scale, nearby light approximation, not a particular weather forecast or a real measured pupil observation. For the separate observational daylight topic, actual time-separated observations are still required.
