## Resources and preflight

Use one real laptop/tablet, its actual available input/output components, and a locally usable educational block editor. Scratch is one optional implementation, not a national platform requirement. Use the official [getting-started resource](https://www.scratchfoundation.org/learn/learning-library/getting-started) and its captured guide, pages3–4 and7–8, for editor components, event-led sequences and changing block order. Official [local app help](https://mitscratch.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/4000158825-i-m-having-trouble-saving-from-the-scratch-app-) and [project export help](https://mitscratch.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/4000228128-can-i-use-my-student-account-after-my-class-has-ended-) inform save/load mechanics; they do not require an account or a class-account migration. The [Scratch community guide](https://resources.scratch.mit.edu/www/guides/en/scratch-community-guide.pdf), page9, supports privacy and respectful conduct. Official resource capture hashes and visual-readback notes are retained privately. These are published-resource checks, not proof of actual execution.

Before using this kit, the adult must verify on the actual device: editor opens; needed blocks can be assembled and run; the selected sprite visibly moves, speaks and changes costume; local export creates a recoverable file; the intended folder can be created and navigated; a fresh editor can reopen the saved project; a second version can be distinguished. Record the actual application/version, device/input access, save/load actions and result. Avoid assuming that double-clicking a project launches the right application. Keep the original version and practice folder distinct from personal files. A file icon or download notification does not prove recovery. The current automation inventory has no browser surfaces, so this preflight has **not** been performed here.

Offer accessible input and a familiar language for explanation. Physical peripheral cards can supplement unavailable hardware when identifying its function, but cannot replace actual app, file or program procedures. An adult may model a worked example and assist navigation during teaching; mark that separately from the independent assessment. No account creation, public posting, real private details or contact with another person is necessary.

## 1. Hardware, software and purposeful use

Target `mt_SZrMv3YAky` requires both distinguishing touchable components from the app/program and using an app for a simple purpose. Teach with the actual device: point to a screen, button or camera, then identify the application as the instructions/program rather than the glass display. Model one small learning purpose, such as arranging drawn shapes into equal groups and checking the total. Have the learner operate a second example with adult guidance, as this whole claim permits, and explain what the device/app helped them do.

If they call the screen “the software”, contrast the same hardware displaying two different apps. If they can name a brand but cannot use it for the purpose, return to the visible action and its outcome. Recognition cards alone are insufficient. For independent evidence, ask for two actual hardware parts, one actual software application, use of a chosen app function, and a meaningful explanation. Transfer to another function while the device remains the same. Allow guided app use where the claim permits it; do not imply independent programming from this introductory evidence.

## 2. Components, peripherals and information direction

Target `mt_o0vTMl8vj5` includes screen, keyboard, mouse/trackpad, touchscreen, speaker, microphone, camera, printer, scanner, headphones and storage devices. Teach labels on the actual chosen device, supplemented by original name/picture cards for absent peripherals. For each item, ask what information enters, leaves or is retained. Contrast microphone with speaker and camera with display; a touchscreen can both show output and receive touch input. Storage retains work and should not be forced into a single input-only/output-only category.

Have the learner sort the components by function and explain one ambiguous or combined case. Then choose components for a purpose, first listening to a recorded explanation, then making a recording or digitising a paper picture. A memorised list without a functional reason is insufficient. If “input” is understood as electrical power, trace a spoken word into the microphone and the sound coming out of the speaker to distinguish information from power. Transfer by changing the purpose. Full whole-topic evidence includes the wider peripheral repertoire, not just the subset visibly present on one laptop; actual operation of every named peripheral is not invented as an additional source clause.

## 3. Files, folders, conventions and recovery

Target `mt_XeT37rd69f` requires saving, meaningful names, agreed conventions, folders, moving related files, recovery and version distinction. Agree a nonpersonal convention such as `pattern-stage1-v1.sb3`; explain that the name helps a reader find the right work. The adult first models a different sample: save, find the folder, close/reset the editor and reopen. Ask the learner to create an agreed practice folder, save their own project, move a related practice file into that folder, and demonstrate that the saved project can be reopened with its intended content.

Teach the distinction between the project title shown in an app, the filename, the folder and the actual saved version. Ask the learner to save a second version under an agreed name, then reopen the first and second to explain the difference. Do not overwrite the only copy during teaching. A missing project, wrong folder or incorrect version triggers a find/check/resave repair; do not mark a download toast as successful recovery. Teach other applicable agreed conventions explicitly: a consistent heading in an accompanying note and a source credit if an external image is used. Use learner-created shapes in this kit so copying third-party artwork is unnecessary; “drawn by me” accurately records an original asset.

The independent probe starts from a fresh editor and requires the learner to find the intended file, explain the name/folder/version and reopen the correct content. Change the folder or requested version for transfer. For the whole topic's delayed-retrieval claim, revisit in a later lesson; immediate reopening alone does not certify “find it again next week”. Source-level practical file management and whole-topic sustained retrieval remain distinct evidence. If device restrictions prevent folder creation or local saving, record that condition; a screenshot of a folder is not an equivalent performed procedure.

## 4. Privacy, kindness and help-seeking

Target `mt_zIzJGkaj0Q` requires rules, reasons, people’s feelings and action when uncomfortable or asked for private information. Teach with fictional cards: an invitation to share an interesting project idea; a request for a password or home address; an unkind comment about another learner's drawing; and an uncomfortable request from someone who appears familiar. No real message is sent. Explain why another person's feelings remain real behind a screen and why a friendly-seeming sender does not make private information safe to share.

Have the learner state at least three relevant rules and apply them: protect private information, treat people respectfully, stop and seek a trusted adult when uncertain or uncomfortable. Ask who could help and allow the learner to identify a trusted adult privately without publishing a name. For a wrong response, explain the consequence using the fictional case, then offer a changed case; do not collect passwords, disclosures or a history of distress. Teach help-seeking without blaming a child who has already clicked or shared. For assessment, require the action and reason in a new scenario, rather than a repeated slogan or a signed pledge. Change the apparent sender or the request so familiarity does not supply the answer.

This is basic citizenship evidence. It does not certify an adult cyber-hygiene course, create a social-media-account duty or prove every real-world safety prerequisite. The source's broader implementation campaign remains separately scoped.

## 5. A real event-led visual program

Target `mt_sPV503BQnI` includes an event, learner-assembled blocks, motion, speech, costume change, running/observing, a mathematical movement and an order-change explanation. The adult preflights a sprite with two distinct costumes and a clearly stated reset position. Teach a different worked sample by predicting the outcome, attaching a start event and running. Then remove the sample before the learner constructs their own sequence. A paper algorithm can prepare the plan but does not replace block assembly and execution.

For the learner task, use a displayed number-line scale with ten screen steps representing one mathematical unit and an origin reset supplied by the adult. Have the learner connect a start event to three movements of20screen steps, a timed speech output between movements and a costume change. They predict that the three equal mathematical jumps of2 finish at6, and test the actual outcome. Explain the mapping: 20screen steps is2number-line units; the screen-coordinate60 is not the mathematical answer60. If the environment uses a different movement scale, restate and check that scale before the task rather than silently reuse these numbers. A marked start and reset are necessary so earlier runs do not accumulate a new origin.

Ask the learner to swap a movement block with the adjacent timed speech block, predict where/when the speech will occur, and run again from the same reset. The final position can remain the same while the order/location of output changes. Swapping two additive moves that commute is insufficient as the sole order probe. If nothing happens, check the event/attachment, selected sprite and run control before treating it as an algorithm misconception. If the visible output differs, trace the blocks in sequence and repair one change at a time. Do not credit a teacher-built script, prerecorded animation, unplugged list or a copied final screen as independent execution.

For transfer, change one motion value and predict the new mathematical total and displayed position, or move the timed output to another place in the sequence. Full whole-topic evidence includes actual learner construction, all three output actions, mathematical mapping, running, observation and an explained order change. The source component does not certify loops, branching or debugging claims beyond this unchanged whole card.

## Operational support for the five prerequisite claims

No saved teaching-research guide exists for any of the10cards in this hard closure on the accepted base. The following original prerequisite protocols supplement the direct five above. They remain separately assessed whole claims, with no source-only certification and no new prerequisite links.

**Computers in Everyday Life (`mt_XbGfVhfiUz`).** Provide five declared computer-containing examples—a laptop, tablet, phone, programmable toy and smart speaker—and contrast a paper book, wooden spoon and purely mechanical hand tool. Supply the relevant information about unfamiliar models rather than ask the learner to infer internal electronics from appearance. Teach that a computer processes information by following instructions; it need not look like a desktop. The learner identifies the five examples, explains the common property and sorts a changed set. If they classify everything with a button as a computer, contrast a declared mechanical button mechanism with a programmable device. If they say only laptops count, trace an input/instruction/output cycle in a second example. Independent evidence requires both the classification and its reason, not a memorised five-item list. Transfer to an unfamiliar device with its features supplied.

**Step-by-Step Instructions (`mt__p5n8z5soJ`).** Use two coloured blocks and a tray. Show a target arrangement, then have the learner dictate or arrange precise actions for an adult executor to build it, with a stated starting position and shared meaning for any directional term. The executor follows only the supplied instructions, without repairing omissions. Model one different example first, then let the learner construct and test their own sequence. Remove or swap a necessary step and ask them to predict the resulting mismatch, locate the cause and repair it. Explain that this executor follows the specified steps; the computer program likewise does not acquire an unstated intended step merely because the author meant it. If the learner says “do it properly”, ask which observable action that instruction specifies. Independent evidence includes a complete everyday sequence, a correct missing/order-error prediction and an explanation of exact instruction following. Transfer to a different target arrangement. Paper success prepares programming; actual block assembly and running remain separate.

**Vocabulary: understanding others (`mt_wRlf0g2MbB`).** Use fictional everyday situations and a small illustrated word bank in the learner's familiar language: empathy, perspective, kind, fair, community, similar, different and care. Model one sentence connecting a word to a real action. Explain empathy as trying to understand another person's feelings/view; distinguish sympathy as concern for someone without assuming the same feeling or perspective. The learner uses the terms to explain two people viewing the same event differently and describes community, belonging and inclusion in a familiar group. Avoid making an English word form or one fixed language pair the criterion. If they merely repeat a definition, ask for a concrete contrasting example and why the word fits. Independent evidence requires accurate use, the empathy/sympathy distinction and perspective/community examples. Transfer to a new fictional situation. Correct vocabulary alone does not prove a real-life caring habit or every empathy claim.

**Other People's Feelings and Thoughts (`mt_wzUzVEBqJb`).** Begin with a fictional shared event that two people enjoy or fear differently. Model noticing the difference without declaring one feeling invalid. Have the learner explain both perspectives and give an ordinary, non-sensitive example of different reactions to the same event; a fictional alternative is acceptable if recalling a personal example would be uncomfortable, with the evidence condition recorded. Practise a response that acknowledges a peer's feeling while retaining their own preference. If they say everyone must enjoy what they enjoy, compare the two supplied reactions and ask whether both can be real. Independent evidence requires recognising differing thoughts/feelings, a relevant example and acceptance of the other feeling's validity. Transfer to a changed event or point of view. A word definition or a kind slogan alone is insufficient. Do not infer the whole claim from one online-safety answer.

**Everyday Safety Awareness (`mt_q3vRl4dddK`).** Use familiar, age-appropriate fictional road, water, unfamiliar-person and online situations, with the local supervised safety arrangement supplied by the responsible adult. Teach a safe action and why for each, identify a trusted-adult/help-seeking route and model asking for help when a situation is uncertain. The learner applies at least three rules with reasons, explains when an adult should be told, then demonstrates a safe response in a controlled familiar role-play without a reminder. No actual road crossing, water exposure, contact with a stranger, unsafe link or disclosure is needed. If they repeat a rule but choose an unsafe action in the changed case, reteach its reason and retry another safe simulation; distinguish a prompted rehearsal from independent evidence. Transfer by changing the setting or apparent familiarity while preserving the principle. The real-life independent-behaviour evidence in the whole card remains a later responsible-adult observation; a role-play alone must not be reported as that observation. Avoid blame, requests for personal disclosures or a claim that one rule removes every risk.
