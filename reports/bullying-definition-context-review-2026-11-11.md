# Bullying definitions and help in context

The shared Understanding Bullying claim retains its identity, forms and help duties while correcting two explanatory/assessment fields. A learner uses the stated school or community definition, attends to power, harm and context, and distinguishes ordinary disagreement from bullying. Harmful conduct can require help even while the label or another person’s intent remains uncertain.

The actual [Ontario public guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/bullying-we-can-all-help-stop-it) allows a single event to qualify in context. [Provincial policy](https://www.ontario.ca/document/education-ontario-policy-and-program-direction/policyprogram-memorandum-144) describes typically repeated aggression, power imbalance and intended or reasonably foreseeable harm. The [Australian national school frame](https://www.bullyingnoway.gov.au/understand/what-is-bullying/) instead retains intentional harm and repetition or potential repetition; its [published explanatory transcripts](https://www.bullyingnoway.gov.au/resources/the-definition-of-bullying/) distinguish isolated conflict and online recirculation. Neither frame is silently made a universal legal rule or declared wrong.

The [original cases](bullying-definition-context-review-2026-11-11.json) hold harm/help apart from classification: equal-peer disagreement, isolated power-imbalanced harm, repeated exclusion, online recirculation and incomplete facts. There is no required confrontation, real abusive content, personal victim history or wait for harm to repeat before adult support.

This is a real change to explanatory and assessment criteria, not a wording-only equivalence. Consumers must retain the old claim revision and evaluate compatibility; no automatic learner mastery equivalence, evidence transfer or reset is authorised. All other claim fields, every old context and all five affected edges remain unchanged. No teaching guide exists for this claim and none is added.
