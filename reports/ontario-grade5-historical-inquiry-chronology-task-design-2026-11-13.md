## A2.5 chronology, interaction and perspective assessment

Use the three category inquiries above, including actual First Nations and Inuit parties and supported differences between the accounts. Add three explicitly attributed present-day historical accounts. These are historical interpretations by named organizations, not testimony from invented pre-1713 Métis participants:

- Métis Nation of Ontario, *Historic and Contemporary Métis Timeline — Ontario Westward*, opening and early entries: `https://www.metisnation.org/about-the-mno/metis-timeline/`. Its stated regional remit and chronology distinguish early fur-trade conditions from the later emergence of the communities it represents. Use its late-1700s onward statement and its separately dated Mattawa example without treating its page's unrelated typographical errors as lesson facts.
- Gabriel Dumont Institute, *Timeline of Métis History*, opening and early timeline, PDF page 1: `https://www.metismuseum.ca/media/document.php/149674.Timeline%20of%20M%C3%A9tis%20History.pdf`. Its Great Lakes settlement chronology is attributed to that source and region. Different regional histories are not automatically contradictory.
- Métis Nation British Columbia, *History*, opening “Who are the Métis” paragraphs: `https://www.mnbc.ca/about/history/`. It describes early fur-trade intermarriages and other relationships, followed by ethnogenesis, and expressly distinguishes a distinct people from mixed ancestry alone. Do not turn the account into a fixed universal founding year or backdate its later documented B.C. arrivals.

Prepare original blank evidence fields: actual relationship described; named parties; event period and location; account author/date; what it supports; what it does not establish; and whose perspective is represented or unavailable. The pupil reads or listens to the actual attributed selections, makes a dated comparison, and evaluates whether a claim about a pre-1713 distinct Métis community is supported by the supplied evidence. They must distinguish a historical relationship that contributed to later community formation from evidence that a named distinct community already existed at the earlier date. “All mixed families were Métis”, “a later date proves there were no Métis anywhere earlier”, and “this modern organization witnessed 1701” are unsupported conclusions.

Ask the pupil to answer the three historical inquiry questions and then the explicit chronology question in an original evidence-based report. Their report compares supported First Nations, Inuit, European and attributed Métis historical perspectives without fabricating a symmetrical interaction among every possible pair. They identify where an actual source supports an interaction, where sources differ, and where the supplied record cannot establish the proposed time-bound identity claim. Require a specific additional source request that could clarify the limited uncertainty; additional names or websites alone do not resolve it.

For fresh transfer, present an explicitly fictional museum caption claiming that a photograph of a later Mattawa community proves the same distinct community took part in a pre-1713 event. The pupil explains the date/identity inference problem, proposes an accurate qualified caption, and says what contemporary evidence would be needed. A second caption claims early intermarriage could have no relationship to later Métis history; the pupil explains why that opposite inference also exceeds the accounts. Assessment accepts a supported bounded uncertainty, not a preferred invented positive case. No judgement of a real person's identity or present rights is requested.

This complete critical inquiry qualifies A2.5 as an operational evidence-evaluation and conclusion duty, including a supported bounded uncertainty. It does not verify a pre-1713 distinct Métis community or establish all-Canada absence.
