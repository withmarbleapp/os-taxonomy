## Authoring, safeguarding and evidence boundary

Each record has at least three topic-specific activities (teacher moves, representations, learner actions, intended insight, feedback, rationale and cited sources), an `Exercise design:` constraint describing support to fade, demands to vary, transfer and boundary cases, and an independent assessment whose method follows the topic type: 62 conceptual records ask for an answer and a reason (two-tier), 28 procedural records require an observed performance in a fictional role-play or structured task, 11 language records require use in context, and 16 META records use an open epistemic probe whose exemplar is labelled "not an answer key". These are proposals, not calibrated difficulty scales or new age/mastery thresholds.

- **Safeguarding first.** Every record carries the same safeguarding constraint: fictional characters, stories and distancing techniques; no requirement to disclose personal feelings, family matters, trauma, health or peer conflicts as evidence; a right to pass; disclosures handled by a trusted adult under the school's safeguarding policy, never assessed. Assessment judges reasoning about the scenario, not the child's own emotional state. Where a topic's parent-facing `assessmentPrompt` asks about the child's own life, the record uses a fictional case instead and treats any self-example as optional.
- **No norm of emotional expression is scored.** Records do not score eye contact, stillness, calmness, quietness or a particular facial display, and do not treat one cultural way of showing feelings as correct. The EEF guidance's "appropriate eye contact" listening example and its Box 3 personal-writing task were deliberately not adopted. Neurodivergent differences are framed using the double-empathy account rather than as deficits.
- **Body safety, protective behaviours and online safety** (Early warning signs, My body belongs to me, No go tell, Protective actions online, Analysing risky situations, Everyday Safety Awareness) route every harm scenario to a trusted adult, state that harm is never the child's fault even if they froze or went along, never make the child responsible for adult decisions or for confronting an adult or aggressor, and leave vocabulary, sequencing and parental consultation to the school's RSE policy. Images: do not save, screenshot or forward; an adult helps record and report mean messages.
- **Emotions are not pathologised.** Ordinary worry, disappointment and mixed feelings are treated as normal (DfE RSHE). Mental-health content for ages 11–14 routes warning signs to trusted adults and professionals, with no self-diagnosis.
- **Contested evidence is qualified.** Growth-mindset intervention effects are weak (Sisk et al., 2018); delay-of-gratification links to later outcomes largely disappear after controls (Watts et al., 2018, via APS); reappraisal/suppression findings are adult laboratory evidence and restraining expression can be culturally appropriate (Gross, 2002); posttraumatic growth is possible but contested and never expected; eustress/distress is distinguished from Harvard's positive/tolerable/toxic stress framework; procrastination is "often" an emotion-regulation problem rather than "only". Emotional-intelligence outcome claims, leadership-style taxonomies, unconscious-bias measurement, effective altruism and the three-framework ethics simplification are presented as contested.
- **Factual content.** Australian civics answer keys follow the Parliamentary Education Office and AEC (both houses in identical form plus Royal Assent; statute overrides common law; compulsory federal enrolment 1912 and voting 1924; private voting). The 1856 secret-ballot history and the enrolment year disputed between AEC pages (1911/1912) are not assessed. Flag meanings come from the Australian Museum and the Torres Strait Regional Authority; the golden wattle dates from the Australian National Botanic Gardens. The Coat of Arms official pages (PM&C/APH) were blocked, so records keep to the topic's own description. "More than 60,000 years" is presented as the Australian Curriculum's frame, with dating estimates differing by site and method; "more than 250 languages" is presented as a commonly cited figure for teachers to confirm from an approved source (AIATSIS could not be fetched). Rights are universal and not conditional on responsibilities (UNICEF UK). No gender pay-gap percentage is stated.
- **Diagnostics.** All 236 diagnostics are explicitly **authored observable performance/reasoning checks** with an observable tell and a next teaching move; none claims documented misconception prevalence, none supplies a distractor, and `commonMisconceptions` is empty. Any prerequisite recovery uses an actual edge. Each diagnostic names its own evidence basis: 64 name the one cited source whose checked claim supports the requirement being probed, and 172 say they rest only on the topic's stated mastery evidence. `validate.mjs` rejects a basis that is not one of the record's own sources.

### Sources and practical use

[source-checks.json](source-checks.json) records **41 checked source entries**, the sections used and exclusions. Core SEL/PSHE/RSE grounding comes from CASEL, the EEF primary SEL guidance report, the DfE RSHE statutory guidance (2025, in force 1 September 2026) and the ACARA Personal and Social capability; topic-specific claims use government, museum, curriculum and peer-reviewed sources. Source verification applies to the precise citation claim, not every sentence on a page or an activity's efficacy. Sites that blocked automated fetching (eSafety Commissioner, NSPCC, PM&C, AIATSIS, BBC, PSHE Association guidance PDF, EEF website) are **not** cited; where a record depends on their subject matter it says so and is rated low. England's RSHE guidance and Australian civics sources are jurisdiction-specific references, not universal rules.

These are teaching-research records, not finished lesson packs. Schools still supply their own safeguarding policy, reporting routes, approved local cultural and faith materials, current statistics and age-appropriate texts. Physical or real-world tasks (community action, petitions, interviews) remain under existing school supervision and permission arrangements.

No learner trial, practitioner or safeguarding-lead review, community/cultural review or independent content review has occurred. The author's source/content pass and automated checks do not replace those review gates.

### Review corrections (after Greptile review)

Greptile's first review found a diagnostic whose evidence basis was a habit-formation study (Lally et al., via the BPS digest) although it probed the emotion-regulation account of procrastination, and a vocabulary story that called a division of labour a compromise. The cause of the first was systematic: the staging builder had attached each record's first source to every diagnostic. Every diagnostic now carries an explicit basis (above). Both flagged records were rewritten, and a fresh audit of all 116 records then present (before the bullying record was added under the same per-diagnostic basis rule) checked every activity's citations against the checked claim text, removing citations that did not support the activity and rewording insights that went beyond their source (for example, "habits form in a consistent context", which the BPS digest says the data could not settle). The same audit corrected other concept slips: a turn-taking reply and a swing story labelled as compromises; jealous used for envy; rumination labelled as suppression; a groupthink example that described false consensus; bystander/upstander definitions; an NVC "observation" that overgeneralised; deontology described as ignoring intentions. One record (Fairness, Equality and Equity) was lowered to low confidence because no fetched source covers equal versus equitable treatment.
