## T9 — vocabulary/reference input and final boundaries

Actual concise reference entries for explicit teaching: label—a sign naming/identifying something; return—put back or bring back; revise—change a draft in light of purpose/evidence; response—answer to a stated question; timetable—plan of times; route—path from one place to another; sequence—order of events; evidence—information supporting or challenging a claim; inference—interpretation reached using evidence and relevant knowledge; perspective—a position/view shaped by situation, purpose and experience, distinct from grammatical first/third narration; credibility—grounds for trusting a source for a particular question; reliability—dependability judged from its source/method/corroboration, not just attractive design; bias—selective favouring/framing that may neglect relevant evidence or people, needing evidence rather than merely disagreement; corroborate—seek supporting information from an independent relevant source, not copies of the same claim; accessible—usable by a particular person in the relevant context, not universally guaranteed by one feature; proportion—a part's relation to a stated whole; interpret—explain a supported meaning; implicit—suggested rather than directly stated; morpheme—a meaningful word part, not any similar-looking letter string; genre—a category with characteristic conventions, not an unchangeable checklist for every text.

Teach the selected words in actual sentences, use them in reading/oral/writing and another subject/context, then ask the learner to apply/interpret a fresh related form with evidence. A dictionary definition alone is not productive vocabulary or automaticity. Current broader source/contributor vocabulary inventories remain separate.
