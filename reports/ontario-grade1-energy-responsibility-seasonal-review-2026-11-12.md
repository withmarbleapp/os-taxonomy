# Ontario Grade 1 energy: responsibility, consequences and seasonal uses

This reviewed source-authoring follow-up closes C1, C1.1, C1.2 and C2.6 through
six component pairs in five already existing Ontario 6–7 contexts. It adds no
topic or age interval and changes no claim, guide or prerequisite. The
[current energy ledger](ontario-grade1-energy-current-ledger-2026-11-12.json)
contains eight Full source judgments across both energy portfolios; C2 and C2.5
remain Partial. Seasonal-cause E2.1 remains a separate Partial.

The [complete readable resource](ontario-grade1-energy-responsibility-seasonal-task-design-2026-11-12.json)
provides read-aloud/printable study cards, teaching sequences and separate
independent assessments. Supplied facts establish the model premises; learners
must produce the assessed choices, causal explanations, comparisons and transfer.
Copying a worked choice, receiving cards or hearing an adult's explanation does
not establish performance. These are authoring coverage judgments, with no
classroom delivery, efficacy or observed learner attainment claimed.

The seasonal teacher wording explicitly calls the introductory energy examples
**light energy, thermal/heat energy and movement energy**. Illumination, warming
and movement support those examples; an arbitrary effect does not automatically
become a separate formal energy form. Ordinary words with correct energy
reasoning are accepted. No full older chemical/elastic/gravitational vocabulary,
formal stores-and-pathways repertoire or kinetic-word recall gate is added.
Food-enabled movement can remain across seasons while room-lighting, warming and
fan uses differ. The cases are selected days, not universal weather or energy
consumption rules.

The electricity-loss model removes usable mains, battery and generator supplies
for the selected devices while natural sunlight continues. It therefore differs
from a street-mains outage. The indoor plant loses its stated adequate-light
provision; the outdoor plant in sufficient sunlight has a different consequence.
The task does not assert that all plants need electric lamps, predict instant
death or remove all biological electric phenomena. No real organism deprivation
or device opening is involved.

The fictional generation model explicitly specifies its lower-output/lower-fuel
response. [EIA's conservation account](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/use-of-energy/efficiency-and-conservation.php)
supports unused-light demand reduction and potential emission benefits;
[EIA's electricity/environment account](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/electricity-and-the-environment.php)
supports differing generation impacts and pollutants from fuel burning. Bounded
read scopes exclude laws, programme counts, prices and clinical effects. The
model is not a guarantee that every real grid immediately responds in the same
way. The selected plant facts use the bounded opening of [University of Minnesota
Extension's lighting account](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/lighting-for-indoor-plants).

Full contributor claims remain independently assessed: RHI retains three harms,
three solutions and explained benefits; plant and sunlight observation duties,
living-needs repertoires, real-year seasonal observations and actual clothing
choices remain intact. Declared device examples do not offer the broader whole
appliance claim at a younger age. All historical snapshots and unaffected source
contexts are preserved; the earlier four-record energy report remains its own
accepted checkpoint, with this report supplying the follow-up.
