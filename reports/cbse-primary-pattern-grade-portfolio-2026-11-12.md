# Three grade-specific pattern portfolios: original reviewed portfolio

This reviewed authoring portfolio addresses three separate catalogue parent positions G3-LO02, G4-LO02 and G5-LO02 in the CBSE CT/AI supplement (Classes III–V). The companion review authors three independent source-authoring Full dispositions and six component associations; no learner attainment is inferred. The tasks below are original. A short prefix can support several rules: accept a clearly stated alternative that fits every shown term and independently predicts a fresh case. Never treat the teacher's intended continuation as the only logically possible one. Ask the learner to state their rule before revealing the changed case; record when a newly revealed term requires revision.

Use blank cards, counters, coloured pencils and a square with one marked corner. The adult may read instructions or record a faithfully dictated explanation. The learner chooses the relation and constructs the next terms. Model a different small example first; hide all completed examples/keys during independent checks. Check both the ordering of items and the changing feature. A lucky next-card choice is insufficient. Rehearse separately, then give fresh starts, different objects and changed conditions without step-by-step prompts.

## Class III independent portfolio

<a id="p3-one-change"></a>
1. **A stable numerical change.** Cards show 4,7,10,13. Say a rule, build three further cards and test a new start of 8 under the same stated rule. Intended add-three key:16,19,22; changed start8,11,14,17. Explain why merely copying the last digit fails. A consistent alternative needs its own independently tested continuation, not an after-the-fact rule for each item.

<a id="p3-two-features"></a>
2. **Two visible features.** Construct cards showing a row of1red square,2blue squares,3red squares,4blue squares. The learner predicts and constructs cards5and6, states the count rule and colour cycle, and explains a wrong card containing5blue squares. Key:5red,6blue. Change the first colour to blue, leaving the count start at1:5blue,6red. Colour is accessible by an additional R/B symbol or texture; do not make colour discrimination a reasoning gate.

<a id="p3-letters"></a>
3. **A stated finite inventory.** Supply the declared row A B C D E F G H I J K L, naming it a local code inventory, not the learner's assumed alphabet. Shown cards A,C,E,G skip one inventory entry each time. Construct I,K and explain the skipped entries. Changed startB gives B,D,F,H,J,L. This tests the supplied relation without certifying English alphabet knowledge or every language's script.

<a id="p3-transfer"></a>
**Independent integrated transfer.** Provide three dots in an outlined box, then four dots in a double-outlined box, then five dots in a single-outlined box, then six dots in a double-outlined box. Before making another card, state the two relations. Construct the next three cards:7single,8double,9single. Replace dots by counters on trays and change the initial amount to2; generate2single,3double,4single,5double. Explain which relation remained and which starting condition changed. Assess each relation independently, including a deliberately wrong amount with the correct border.

<a id="p4-growth-turn"></a>
2. **Growth with orientation.** Draw five cards with1,2,3,4,5small squares in a row, and an arrow pointing up,right,down,left,up respectively. The learner independently draws the next three cards:6right,7down,8left, explains the quantity progression and quarter-turn cycle, and corrects a7left card. Change the arrow's first direction toleft while retaining the same clockwise progression:cards6up,7right,8down. A printed compass reference may remove vocabulary barriers; an adult must not rotate the arrow for the learner.

## Class V independent portfolio

<a id="p5-progressive"></a>

<a id="p5-transfer"></a>
**Independent integrated transfer.** The learner designs and demonstrates a new six-card progression with increasing amount steps, a declared repeating symbol relation of length at least3, and a stated directional change. Before giving cards to a partner, write or dictate the rules and construct hidden cards7and8. The partner proposes continuations; the author checks every feature against the recorded rules and explains any consistent alternative rather than declaring their intention unique from a short prefix. Then swap to a fresh teacher-given design:counts3,5,8,12;symbolsA,B,C,A;directionsup,left,down,right. Intended next cards17/B/up and23/C/left, using increments2,3,4,5,6, a three-symbol repeat and counter-clockwise turns. Change only direction toclockwise and generate a fresh first-six demonstration. Do not award from the author's own familiar example alone.

## Whole retained claims and source boundary

For **Patterns & Sequences**, independently construct a repeat unit longer thanAB, identify the smallest unit, continue it, reproduce the same structure using different materials and invent a different repeating arrangement. The changing-step tasks above do not replace this repeating-pattern repertoire. A learner may perform a growing pattern while still failing to isolate a repeat unit.

For **Shape patterns**, independently generate from a supplied number/shape rule and identify a feature that the wording did not explicitly state, explaining why the rule produces it. Use the retained original C2 operational protocol and fresh number/growing-shape cases. Example: start3andadd4gives only odd values, because adding an even amount preserves oddness. Change tostart2andadd3and explain why parity now alternates. Full multiplication/generation duties, current hard prerequisites and fresh changed cases remain independent; the parent task alone awards none automatically.

The companion review uses those two unchanged existing component claims at each exact grade context, after complete hard-path age/access review. It adds no topic or direct link to time, formal long division or an unspecified generic 'pattern skill'. No CG1/CG2 goal, other parent, named-language inventory or prerequisite mastery is promoted from these three portfolios.
