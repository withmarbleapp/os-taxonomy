# Grade 2 heritage channels: bounded reuse/resource review

This bounded follow-up starts at `dab1194` on the G2 world-reference proposal. It reviews A3.6/A3.7 with actual attributed cases and existing full claims. The [machine dossier](ontario-grade2-heritage-resource-review-2026-11-10.json) records candidate contributions, exact limits and two source-fit protocols. Following full source/case/claim review, the two protocols resolve A3.6/A3.7 as Full authoring coverage and add only the corresponding existing-topic associations. No new claim is added.

The [official Grade 2 clauses](https://www.dcp.edu.gov.on.ca/en/curriculum/elementary-sshg/grades/g2) ask identifying some ways heritage is passed through family/community practices. They do not require an abstract cultural-transmission theory, comparison with genetic inheritance or every illustrative ceremony/event. A3.7's specific First Nation, Métis or Inuit family practice remains mandatory.

## Attributed ordinary channels

The [Toronto & York Region Métis Council's Kathy Morgan account](https://tyrmc.org/spotlights/the-journey-to-metis-self-identity-a-collection-of-stories-from-my-great-aunt-kathy/) describes family-history conversations with her mother/grandmother and family learning through Métis gatherings. The [MNO council list](https://www.metisnation.org/community-councils/council-contacts/) directly links the council's site. Use a short factual family-sharing paraphrase, not unrelated adult content from the full account. It is a particular family, not every Métis household; the interviewer explicitly identifies as non-Indigenous, so participation or family connection does not automatically confer Métis identity.

[LAC archivist Dylan Roy's 2024 first-person account](https://thediscoverblog.com/2024/04/25/sweet-potato-pie-a-timeless-delight-from-1909-to-today/) uses a 1909 Sault Ste. Marie community cookbook. A written community resource preserved practical knowledge for a later reader. This demonstrates a channel, not an unbroken annual family custom or a practice shared by every cultural group. No cooking or recipe/image copying is required.

[Nipissing First Nation's own language resource](https://nfn.ca/culture-heritage/anishinaabemwin/) describes fluent speakers supporting community classes and language/cultural learning in children's programmes. This is another way community knowledge is shared, not evidence of every family's practice or a pupil's language proficiency. Learning about the programme requires neither enrolment nor purchasing its separate materials.

## Exact reuse and meaningful assessment

EvidenceFromPast explicitly explains how surviving stories/documents/objects let later people know past lives, directly contributing to the oral/written cases. FamilyOrigins contributes relationships while interpreting the case, but its whole claim concerns the pupil’s own family; another family’s account does not demonstrate that whole claim. Celebrations describes shared practices and how occasions are marked. Clear description and vocabulary help communicate what the facts establish. None of those labels alone is sufficient: the task must identify **what is shared, by whom, how another generation learns it**, with the particular source retained.

A near miss only names a holiday/food/relative or says everyone has traditions. Transfer uses another source-supported family/community practice and a different channel. The family protocol includes the verified Métis family case plus another ordinary attributed or voluntarily chosen family example. It does not replace that mandatory qualifier with a fictional generic story.

ChildhoodThenNow and TechnologyChanges are separately useful for comparing continuity/change and an evidenced cause; their scope is not silently expanded. RetellingStories has a different narrative-structure claim. This task does not impose a universal beginning/middle/end on Métis oral traditions or require performance of a protected story/ceremony.

The approved CanadianRenewal card retains its specific **colonial disruption → community-led renewal → identity** relationship. It is not broadened into all ordinary heritage transmission, and its G2 A1.4 reuse remains separately dependent. The bounded source-fit review supports reuse for these identification requirements; a generic theory node is unnecessary.

This is an authoring proposal, not learner attainment, optional Native Languages integration, community-membership certification or DB publication. No private learner records or restricted curriculum wording are included.
