## Portable claim and reuse proof

Neutral name **Supporting loads and stabilising structures**, Science / Design&Technology, CONCEPTUAL. Explain how a structure's material, form and member arrangement support a load and resist unwanted changes of shape, balance or position; distinguish load-bearing strength from stability and use struts/ties to explain a simple braced framework. Initial supported context Ontario8–9 only, grounded in the complete Grade3 repertoire below; do not invent a global8–12 offer. No new hard prerequisites: local push/pull/material/shape words can be introduced within the claim, while full broad ForcesVocabulary, formal force diagrams or older beam-bending mastery are not necessary to every complete response.

Four whole-claim evidence criteria:

1. Identify a natural and a built supporting framework, its load, form/size and function, and explain how the form serves the function rather than label every object a structure without a load/support relationship.
2. Use evidence from controlled model comparisons to explain strength as ability to support a load, and how changes in member/material arrangement can improve it. Include more than one material or strengthening technique; distinguish tested results from “thicker/folded always wins”.
3. Distinguish strength from stability using cases where a strong object can topple or slide and a balanced-looking object can collapse under load. Explain a justified improvement and transfer to changed load/force/position cases, including a floating structure as a supported comparison, without assuming every structure must float.
4. For a specified simple framework under load, explain how a rigid strut resists compression and a tie resists tension, then predict/check a changed loading direction or member substitution. The role depends on how it is loaded, not “all diagonals are struts” or whether the member visibly breaks.

Assessment prompt: Can {{name}} explain how two structures support loads, use fair comparisons to improve strength and stability, and show why a strut and a tie help a simple framework differently when the load changes?

## Reproducible original model resources and first teaching

**Load cards.** Draw three original simple panels: a tree trunk/branches supporting its crown; a human skeleton supporting the body; and a supported footbridge carrying its deck/people. Label these as explanatory diagrams, not evidence every tree/building is safe. Add a school roof supported on walls/pillars. Learner marks framework, what it supports, form/size and purpose. A load may be the structure's own weight as well as added objects. Use actual safe external observation or attributed images to connect to real forms; no climbing or loading real buildings.

**Strength bench.** Use small identical sheets of paper over equal low support gaps with a catch tray. Compare flat, accordion-folded and rolled/folded-section paper while keeping sheet amount, span, support and load position controlled as far as the chosen comparison permits. Add small identical counters gently, record load before a stated safe limit or observed deformation, repeat and compare actual outcomes; stop before uncontrolled collapse. Draw the cross-section and keep the material “paper” unchanged. Do not call different spans/material amounts a clean shape comparison. For a second material, compare an adult-selected thin card/wood-strip assembly with one versus two securely joined layers under matched conditions; record that added material/joining is the changed factor. No child snaps wood/metal or performs hazardous failure testing. State actual preflight, result and uncertainty; a thick sample is not universally stronger for every purpose.

**Stability bench.** Stand the same light block/tower in a tall-narrow and low-wide arrangement; compare a small reproducible lateral push with a safe enclosed model/catch area. Keep the applied point/strength and floor as comparable as feasible and discuss remaining confounds. A wider base/lower load position can resist tipping, while anchoring/friction can resist sliding; these are different mechanisms. Compare a stiff tall model that topples with a low but flimsy paper support that collapses under a small load. Strong does not guarantee stable; merely staying upright unloaded does not prove strength. A shallow water tray with a light adult-selected floating foil/card boat supplies a separate load-distribution case: compare central versus off-centre equal load, record actual balance/position and revise the form/load placement. The model must actually float before its stability is assessed; no claim all structures need to float or every wider boat is better. Pupil contact with deep/open water is unnecessary.

**Strut/tie frame, exact configuration.** Draw a square with corners A bottom-left, B bottom-right, C top-right, D top-left. Build four equal10cm rigid card strips with freely moving adult-prepared corner fasteners. Fix/hold bottom edge AB in the same position and gently move the top edge to the right. Before bracing, the square can shear into a parallelogram; actual friction/joints must be preflighted. The diagonal AC tends to lengthen, so a taut low-stretch string tie from A to C resists that separation in tension. The diagonal DB tends to shorten, so a fitted rigid diagonal strut from D to B resists compression. Draw solid members separately and label force tendencies, not numerical forces. The string does not push effectively when slack; the rigid member may resist compression without visibly shrinking or breaking. Under a leftward change, those diagonal length tendencies reverse; a previous tension-only string can become slack. The loading/support configuration is part of every answer, not an invisible assumption. Use actual gentle model observations and an explicit hypothetical changed case if the alternative setup cannot be safely demonstrated.

Independent material preparation supplies all four frame strips, two named diagonals/string alternatives and blank before/after diagrams; the fully reproducible instructions above are the original resource. Adult hole-making/pre-cutting/holding support is allowed and recorded; adult choosing every member/loading conclusion is not independent evidence. No formulas, force meters, professional structural calculations or always-vertical strut rule.
