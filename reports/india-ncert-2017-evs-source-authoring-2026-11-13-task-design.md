## A — Animal groups, nesting, senses and responses

Use these attributed short fact cards. Smithsonian National Zoo's actual Asian-elephant Communication/SocialStructure and trunk/senses sections were read at https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/asian-elephant . RSPB's actual nesting body was read at https://www.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/birds-nests-and-nestboxes . Only the bounded facts below enter pupil material, not zoo statistics, adult reproduction detail, wildlife law or a UK nesting calendar presented as India's.

Original Asian-elephant card: Related females and young commonly live together. Members help raise young and protect the group. Warning calls can prompt the herd to gather and move away. Elephants can communicate with very low sounds difficult for people to hear. Their trunk also takes in scent information; smell helps detect other elephants or possible danger. This is a species account, not a claim every elephant always behaves identically or every herd has a fixed matriarch.

Original nesting card: RSPB describes UK Robins and Wrens bringing material such as moss and small twigs into sheltered nesting places. Vegetation can support a nest and shelter eggs and chicks. This is an attributed example of bird behaviour, not a claim that all birds build identical nests, every nest is occupied now or these species are the pupil's own local birds.

Teaching: draw a declared representation of the herd, call, changed grouping and movement; have pupil explain what group cooperation does beyond naming several elephants. Separately identify nest-building behaviour and relate material/place to support/protection. Explain low-sound communication and scent detection using the actual card, distinguishing sense, body feature, information and response. A loudness or pitch experiment on pupils is not evidence of elephant sensitivity, and playing an inaudible tone is not required. No animal approach, nest search, feeding or disturbance is required.

Fresh independent assessment: present the same actual fact inputs without completed arrows. Pupil explains why gathering/protecting young is group behaviour, contrasts it with one animal drinking alone, explains one nesting material/place function and explains two different elephant information/response routes. Change the declared model: an elephant has no visual view of companions but can receive a low call; what information route could still work? A scent clue supplies different information; the pupil must not claim every low sound is heard by every animal or that smell reveals a person's thoughts. Compare a new checked local species case if local-nature transfer is claimed; do not invent the species fact.
