## Distinguish the remaining work

**Resource/localization work.** A complete existing claim may need a verified
Ontario case or culturally appropriate resource. Examples include community
contributions (A3.3 across grades), Grade 2 drinking-water access and household
substance symbols, Grade 3 cultural plant uses and local-food comparisons, Grade 4
community geological knowledge, Grade 5 conservation knowledge and Grade 6
agriculture/electricity community impacts. Country names and examples do not
justify duplicate mastery topics. The existing soil/crop and invasive-species
localizations demonstrate how source verification can resolve a condition without
adding a topic. Cultural specificity still needs appropriate community authority;
generic ecological knowledge or Australian Country evidence cannot be relabelled.

**Integrated task/performance work.** Investigation, coding-in-science,
interdisciplinary design and audience-specific reporting are combinations of
existing operations. Waste response, local conservation and school advocacy
include action, not only a planned answer. Grade 2 life-cycle samples and simple
machine categories require complete case checks. A union of links does not prove
the integrated task, but a missing integrated task is not automatically a missing
mastery claim. Keep assessed operations separate from evidence of actually
performing the mandated activity.

**Whole-claim age fit.** A later claim can contain the matching elementary
component plus substantial additional demands. Examples are technical energy
vocabulary for Grade 1, full conservation/dissipation for Grade 5, pressure-wave
or ray-diagram accounts for Grade 4, the extended circuit-rule claim for Grade 6,
and the Mars/SETI claim for Grade 6 space needs. Retain the real contribution as a
candidate; review the entire claim's developmental fit before adding an earlier
interval. Do not silently lower it from one easy clause, or clone the same
knowledge for a younger source grade. A later component is not a baseline gap.

**Context/readiness work.** The prerequisite index retains topic IDs, contributing
holders, source codes and selected intervals. Review actual consumer behavior and
appropriate guidance before deciding whether any context work is needed.
Semantically unjustified source links and invented dependencies are not a fix.
There are no age-only or dependency edits in this chain.

**Source scope/interpretation.** All selected official meanings were available;
there is no wording-access blocker. Some requirements remain broad or do not
specify a fixed exhaustive case list. For example, a body-system requirement does
not impose an unlisted compulsory seven-system checklist. Grade 6 D1's
environmental aviation judgment differs from D1.1's societal judgment; neither is
inferred from the other. Such distinctions explain independent review, not a
claim that the source is defective or that more nodes are needed.

## Recommended sequence

1. Review and reconcile the grade proposals in their dependency order, preserving
   every earlier scoped association and exact base. Repository review accepts
   semantics; it does not publish or certify learner state.
2. Build a small set of source-fit task/resource dossiers around recurring
   investigation/design/coding and environmental/cultural case conditions. Use
   positive, near-miss and transfer evidence on stable existing claims. Keep actual
   action requirements explicit.
3. Review consumer readiness against the indexed real prerequisites and existing
   global/framework guidance. Decide context changes from semantics and selection
   behavior, rather than trying to make the diagnostic count zero.
4. Review the few independent mismatch candidates and narrow scientific corrections
   separately, with supported authored-change tooling. Existing-ID cleanup is its
   own evidence/identity decision; it is not a merge instruction.

The next useful alignment work is evidence/resource and integrated-task review,
not a batch of new topics. The comprehensive primary Science audit can be handed
off for review while Full-coverage conditions remain visible. The broader Ontario
subject inventory remains a separate [primary status report](ontario-primary-status-2026-11-09.md).
