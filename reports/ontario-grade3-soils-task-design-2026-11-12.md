## Complete original study kit

Draw three original soil cross-section cards, labelled as conceptual models, not photographs. CardA shows mineral particles, decomposed plant material, spaces containing air/water, roots, a worm and a magnified inset marked tiny living organisms. CardB has tightly pressed particles, little connected pore space and water pooling above; no specific field diagnosis is implied. CardC has an arrow from a salted road to adjacent soil labelled runoff/splash. The child is given the physical facts; an explanatory conclusion is not printed on the learner card. The word “healthy” is not attached to a single colour or creature.

Teacher factual key: soil includes living organisms and non-living mineral material, decomposed material, water and air. Soil function includes supporting roots/organisms and movement/storage of water and nutrients. Structure, water movement and biological evidence are useful indicators together; one worm, dark colour or crop result cannot establish every aspect of soil health. Do not infer soil is sterile from a picture with no visible organisms or test drinking-water safety from soil appearance.

Make original addition cards: **finished compost** (decomposed organic amendment), **road-salt runoff** (an input capable of harming soil/vegetation at sufficient concentration), and **water** (a soil component whose movement and availability matter). Finished compost can improve organic matter/structure and water/nutrient retention. A salt input is not always harmless because the road, rather than the garden, received it. Avoid “more is always better”, universal application advice, numerical thresholds, practical dosing or actual contamination trials. Water is not a universal cure for every plant/soil problem. These are conceptual cases, not instruction to apply substances.

Primary factual references actually read: NRCS SoilHealth and SoilHealthAssessment main bodies; EPA CompostingAtHome complete selected process/benefits body; ECCC road-salts preface and soil/vegetation passages. Actual bounded sources are [NRCS assessment](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/conservation-basics/soil/soil-health/soil-health-assessment), [NRCS soil health](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/conservation-basics/soil/soil-health), [EPA composting](https://www.epa.gov/recycle/composting-home) and [ECCC road salts](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/pollutants/road-salts/code-practice-environmental-management.html). ## E2.2: entering substances and condition-bounded effects

Teach different initial examples using the addition cards. For independent assessment give a new diagram of a garden receiving finished compost and another receiving repeated salty runoff. Learner identifies what entered each, describes a supported effect on soil function/organisms/plants and explains which missing facts prevent an exact outcome prediction. Correct near misses: “Every addition is food”, “salt on a road can never reach soil”, “compost removes any unknown contamination”, and “if a little helps, unlimited quantities must help more”.

Changed case: a bag contains only dried/grind-up scraps, not finished decomposed compost. Ask whether the same finished-product claim follows immediately; key no. Another case changes the root/water condition without specifying any new substance; pupil distinguishes a physical condition from an invented pollutant. An independent explanation must use the actual case facts; the teacher does not read the completed response. No agricultural or clinical recommendation is scored.

## E2.6: original managed composting sequence

Make six blank drawing cards with simple original symbols and these neutral labels: suitable plant scraps/dry leaves; air and suitable moisture; tiny organisms acting over time; changed decomposed material; finished compost; soil use. Teach the factual process on a separate example, then shuffle the learner cards. Pupil orders and explains the sequence, identifies what people manage and what organisms do, and explains two bounded benefits (less discarded organic material, improved organic matter/soil water or nutrient retention). Air/moisture are continuing conditions, not a single magic step or an instant transformation.

Independent error case: a closed dry bag contains chopped leaves and dried vegetable scraps, and someone calls it finished compost immediately. Learner explains why grinding/drying/collection alone is not the complete managed biological process. Change to a correctly decomposed finished material and ask what use/benefit is justified and what exact soil outcome remains unknown. A fresh missing-condition sequence checks transfer. The teacher supplies reproducible drawings; no nonexistent asset or unperformed multi-month observation is implied.

The study task is conceptual. Any actual adult-managed compost observation uses its own authorised setup/date/material record and is reported only if performed. Do not use a hot-compost recipe, child waste handling, worm procurement or invented “before/after” evidence as an invisible requirement. Existing WorldOfMinibeasts retains all three ecological roles and taxonomic/recognition repertoire; decomposer naming alone does not demonstrate composting, and a compost explanation does not award that whole topic.

## Retained Ontario soil-type and erosion case set

Use the actual pinned Ontario farm-soils source printed14–15: sandy soil is coarse and drains quickly; clay is finer with slower drainage and can compact when wet; loam combines intermediate textures and can hold water/nutrients while draining. The actual vegetable guide printed130 describes carrots in deep well-drained muck and sandy-loam soils; printed184 describes garlic across sandy-to-clay loams. Preferences overlap and other conditions matter. Neither crop is exclusive to one soil; no pH, planting calendar, irrigation or fertilizer-rate gate is added.

Pupil independently compares at least two soils and explains why a selected documented soil property could suit the crop. New case: two soils have the same crop label but different drainage/structure. Ask why the label alone does not guarantee equal growth. Full PlantNeeds retains air/light/water/nutrients/space, varying requirements and actual observation checks from its unchanged guides; a supplied crop preference is not an experiment.

The actual Ontario SoilManagement infosheet PDFpages2–3 documents water/wind removal of productive soil and protective cover/residue/windbreak strategies. Explain cause→soil movement→loss of soil/organic matter/nutrients and possible effects on nearby water, rather than only say “erosion is bad”. For the full PreventingErosion claim, provide actual reproducible test instructions: two same-size sloping trays with the same selected clean substrate and equal measured applied water, compare two deliberately different protective arrangements under the same setup, collect runoff into clear containers and record actual soil movement. ## E1: independent overall society/environment and human action judgment
