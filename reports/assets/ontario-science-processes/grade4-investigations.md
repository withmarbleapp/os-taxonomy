# Grade 4: habitats, light, mechanisms and rocks

Use the [common process records](shared-process-records.md) with four actual contexts. These original small-scale investigations do not certify a habitat intervention, lifting device or geological identification. Read the selected Ontario 2022 habitat/light/machine/rock content passages with source identity and compare them with actual observations; the curriculum gives scientific questions and requirements, not validation of these particular methods.

## B — A model of shelter conditions

Question: does a model cover change water loss from otherwise similar damp surfaces in our classroom setup? Use identical shallow trays with equal clean damp paper surfaces, one covered above with a ventilated model shelter and one exposed, at the same place and interval. Measure the same starting water amount and actual remaining mass/visible dampness by an adult-approved method. Repeat where practical and record inconclusive results. Compare this bounded model with an actual non-invasive observation of an adult-selected accessible school patch: record the shade/shelter, visible surface and organisms only from a safe path, without handling, sampling or disturbing living things. No organism is placed in the model or deliberately dried. The model result is not proof of species abundance, food-web stability or which habitat an animal must choose. Changed case: a different ventilation opening; test before claiming every shelter has the same effect. A clearly fictional prepared patch may support source interpretation if access is unavailable, but it does not become a real field observation.

## C — Shadow size and position

Question: how does changing the distance between a safe cool classroom light and an opaque object affect its shadow on a fixed screen? Use an adult-approved cool low-voltage light, no lasers, bright eye-directed beam, heat source or Sun viewing. Keep screen/object position, object orientation and light setting constant while changing one distance. Record actual positions and shadow sizes using a consistent ruler method; repeat and note blurry boundaries. Draw the arrangement with labels. Changed case: change the object's orientation while keeping distance fixed; distinguish this different variable from the original test. Do not call a shadow measurement a direct measure of temperature or proof of every property of light.

## D — A low-load wheel/axle or pulley model

An actual partner needs to move a small classroom model load along a low safe route. Define two criteria (reaches the marked destination and stays controlled within the route) and material/time constraints. Compare three sketches, then build two alternatives with adult-supplied intact classroom mechanisms and suitable card supports. All loads remain small and near the surface; no suspended loads over people, neck loops, riding or real equipment alterations. Compare the same load, route and movement method, recording actual movement distance and observable control; an adult-approved force meter may be used if available and its safe range/instructions are understood, but do not invent force values from perceived ease. Test both, record strengths/weaknesses, obtain partner feedback and revise/retest. Changed case: a longer route or different floor surface; reconsider the comparison. Model observations do not certify real lifting capacity or an exact ratio without the relevant measurements.

## E — Water and selected rock samples

Question: do the selected intact rock samples take up the same amount of water under our method? The adult supplies large smooth known classroom specimens, a suitable balance and small room-temperature water containers; no unknown minerals, sharp fragments, acid tests, crushing, hammering or dust. Observe visible same/different features using a hand lens indoors. Record initial dry mass with a consistent method, immerse for the same interval and remove surface water consistently before reweighing; repeat where practical. Explain that apparent change may include residual surface water and measurement limits. Do not infer the whole rock cycle, every mineral or a rock's formation solely from this comparison. Changed case: a sample with a visible crack; discuss the limitation and test rather than confidently assign its entire composition.

## Communication and assessment

Across all four contexts, pose at least three own questions and match the investigation method. Prepare a shared written/drawn source-and-observation project with the actual published source identity, firsthand records and limits distinguished. Use meaningful tables/measurement plots only when supported by actual records, a labelled arrangement diagram and an observable-feature key where checking the full existing data claim. Give an evidence-backed conclusion, a changed-case prediction and a relevant improvement. Present an explanation to the partner who will use the design and a concise method/results account to another investigator. The common source-assessment contrast, actual safe tool use, independent choices, two tested solutions and feedback/revision are required programme actions; no illustrative sample number is entered as a measured result.
