# Grade 3: plants, forces, structures and soil

Use the [common process records](shared-process-records.md). Perform all four contexts, with adult-approved materials and actual records. Selected Ontario 2022 content passages on plants, forces, structures and soil are read with source identity and compared with the pupil's firsthand records; their content requirements do not validate our original methods. Do not import a result from a textbook into the pupil's result column.

## B — Seedling response

Question: does the amount of water supplied affect the growth of the selected seedlings in this setup? Use the same untreated species, similar starting seedlings, the same clean growing medium, container, light, place and observation interval, changing only the measured water amount between two suitable conditions. The adult chooses amounts that permit an ethical short classroom investigation and a care/recovery plan; do not deliberately kill a plant. No tasting, mould handling or public collection. Record actual appearance/height at agreed times, including little growth or variation, using more than one seedling per condition where practical. Explain why initial differences and the small sample limit the conclusion. Changed case: different initial seedling sizes; identify the confound rather than claim the old comparison still isolates water.

## C — Magnetic force through a barrier

Question: does an intervening material affect whether a weak classroom magnet can move a large enclosed magnetic target in this setup? The adult supplies intact age-approved weak magnets and a sealed large target, no loose small magnets, medical-device exposure or electronics. Compare equal-thickness barriers where practical with the same magnet, target, gap, orientation and slow approach. Record movement/no movement and repeat; if measuring the greatest gap, use the same start and ruler method. Material and thickness are not interchangeable explanations. Changed case: a thicker layer of the SAME material; test rather than infer every material of that name blocks magnetism. This is a qualitative force investigation, not field equations or a claim that all metals are magnetic.

## D — A paper support for a classroom model

Question: which paper support arrangement holds the same small classroom load more stably? Define a real partner's need for a tabletop display. Use safe card/paper, blunt scissors, tape and small low loads supplied by the adult; no standing on structures or high drops. Compare at least three sketches against two criteria (support the specified load and remain inside the marked footprint), then construct two selected alternatives. Keep load, support surface, placement and loading method comparable. Record the actual load held or displacement and the point at which the test is stopped safely, not only a dramatic collapse. State each design's strength and weakness, explain a shape/function choice, collect partner feedback and improve/retest. Changed case: a wider model object with the same mass; reconsider support and footprint rather than assume the old result transfers unchanged.

## E — Water passing through prepared soil samples

Question: do the selected clean soil samples allow water through at the same rate under our method? The adult provides labelled clean commercially prepared samples, safe trays and pre-prepared filter containers; no unknown contaminated garden soil, dust clouds, aerosols, tasting or mould culture. Check allergy/skin needs and school hygiene; wash hands. Compare the same measured amount/depth of sample, container opening and slowly supplied water amount, recording actual drainage amount after the same time. Use a filter retaining particles; collect small spills and dispose appropriately. Samples may differ in several properties, so this compares samples rather than proving one isolated soil constituent caused the result. Observe actual texture/visible particles with a hand lens indoors and record same/different features. Changed case: a more tightly packed version of one sample; test packing separately before generalising. Do not infer soil health, living communities or farming suitability from drainage alone.

## Programme assessment

The pupil poses at least three own questions across these contexts and chooses observation, comparison test or source research appropriately. A shared project includes attributed published-source notes, actual observations and distinct inferences. Build a headed table and labelled drawing/key; choose a relevant display for actual counts or measurements rather than fabricate a graph for a qualitative answer. Explain the result to a partner and provide a method another investigator can follow, using actual scientific vocabulary. Retain prediction-versus-result, evidence-backed answer, a new-case prediction, improvement and limits. Sketch/build/test/revise actual models and follow the common source-assessment and safety protocols. Existing broader whole-claim requirements remain separately checked, not inferred from one plant or magnet example.
