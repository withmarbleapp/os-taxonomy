# Common scientific-process records and assessment

This original teacher-prepared programme supports the Grade 1–6 investigation sheets. It is a source-fit authoring design, not a claim that a learner has performed an investigation. The Ontario 2022 process guidance allows revisiting stages, different entry points and developmentally appropriate support. The records do not prescribe every process component for every activity. Across the grade programme, pupils undertake actual research, experiments and engineering in varied contexts across all four content strands.

## Research and source checking

Before the activities, each pupil poses at least three investigable questions across the chosen contexts. Ask which can be answered through close observation, a comparison test, a source account or an informed adult. Distinguish an opinion from a claim that could be checked. Revise a question that is too broad or cannot be investigated safely.

For a shared written or multimodal research project, pupils contribute their own questions, source notes and findings rather than only decorate a group poster. Use both an actual firsthand record from the investigations and the selected published resource named on the grade sheet. Attribute its author or organisation and purpose; record where the useful information was found. Explain which question it helps answer, whether it actually supports the proposed conclusion, and what remains unknown. These are different kinds of evidence, not two independent confirmations of the same result. Younger pupils may dictate notes and use drawings; the pupil must still make the relevant decisions and explanations. Older pupils compare the relevance and limits of accounts and explicitly distinguish a published general account from a result obtained with their own materials.

For a concrete source-assessment contrast, these are original, explicitly fictional accounts about the SAME invented trial, not real product claims or observed pupil results:

- **Class trial note, dated Trial Day 1, recorded by the trial team:** two paper covers stood over identical dry paper markers. Each received five equal drops from the same height. Cover A's marker was wet, Cover B's was dry. A second run gave the same records. The team has not tested heavier rain, wind or other materials.
- **Cover A advertisement, written by the fictional seller:** “Our cover keeps everything dry in any weather. It is the best because we like its colour.” No method or records are provided.

Ask which account can support a claim about this small test and why. Identify the seller's purpose and the unsupported generalisation. Explain why even the trial note cannot establish performance in every weather. Change the advertisement to include a reproducible method and results: reassess the evidence rather than dismiss every commercial source automatically. These original records rehearse source assessment; they never replace the child's actual experimental records or become measured scientific data.

## Actual experimentation

For each grade's B/C/D/E activities, retain a question, reasoned prediction, materials and method, what is changed and observed, what is kept reasonably alike, and actual observations/results. Repeat comparable trials where appropriate. Record unexpected or inconclusive results rather than overwrite them with the prediction. Separate an observation from an interpretation and from a supplied model. Use a table or labelled drawing suited to the question; use a graph only when the actual variables and records make it useful. A numerical display is not required for qualitative documentary research.

In Grades 5–6, include an independently planned comparison: identify the independent and dependent variables and at least three relevant controls, choose an appropriate range and repeats, explain a risk/control and justify why the method can address the question. Actual repeated measurements, not copied sample numbers, support a reliability discussion. Keep instrument precision, range, reading position, units, anomalies and sample limits explicit. Whole broad measurement and graph inventories remain separately assessed; a single ruler or bar chart does not establish them.

For the existing older independent-planning claim, the worked grade sheets are teaching examples, not an answer sheet supplied during the independent check. At that check provide the question, safe available kit and non-negotiable safety constraints, then have the pupil choose and justify the method, variable roles, controls and recording before doing it. Ask why each control removes a rival explanation. Compare five enquiry choices explicitly: a fair/comparative test; observation over time (how the selected seedlings change); pattern-seeking (whether visible pore features and water uptake vary together across the selected rock collection, without a causal conclusion); identifying/classifying (grouping the actual objects by observable features); and research (what the selected source establishes). Do not insist every question is a controlled experiment. Explain that controlling relevant factors addresses validity, while repeated readings address consistency/reliability: many consistent repeats cannot rescue a confounded causal comparison. Accuracy concerns closeness to the value being measured and is not guaranteed merely by repetition. A hypothesis needs a scientific reason, not only a directional guess; use the safe selected context rather than importing acid-reaction examples from an inherited guide. These retain the whole existing planning requirement; they are not five new topics or a terminology examination imposed on younger grades.


## Actual engineering

Identify an actual classroom user and a real need in the selected model setting. Ask the user what would make the solution useful. State at least two observable criteria and the material, time and safety constraints. Produce alternative sketches, label how a shape or structure would perform its job, construct and test solutions under comparable conditions, record strengths and weaknesses and recommend an improvement from the evidence. Include user feedback and a revised build/retest. An attractive sketch or a teacher's demonstration cannot replace the pupil's model, decisions and actual test.

The younger design comparison uses at least two solutions. Where the existing whole fair-testing/design-readiness claims require three alternatives, compare at least three sketches against the stated criteria before selecting the builds. This is a whole-claim check, not a new universal count imposed by Ontario on every activity. Weigh material use, disposal/reuse and accessibility where relevant. A small model is not a certification of a real building, environmental intervention or product.

## Established safety in every selected activity

The adult checks the actual room, materials, equipment, clothing/PPE, tool instructions, allergy/access needs, hygiene, supervision and school procedures before delivery. The pupil identifies a relevant hazard and matching precaution, uses the provided tool correctly, follows instructions and cleans/stores it safely, explaining why. Safety is observed during the actual activity rather than only recited afterwards. Where a risk table is part of the older whole-claim assessment, record hazard, possible harm and a suitable control, then review whether the control worked. Children do not design unsafe exposures in order to obtain data.

No tasting, biological samples, mould cultures, fumes, flames, mains electricity, strong magnets, sharp projectiles, neck-pressure pulse checks, breath holding, maximal exercise, deliberate heat exposure or viewing the Sun. Adult-provided classroom equipment is checked for the intended use. PPE is chosen for the real task; wearing goggles is not a substitute for removing an unnecessary hazard. Activities may be adapted or replaced by an equally qualified safe investigation when a school cannot provide the stated conditions; an unperformed replacement is not recorded as a completed test.

## Actual communication and changed cases

For the pupil's actual findings, prepare an oral explanation and a written/drawn report with question, method, selected results, conclusion answering the question, specific evidence, a new-case prediction, a justified improvement and limits. Choose vocabulary and form for the actual audience and purpose. Explain a graph/key/diagram to a partner, collect feedback and revise a misleading label, unsupported inference or unreadable comparison. For the shared writing/vocabulary claims, retain planning, drafting, revising content, editing/proofreading and appropriate new-word use in speech and writing; the whole terminology inventory is not inferred from one science word.

Every grade sheet includes a changed case. Require the pupil to decide what can be transferred and what needs a new test. An unsupported winner, invented result, unsafe shortcut, claim that a model proves real-world biology, or a conclusion wider than its method is a near miss. A source-fit protocol accounts for mandatory performance at authoring time; it neither records learner attainment nor requires a classroom study before a programme can be reviewed.

## Whole-claim and readiness boundary

Existing source associations are components. Whole question generation, close observation, data/key/diagram, actual fair tests, evidence-based conclusions, independent experimental planning, design comparison, real safe construction and broader vocabulary/measurement inventories retain their existing requirements. Their hard prerequisite paths remain unchanged and must be checked for the intended learner and task. A brief component activity is not a reason to lower an entire older claim, create an age clone or infer mastery transfer. No canonical claims, research payloads or dependencies are revised by these task sheets.
