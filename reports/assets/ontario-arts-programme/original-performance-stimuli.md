# Original performance stimuli

These short texts are original task materials. They are not curriculum quotations,
traditional stories, research interventions or records of a child's performance.
Use them alongside the attributed source examples in the task programme.

## A box of seeds — a short script

Characters: Ari, Jo and Mae. A light empty box stands for a delivery. Nobody
crosses an unsafe bridge; the situation is fictional.

*The three meet beside a notice saying that the bridge is closed.*

**Ari:** The garden club is expecting this box today. The path I planned is closed.

**Jo:** We could take the longer path around the park. The map says it is open.

**Mae:** That path has steps. The person carrying the box needs a route they can use.

**Ari:** Then we need another plan, not just the shortest line on the map.

*Jo studies the map. Mae looks towards the audience and pauses.*

**Jo:** There is a level path beside the library. It will take longer.

**Mae:** We could tell the garden club when to expect us. Then nobody has to hurry.

**Ari:** I can send the message. Who will check the route with the adult helper?

**Jo:** I will. Mae, will you help me explain which path we chose?

**Mae:** Yes. We changed the route so everyone can take part safely.

*They agree on the next step and leave the box in its safe place until the route
has been checked.*

Rehearse a purposeful pause, a difference in pace or a change in gesture. Try
another interpretation and explain its effect. Change one circumstance for a
separate improvisation: the club changes its meeting place, or a different person
needs to carry the box. Do not merely repeat the script as the improvisation.

## A small beginning — an original poem

A seed beside an empty space,
A hand that finds a sheltered place,
A pause before the work is done,
A task made lighter, shared with one.
We try, then turn, then try anew:
A little change can change the view.

Choose a pace, emphasis, pause and action that support an interpretation. Compare
another performance rather than impose one correct mood. The poem does not
require a learner to feel cheerful, disclose an experience or endorse a gardening
claim.

## Movement and visual-art stimuli

Use the poem's idea of a small change for a short own movement sequence. Try an
opening, a contrasting middle and an ending; then use another structure. An
original classroom image can show two groups preparing to connect two places.
Learners choose how to organise the image or movement; an adult's completed model
is not the desired product.

Observe an actual safe object or natural motion before transforming it. A leaf
moving in air, a cloth settling or the action of reaching and putting something
down supplies an observable starting point. Show what was retained and what was
changed in the artwork or movement.

## Fiction, traditional story and nonfiction remain distinct

The script and poem above are contemporary original fiction. The programme links
the Library of Congress edition of *The Crow and the Pitcher* as an attributed
traditional-fable example. The official Versailles account supplies a separate
nonfiction context about court dance and performance. Learners may invent a scene
from those facts, but must not present invented dialogue as a historical quotation.
