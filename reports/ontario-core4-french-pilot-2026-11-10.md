## Phonetic accuracy and variety

Primary [OQLF vowel descriptions](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22205/la-prononciation/notions-de-base-en-phonetique/les-voyelles-phonetiques) and [consonant descriptions](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22204/la-prononciation/notions-de-base-en-phonetique/les-consonnes-phonetiques) were read. OQLF's Quebec model includes four nasal vowels and explicitly distinguishes Quebec/European transcription conventions. Its [nasal-vowel spelling account](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24050/lorthographe/problemes-lies-aux-consonnes/emploi-du-m-devant-les-consonnes-m-b-et-p) independently lists the four; no new spelling requirement is imported into this oral pilot.

The adapted nasal card avoids a universal three-vowel inventory. A qualified French model establishes the intended variety, including in/un where distinguished; a legitimate regional merger or phonetic realization is not marked as a learner deficit. The examples bas/banc, beau/bon and paix/pain are meaningful lexical contrasts, not a claim that every pair differs solely by nasalization. Pronunciation is judged through intelligible intended distinctions, not imitation of one accent. Phonetic-symbol memorization and clinical diagnosis are not required.

## Source-fit protocols and remaining work

The vocabulary protocol checks actual French meaning in varied messages, then independent appropriate retrieval after a few days in another familiar context. Immediate echoing, an English word alone or a guessed picture is insufficient. The consonant protocol uses familiar words with known meanings, a qualified model and transfer to another word/phrase/listener. The nasal protocol separates listening identification (pointing or English explanation may isolate receptive meaning) from actual French production, then checks another voice and word.

These are teaching/assessment designs, not claims that a child passed or an empirically validated intervention. No classroom study or private learner records are required to establish source-fit authoring. Qualified French input and explicit corpus/variety remain real materials requirements of the design.

A1.1 and B1.1 still need whole-claim reuse review of strategies in actual French. A1/A1.2 need varied genres, purpose and connected meaning beyond a taught corpus. B1/B1.2 need coherent purposeful familiar messages with appropriate supports. B1.3 also needs pace, intonation and connected-message intelligibility. The existing community sustained-message and prepared-talk cards remain candidates with their complete requirements; they are not weakened into name/age-frame clones. All-person être/avoir in speech **and writing** likewise remains a broader grammar candidate, not equivalent to knowing j’ai or je suis.

No mastery transfer from English, FrenchL1 or age labels is inferred; no crosswalk drives learner-state changes. Source ages in the unaccepted proposal are not imported as accepted contexts. The pilot's exact Ontario9–10 interval is a source-context association, not a difficulty tier or a restriction that French phonology can only be learned in that grade. No DB or learner-state write is performed, and public acceptance remains separate from downstream publication.
