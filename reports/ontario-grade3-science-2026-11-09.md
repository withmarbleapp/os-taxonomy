## Reuse and bounded source-fit decisions

- **Plant needs/cycles:** current requirement and growth-condition claims together
  cover the selected needs set; existing germination, reproduction/dispersal and
  cycle-model claims connect the life history. These are associations on stable
  topics, not another country-specific plant progression.
- **Plant responses:** existing structural adaptation examples and predictions
  under changed requirements supply different contributions. Later evolutionary
  theory is not made a new requirement.
- **Forces:** contact/distance concepts plus vocabulary provide varied force
  types. Existing pushes/pulls and solid deformation cover motion, magnitude and
  shape effects. No separate starting/stopping or Ontario-force clones are added.
- **Structures:** bones explicitly provide a natural supporting-framework
  component; models relate form to function; material choices and force balance
  contribute where their evidence actually matches. Force balance is not equated
  with structural stability, nor is a successful construction treated as proof of
  all load/strength concepts.
- **Cultural/source cases:** production and use knowledge contributes, but verified
  local and community-specific case sets remain explicit. Australian cultural
  evidence is not relabelled. A missing resource is not automatically a missing
  topic.

## Explicit follow-up conditions

The complete part set, photosynthesis gas exchange, cultural/local uses, native
plant protection, local-food comparison, structural strength/stability/bracing,
soil-health additions and composting retain concrete follow-ups. Existing later
photosynthesis, biomass, supply-chain, ecosystem and mechanics claims remain
candidate-only when the whole claim has not been established for 8–9. None is
silently lowered because one clause matches.

The same broad source requirement can receive several components while still
remaining Partial. For example, bones support a natural framework, but do not
certify the whole natural/built structural account. Decomposers and humus are
real composting components, but not a complete managed-compost sequence. Such
findings do not authorize new mastery topics or changes to existing ones.
