# Canadian financial concepts — bounded original teaching cards

Original source-attributed inputs for the historical Grade4–6 Mathematics portfolios. Not personal financial or tax advice, product recommendations, current account offers or a pupil performance record. No family account data, actual transfer, borrowing or transaction is needed. Link to the actual official sources; do not copy entire pages or require the pupil to navigate adult financial products.

## Card T — taxes and public services

Canada has federal, provincial or territorial, and municipal governments that collect taxes. Income tax has federal and provincial/territorial parts. GST is federal; some provinces have PST, and some combine their provincial sales tax with GST as HST. Municipal governments assess property taxes on land/buildings. Taxes help pay for public programs and services such as education and health care. The precise rate, item, payer and rules depend on the applicable circumstances, so a classroom percentage is not automatically a current tax rule.

[CRA, Purpose of taxes](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/educational-programs/purpose-taxes.html), actual common-tax and government/service body read. This card does not imply every tax funds one exclusive service or make a child’s family liability a lesson task.

Teach matching one tax type to its stated level and explaining one supported service purpose. Independently give new original cards: “municipal property-tax revenue”, “federal goods/services tax”, “provincial income tax” and “public education/health services”; pupil describes types/levels and the general public-service relationship, then explains why one cannot calculate a real family’s tax from these cards. A supplied tax calculation alone does not satisfy the conceptual source clause.

## Card B — accounts, payments, borrowing and costs

A chequing account supports everyday transactions; savings accounts usually pay interest but may charge for transactions. Debit access uses account money. Credit permits an agreed deferred payment or borrowing and creates repayment obligations. A personal loan is repaid over time, with any interest or fees set by its agreement. Account fees and borrowing interest are different costs. A fixed loan rate stays unchanged during its term; a variable rate may change. A promotional rate may end. Particular accounts and lenders have their own terms, so no real institution is promised to offer a model rate or fee.

[FCAC, Choosing financial products](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/banking/choosing-products.html); [Personal loans](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/personal-loans.html); [Interest rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/interest-rates-rise.html). Actual bounded account/loan-cost/fixed-variable bodies read. No legal ceiling, eligibility, live product comparison or universal recommendation is taught.

Original MODEL offer cards, not bank quotations: savings A pays a declared annual simple interest rate2% with no fee; transaction B charges a declared$2monthly fee and pays no interest; loan C has a declared fixed annual simple rate5% plus a one-off$3setup fee; loan D has a declared variable rate starting4% that may change and a declared$1fee. Specify a one-year term, unchanged$100principal and no deposits/withdrawals/repayments only for this arithmetic model. The model keys are A$2interest, B$24fees, C$5interest+$3fee=$8cost; D’s starting4% does not establish its full-year cost unless its path is supplied. These are simplified mathematical cases, not realistic repayment schedules or APR disclosures.

Pupil identifies account versus loan, interest paid/received versus fee, fixed versus variable and what additional information is needed. A fresh model changes principal/rate/fee/term and asks for a reasoned comparison under a declared purpose. Lower headline rate alone is not a complete cost comparison. Explain that a reasoned choice depends on the stated needs and terms, without declaring all credit bad, approving a loan or requesting private debt history.
