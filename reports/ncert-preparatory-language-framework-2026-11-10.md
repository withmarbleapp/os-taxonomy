# NCERT Preparatory language framework review

This slice reviews all 35 formal Preparatory language records in the accepted NCERT baseline: 18 R1 positions and 17 R2 positions. The source is the 2023 framework's October 2024 printed edition, PDF pages 233–234, 236–237 and 239–240. The official stage window is 8–11, covering Grades 3–5; no per-grade age deadline is invented and no Middle-stage R3 scope is added.

The result is 17 Full source-fit authoring dispositions, nine ConditionalGraphRepresentation dispositions, five ConditionalLanguageBinding dispositions and four ContextualReadingEngagement requirements. These categories remain separate from learner attainment. The [record report](ncert-preparatory-language-framework-2026-11-10.json) preserves each code, role, direct contribution, withheld candidate and unresolved condition. The [21 original protocols](ncert-preparatory-language-protocols-2026-11-10.json) account for the source requirements through parameterised tasks; they are not observations, a validated battery, or evidence of mastery in an unspecified language.

There are 56 new associations across 20 existing topics, in exact IN 8–11 contexts. No authored claim, dependency or catalogue changes. Existing Foundational 3–8 contexts are retained separately, including their stage-end and unfamiliar-R2 conditions; they are not unioned with Preparatory intervals.

## Follow the source's roles

R1 and R2 are language roles, not synonyms for Hindi and English. The framework's actual-language choices, progression and practical conditions remain intact. No nationwide fixed pair is needed before curation. Actual language, script, assessor competence, accessible materials and support are declared when a framework task is used. Separate role evidence prevents English or R1 proficiency being transferred automatically to another language.

The precise unfamiliar-R2 emergence and early-Preparatory consolidation exception from the Foundational oral source remains applicable when its condition holds. It is not a permanent exemption from all Preparatory requirements or a new taxonomy difficulty level. Stage-appropriate connected conversation is distinguished from wholly rehearsed turns or word-search that repeatedly prevents meaning, without imposing native-speaker speed or accent.

R1 C-4.1 and C-4.2 remain distinct printed source positions. Their similar wording is not deduplicated, and it does not justify duplicate vocabulary claims. Stage/role/area qualifiers in the public keys are generated identities; literal source CG/C codes remain separate factual metadata.

## Shared operations and complete tasks

Safe shared claims account for responsive conversation, descriptions, organised oral reports, reasoned class contributions, narrative and informational meaning, prediction, inference, revisable visualisation, literary wording, syllable analysis and vocabulary learning/use. The component role is explicit; broad goals are not declared mastered because several topics carry their code.

The tasks retain whole-claim conditions. Reporting includes a factual report, a personal-event account and meaningful self-review of clarity. Reasoned oral contributions cover presentations, role play and class discussion/debate without importing the older Standard-English/Formal-Debates bundle. Visualisation includes text-based mental images, inferred meaning and revision after new information. Poetry composition retains written authorship, sound-pattern work and expressive recitation; merely repeating a supplied verse is not sufficient.

R1 essential conclusions use existing main-information, inference and sequence/message claims. The source operation does not require a written note-taking bundle merely because one candidate has a similar title. Read-aloud evidence remains distinct from independent reading. Familiar and unfamiliar prose, poetry and informational/functional texts receive suitable task cases rather than one rehearsed story standing for all forms.

The source-permitted syllable route uses actual R2 speech units. Whole-claim pronunciation work beside a written word is retained, without equating syllables with letters or akshar boundaries. Vocabulary tasks include discussion, contextual inference, cross-subject meaning and functional application; no universal word count or English lexicon is invented.

Parameterised source-fit tasks require expert-checked actual-language models and response criteria. This is a material/consumer binding condition at use, not a request to choose a national pair now, and not a licence to create language-country clones.

## Access and engagement

The R1 reading-interest goal and its two competencies, plus the R2 reading-interest competency, remain contextual requirements. The borrowing case specifies a real repeated library-loan and home-reading opportunity; showing a library picture is insufficient. Accessible alternatives must still provide a genuine loan/access route rather than silently delete the source condition. Resource scarcity, inaccessible text and unfamiliar language are recorded separately from a child's engagement.

Voluntary choice and developing preferences do not become a binary mastery topic or a compulsory positive-feelings score. No book quota, learner state or forced enthusiasm is introduced. Reading performance and engagement are different evidence channels.
