## Why these claims are distinct

Knowing words for movement, predicting motion or explaining how senses work does not demonstrate organising a gait, balancing through a turn or controlling a moving play object. The existing physiological “balance” claim concerns body regulation, not postural balance. Safe craft-tool handling does not demonstrate timing a striking action. Knowing that exercise supports health does not demonstrate carrying out suitable preparation.

The new claims require actual observable control and adjustment. A changed case distinguishes purposeful release from force-only throwing, tracking and secure receiving from accidental contact, weight transfer from one rehearsed pose, directed foot contact from a chance goal, and timed safe striking from a lucky impact. No physics or biology knowledge is added as a hard prerequisite. No dependency changes are made.

The proposed beat topic was withdrawn after full review of existing `mt_OTFABm2xdf`, Rhythm in rhymes and songs. Its clapping/tapping, rhythm replication and repeated-pattern participation can serve the actual bodily beat requirement through a suitable local piece and stepping task. Every canonical check remains. A PE heading or different body medium did not justify a clone.

## Source scope and performance

Formal Foundational contexts use the official 3–8 stage window and Preparatory contexts use 8–11. These are stage windows with attainment referenced to stage end, rather than deadlines at their youngest ages. The formal source scope includes Balvatika and Grades 1–2; its selected primary view [1, 2] is retained separately and does not replace the full stage or invent a per-grade required band. The suggestive primary view uses the separately established early-primary Grades 1–2 window, 6–8. No separate per-grade cutoff is invented.

LS1 retains its required-resource applicability and LS2 remains the initial implementation baseline. Their separate source positions do not become difficulty levels. All catalogue keys and editions remain unchanged; this is the selected NCERT national baseline, not a claim to cover every Indian board.

The source-specific rope task, complete hopping game, sustained hopping, carried-object task, faster-movement balance and printed 60-second single-leg example with the other leg folded remain explicit. A rope-free gait or shorter still pose cannot silently replace them. These duties are not global distance, load, speed or fitness norms. Actual task, support, equipment, duration and access conditions must be recorded at use; no body-capacity score or learner field is introduced.

The broad body-development goal has its own integrated everyday transfer. Fine-finger precision uses complete existing craft and digit-production tasks; that does not mark all twenty detailed suggestive fine-motor positions complete. Sustained carrying/walking/running are tested directly rather than inferred from one short sprint or maximum lift. The new preparation prompt permits following or choosing a suitable sequence; it does not require leadership, painful stretching or advanced physiology. The actual preparation performance accounts for the NCERT duty; a broader canonical-card reason is checked separately without making verbal explanation a source prerequisite.

## Teaching guidance and evidence limits

All seven claims include original teaching and assessment guidance with skill-specific feedback, diagnostic contrasts, reteaching and transfer tasks. The checked [VCAA movement-assessment guidance](https://www.vcaa.vic.edu.au/curriculum/foundation-10/curriculum-area-resources/health-and-physical-education/movement-assessment-practice/object-control-skills-5-8-years-and-9-12-years), [NSW primary movement resources](https://sport.education.nsw.gov.au/Fundamental-movement-skills) and [SHAPE America standards](https://shapeamerica.org/standards/pe/new-pe-standards/) corroborate performance-based skill distinctions. Their grade cutoffs or scoring systems are not imported into the Indian source contexts.

The particular authored sequences are untested and carry low confidence as teaching recipes. The sources do not establish efficacy of these lessons. Activities keep safe clear space, suitable equipment, appropriate oversight and access conditions explicit. An alternative or inaccessible task does not automatically establish an unperformed operation. Neither vocabulary recitation nor compulsory enthusiasm substitutes for performance.
