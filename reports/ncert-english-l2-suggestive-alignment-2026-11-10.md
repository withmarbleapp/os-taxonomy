## Named English and source ages

The source actually selects English as L2. English letters, phonics, sound/spelling conventions and print direction therefore have a legitimate named-language context here. They do not establish the formal framework's R2 for every learner, and they do not transfer English proficiency into an arbitrary R1/R2 language.

The scoped interval is 6–8, the official early-primary Grades 1–2 window in the separately pinned October 2024 NCFSE edition, PDF page 211. Each position keeps its actual printed grade. No 6–7/7–8 cutoff or preschool extension is invented. Source-specific performance conditions remain explicit, including the distinct short/longer heard-poem line ranges and oral joining of two- and three-part words.

## Reuse the actual operation

Letters and their common sounds, oral syllable joining, multisyllabic decoding, rhyme, sentence endings, basic list commas, sight-word/connected reading, phonemic encoding, cluster spelling and known exception retrieval already have claims. No additional country, grade, step-count or difficulty topic is needed.

Oral syllable joining is not the same assessment as decoding a printed multisyllabic word. The existing two operations are used according to their complete meanings; their similar names are not an instruction to merge them or create another age variant. Likewise, word-level writing from speech does not require the broader sentence-dictation topic merely because its title contains “dictation”. Regular sound encoding, taught cluster words and known exceptions supply direct complementary operations. The material set is declared, rather than claiming the ability to spell arbitrary English words. Complete existing exception-word assessment still includes its weekday and sentence-context cases; these are canonical topic checks, not an invented NCERT word-list mandate.

Heard-poem meaning is checked separately from rhyme detection and expressive recital. A reproduced poem without understanding is a near miss. Narrative listening remains distinct from independent decoding and written authorship. Local-tale retelling retains a suitably sourced parent/elder or authorised telling and actual English retelling, rather than inventing a local-history knowledge node.

Conversation protocols distinguish free exchange, a supplied scenario, picture-supported interaction and actually initiating a topic. They retain relevant responses and turn-giving. A rehearsed adult script or one prompted reply does not establish the complete operation. Immediate commands, relaying instructions and a connected sequence are also separate task cases within the same existing instruction claim.

## Mixed skill and engagement

Three positions retain two channels: inventive spelling, recital and heard-story understanding each has an actual production/performance/comprehension task alongside a contextual response channel. None is reduced to attitude alone.

Inventive spelling preserves a child-authored attempt and an explanation of sound/letter choices. Phonically plausible but unconventional writing is not automatically judged as correct conventional spelling or as failure; accurate taught-word spelling is assessed separately when evaluating the complete existing claim. Recitation retains a chosen-poem performance, with complete-topic presentation/visual-support checks kept separate. Story understanding retains actual relevant comprehension and interaction.

Access, choice, prompting, hesitation and frustration remain contextual. Correct performance does not prove enjoyment; reluctance does not prove lack of skill. No compulsory positive feeling, stable attitude label, reading quota, new learner field or score is introduced. The mixed status is visibly separate from Full graph mastery.
