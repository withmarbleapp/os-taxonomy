# Contextual curriculum requirements

An official curriculum record can express a requirement for learning conditions, participation, values or subject-specific attitudes. Preserve that record and its source status even when a stable binary mastery claim would misrepresent it. This handling is a curation decision, not a new official source kind or a reason to exclude the requirement.

Review the actual operation before choosing this treatment. Independently assessable knowledge or action may need an existing or justified new claim. Equipment choice, questioning, practical tool use and specific knowledge must not be dismissed as context merely to avoid a genuine coverage gap. The ordinary [curation principles](principles.md) and [alignment guidelines](alignment-guidelines.md) still apply.

For a contextual requirement, keep separate:

1. **The source requirement:** retain its code, formal/suggestive status, scope and applicability.
2. **The authoring response:** define a concrete task/environment/reflection protocol with appropriate resources and limits.
3. **The graph representation:** say whether genuine mastery components are linked and what those links establish. An absent binary node need not mean a missing skill.
4. **Learner evidence:** distinguish planned assessment from actual observations. A protocol does not establish that a child has attained the outcome.

A review can name a separate contextual disposition alongside Full, Partial and Gap. Record the previous classification and reason for the change; do not silently count a representational decision as newly Full topic coverage. These labels belong to the review report. They do not alter the official catalogue type or introduce a learner score.

## Example: mathematical confidence and perceived value

Formal NCERT Foundational C-8.14 concerns the learner's mathematical self-efficacy and perceived value. General Growth Mindset knowledge, persistence, a correct sum or joining a game do not individually establish that requirement. A learner can find an activity frustrating while still expecting that a useful approach is available, or can value its purpose while feeling uncertain about one task.

Use low-stakes, domain-specific reflection around accessible mathematical activities. Preserve the task, support and time context. Invite the learner to say what they might find out, what could help them begin, and how the mathematics relates to a purpose they recognise. After the activity, invite an honest account of progress, difficulty, useful support and perceived purpose. Compare signals across contexts without turning one response into an enduring trait.

Evidence can include speech, pointing, drawings or chosen representations. Support access and understanding before interpreting a response. Do not require cheerful behaviour, a prescribed positive sentence, agreement with an adult's preferences, or liking every maths activity. Honest uncertainty is useful information. Keep mathematical performance evidence distinct from reflection on confidence or value.

The [TIMSS confidence reporting](https://timss2019.org/reports/students-confident-in-mathematics-and-science/) and [value reporting](https://timss2019.org/reports/students-value-math-science/index.html) illustrate separate attitude evidence channels alongside achievement. Their questionnaires and cut scores are not imported here, and the original formative protocol is not a validated scale for younger children.

The code-level report should explicitly retain the formal competency, describe its contextual protocol, identify the absence of a binary mastery representation, and name remaining material/observation limits. No new learner field, score, reset or transfer follows from this authoring decision. Future consumers must preserve those distinctions when presenting coverage or collecting observations.
